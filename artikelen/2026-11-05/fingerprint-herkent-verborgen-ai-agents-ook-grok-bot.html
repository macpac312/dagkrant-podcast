HTML_TAG_START CHICAGO, November 05, 2026--(BUSINESS WIRE)--Fingerprint, the industry leader in device intelligence, today announced expanded bot detection capabilities that identify traffic inferred to be undeclared AI agents, including Meta's Muse, Spear Street's Instinct and xAI's Grok Bot. As more AI agents operate without identifying themselves, Fingerprint provides browser-level visibility to recognize previously unidentified automated traffic, distinguish it from legitimate human activity and enable businesses to determine which interactions to allow, limit or block. HTML_TAG_START The AI Agents Operating Without Declaring Their Identity HTML_TAG_END

HTML_TAG_START More than half of all web traffic in 2026 is no longer human, which is largely due to AI agents acting on behalf of consumers or enterprises. While industry standards such as Web Bot Auth were designed to let AI agents cryptographically prove their identity, a growing wave of prominent agents are choosing to operate undeclared or are actively shifting away from signed traffic: HTML_TAG_END

HTML_TAG_START Muse provides no verifiable identity, alters its browser fingerprint and suppresses the standard automation flags that traditional bot detection tools rely on. Fingerprint's research team shipped detection for traffic inferred to be Muse roughly two weeks after the agent was announced, and subsequently identified Muse activity on a major bank's login flows that the bank's other security vendors failed to flag. HTML_TAG_END

HTML_TAG_START Instinct logs into websites using human credentials, including by requesting multi-factor authentication (MFA) codes from the user via SMS, without signing its traffic via Web Bot Auth. In one high-profile case reported by CNN, Instinct pinged restaurant reservation platform Resy hundreds of times every hour on behalf of a user, resulting in account deactivation. HTML_TAG_END

HTML_TAG_START Grok Bot can sign in to websites, work through inboxes, and finish multistep tasks for real users. It used to announce itself on arrival by signing its requests with Web Bot Auth, but around October 9, 2026, it stopped. HTML_TAG_END

HTML_TAG_START "More AI agents are visiting websites without disclosing their identity, and some that used to, like Grok Bot, have stopped," said Dan Pinto, CEO and Co-founder of Fingerprint. "That lack of transparency is already a widespread issue: Fingerprint's recent report found that 79% of financial institutions have caught a malicious bot or scraper posing as an AI assistant. Fingerprint can identify traffic inferred to be Muse, Instinct and Grok Bot without any assistance from their developers, giving enterprises the ability to uncover previously undetected AI agent activity and make informed decisions about which traffic to allow, limit or block." HTML_TAG_START Why are AI agents hiding their identities instead of using Web Bot Auth? Many websites rely on legacy security rules that automatically block any non-human traffic. To avoid being blocked while executing tasks for their users, the companies behind agents like Muse and Instinct design them to mimic human browsers, suppress standard automation flags and omit cryptographic signatures. Additionally, some agents that previously signed their requests, such as Grok Bot, stopped. HTML_TAG_END

HTML_TAG_START How does Fingerprint distinguish between "signed" or "verified" AI agents and "inferred" AI agents? Fingerprint maintains a strict technical distinction based on cryptographic proof. Signed and verified AI agents cryptographically sign their outgoing HTTP requests, providing tamper-proof confirmation of their provider's identity, while inferred AI agents do not present a cryptographic signature. Fingerprint identifies traffic inferred to be a specific agent by analyzing browser, device, network and behavioral signals, and classifies each determination with a high, medium or low confidence score. HTML_TAG_END

HTML_TAG_START How should fraud and security teams handle an undeclared AI agent? Instead of blocking or allowing all agents, teams can use Fingerprint to match their policy to the sensitivity of the action. For example, they can rate-limit undeclared agent sessions so an automated assistant cannot refresh or transact faster than a human user. They can also trigger step-up authentication or explicit user verification when an undeclared agent attempts to access sensitive account settings or authentication flows. Conversely, they can allow low-risk browsing or standard purchases to proceed while inspecting Smart Signals to block coordinated abuse. HTML_TAG_END
