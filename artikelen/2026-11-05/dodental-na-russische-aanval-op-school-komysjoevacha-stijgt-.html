Here is some more on howItaly’s far-right prime minister Giorgia Meloni, who once had good relations with Donald Trump, dismissed questions about the midterm election results,telling journalists “let’s talk about America another time”.

Meloni, wearing a red suit, had been door-stepped while attending a ceremony in central Rome on Wednesday to mark Italy’s national unity and armed forces day and didn’t welcome the journalistic focus on the US midterms.

“I see you’re very interested in the celebration of the republic and the Armed Forces,” she said sarcastically.

Meloni had positioned herself as a key European ally to Trump until April, when the pair fell out, firstly over Italy’s refusal to support the US-Israeli war in Italy and then because of Trump’s extraordinary broadside against Pope Leo in reaction to the pontiff’s condemnation of the war.

They had appeared to be getting relations back on track at the G7 summit in June before Trump claimed Meloni had begged him to take a photo with him - a story Meloni said was a “a total invention”.

Italy’s foreign minister Antonio Tajani did instead comment on the midterms, saying Italy’s relationship with the US “remains unchanged regardless of who is in government or the parliamentary situation”.

In an interview with the Rome-based newspaper, Il Messaggero, Tajani said “we have never been Trumpians” and “Meloni isn’t a Maga supporter either – heaven forbid!”

The League, which is led by joint deputy prime minister Matteo Salvini, who until fairly recently continuously praised Trump, said in a statement:

“When a population votes, their vote must always be respected and understood.”

The party also expressed hope that the outcome would speed-up efforts to end wars and restore peace.
