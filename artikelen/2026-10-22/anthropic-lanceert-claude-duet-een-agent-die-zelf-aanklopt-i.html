Claude Duet launches as Anthropic's agent that messages you first

Claude Duet is an Anthropic app where Claude works as a proactive agent: every morning it sends you a rundown of what matters, checks your connected apps through the day and pings you only when a decision needs to be yours. The company unveiled it on October 21, 2026 as a research preview, for now behind a waitlist and open only to Pro and Max subscribers in the United States.

The announcement came with a post from the official @claudeai account, which boils the project down to four verbs: Duet brings you what needs your attention, completes the tasks you hand it, keeps working when you step away and checks with you before it acts on your behalf.

To get in you need a Claude Pro or Max subscription and a US address: the waitlist is on the product page. Team and Enterprise plans aren't included yet, and no other countries are listed. There's no separate price, it comes with your plan.

What Claude Duet does every day

A day with Duet starts with a morning briefing, a short list of the items that matter most across every app you've connected. From there the agent keeps an eye on those services and writes only when something needs your attention, as 9to5Google explained. No chat to open first: the agent is the one who shows up.

On the practical side, Anthropic's list is concrete. Duet schedules meetings, builds financial or sales forecasts, assembles dashboards (views that pull data from several tools together) and prototypes web apps. Digital Trends adds follow-up emails, the routine ones sent after a meeting. The audience it's aiming at, 9to5Google writes, is professionals and small business owners with too many things to track.

You talk to Duet by text or by voice, inside a single continuous thread. Memory, skills and connected apps carry over from Claude, so anyone already on Claude Pro or Max doesn't start setup from scratch. The app is separate from the main one on iOS, Android and claude.ai. Anthropic says how the two will coexist will depend on the feedback it gets.

How it works while you're away

This is where it parts ways with an ordinary chatbot. Duet runs on a virtual machine with a browser of its own, a simulated computer in Anthropic's cloud that doesn't need your laptop or phone to be on. We'd already seen the principle with Cowork, which has run in the cloud for Pro and Max since October 6; Duet goes a step further and moves without being called.

It doesn't do so continuously. The agent checks connected accounts every few hours, a choice made to avoid burning through the plan's usage limits. Digital Trends points out the price of that caution: Duet can miss an update that lands between two checks, so it isn't a real-time alarm system.

The connectors (the bridges to other services) number more than 6,000 across plugins and integrations in the Claude Marketplace. The examples cited run from Gmail and Slack to QuickBooks, Stripe, Gusto for payroll, LinkedIn, Docusign, HubSpot, Canva and Shopify. On Shopify, Duet already manages the store, with a visual editor billed as "coming soon".

What it asks before acting, and what it never does

The deal fits in one line: before sending messages, making payments, signing documents or deleting anything, Duet shows what it plans to do and waits for the green light. Approvals go through the auto mode classifier, the same filter Anthropic uses in Claude Code and Claude in Chrome to decide which actions run freely and which are held until the user signs off.

Two things stay off limits even with permission: trading securities and moving money between accounts. Purchases go through Link, Stripe's digital wallet, using a virtual card, or through Shopify's Shop Pay. Logging in with passwords saved in 1Password is listed as a feature on the way, again only with your consent. If you want to know which assistants actually buy things on our behalf today, the picture is in the guide to AI shopping assistants.

Security, according to Anthropic

Credentials follow the scheme of Claude Managed Agents, Anthropic's platform for hosted agents: the agent never directly sees passwords or access tokens. Against instructions hidden in web pages and documents (prompt injection, essentially orders disguised as content) the company says it relies on defenses that grew out of its browser-use research. The project went through adversarial testing, red teaming and a penetration test by an outside firm. It is covered by the public bug bounty program on HackerOne.

There is also a classifier dedicated to sensitive information. It refuses to share the most tightly held tier, such as credit card numbers and identity documents, while personal health or financial data goes through only if you explicitly ask for it. Agent access to data has already moved others to act: the story on Apple putting the brakes on Full Disk Access for AI agents covered that. Websites themselves are starting to shut the door too, as the piece on Amazon, Yelp, Delta and JPMorgan blocking agents tells it.

Which model it runs on, and who got there first

Behind it sits Claude Opus 5.5, picked by Anthropic to judge how urgent things are and when to write. Duet isn't, however, the first agent to move without being called: it lands a few weeks after OpenAI's dots, the agent that calls and writes on the user's behalf. We've already compared the assistants of this kind in the piece on Gemini agent, Autopilot, dots and Grok Bot. According to 9to5Google, the launch comes in a market where Meta's Muse has also seen its popularity climb fast.

Anthropic is betting on control as the differentiator. Duet is a separate application, with a single thread, a virtual machine of its own and mandatory approvals on anything touching money, signatures and deletions. Anyone who has worked with Claude Code for years knows the company tends to reach for the brake before the accelerator. Here the brake is the product.

Permission matters more than intelligence

Duet's leap isn't about intelligence, it's about permission. For years an AI assistant waited for a question; now it asks to read your mail, your calendar, your books, and to write first. Useful for anyone with too much to follow. Still, it's a fairly big grant of trust, one to hand over slowly. The most interesting part of the announcement is the least flashy one: the list of things Duet will never do on its own.
