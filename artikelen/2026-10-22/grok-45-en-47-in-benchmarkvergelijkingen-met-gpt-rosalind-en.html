GPT-Rosalind vs Grok 4.7: Health Score vs Biology Gate

Grok 4.7 ships with a number that looks, at a glance, like the answer to GPT-Rosalind: 56.7% on HealthBench Professional, up from 48.5% for Grok 4.6. That is a clinical-knowledge benchmark, reported by SpaceXAI in the launch table for a model released on 21 September 2026, and it is the closest thing to a medical evaluation in either company's material. GPT-Rosalind, on general sale since 5 October 2026 after an April 2026 introduction, publishes nothing comparable — its numbers are chemistry and wet-lab benchmarks, and you need a trusted-access review to run them yourself. So the honest question is not which model scores higher on health; it is what a health benchmark measures versus what a biology gate protects, and why one of these models costs a third as much per token as the other while being available to anybody. Benchmark disputes and access-gate changes that follow a comparison like this one are recorded with their dates and sources in the AI Incident Archive, alongside the rest of the model and vendor incidents we follow.

The overlap that turns out to be narrow

HealthBench Professional asks a model questions of the kind a clinician would answer. It rewards recall, calibration and the ability to hold a clinical frame. It does not require the model to design a perturbation experiment, read a wet-lab protocol, or reason about a structure-activity relationship. GPT-Rosalind's published suite is the other kind of test: MedChemBench covers potency and ADME prediction and retrosynthesis; GeneBench covers long-horizon genomics; LabWorkBench covers linking perturbations to outcomes in real protocols. Different instruments, different targets.

• What is being measured — Grok 4.7: clinical knowledge and judgement, 56.7% on HealthBench Professional. GPT-Rosalind: chemistry and wet-lab reasoning, 27.5% on MedChemBench and 63.2% on LabWorkBench.

• Who ran it — Grok 4.7: SpaceXAI, against its own previous model. GPT-Rosalind: O​penAI, against GPT-5.5.

• Independent reproduction — None for either benchmark.

• What neither number tells you — Whether the model can be trusted with a result that goes into a filing. That is a question about failure modes, not scores.

Put the two tables side by side and there is no row they share. That is not a gap in the reporting; it is the shape of the market. A general model is measured on breadth, a specialist on depth, and the vendors pick the tests that show their own strength.

The envelope, which is where these two actually differ

Specifications are the least glamorous part of a comparison and the part that decides whether a workflow is possible at all. Here the two models diverge more than the benchmark conversation suggests.

• Context window — Grok 4.7: 500,000 tokens, half of GPT-Rosalind's 1,000,000.

• Max output — Grok 4.7: 450,000 tokens, and SpaceXAI's own documentation states there is no text output limit, which does not agree with the 450K figure on our model card. We flag the disagreement rather than pick a side; the vendor is the authority on its own model. GPT-Rosalind: not separately published as an output ceiling.

• Input — Grok 4.7: text and images, JPG or PNG up to 20 MiB each, no documented cap on the number of images, any order. GPT-Rosalind: scientific file inputs through ChatGPT, Codex and the API; no general image-input claim published.

• Reasoning control — Grok 4.7: reasoning_effort at low, medium, high or xhigh, with the published benchmark figures quoted at xhigh. GPT-Rosalind: a reasoning-effort-style control in the API, with tool use at the core of the model.

• Tools — Grok 4.7: function calling, web search, X search and code execution, all server-side. GPT-Rosalind: a life-sciences tool plugin with more than fifty tools inside ChatGPT and Codex.

• Knowledge cutoff — Grok 4.7: May 2026, with no realtime awareness by default. GPT-Rosalind: not published.

The context gap is the one that bites in this domain. A 1M-token window is not a luxury for genomics work; it is what lets a model hold a whole atlas or a full protocol set in one pass. Grok 4.7's 500K is generous by general standards and half of what the specialist offers. Meanwhile the 450K output ceiling is far beyond anything a biology answer needs and exactly what a long agent trajectory or a large refactor wants.

The price gap is the widest in this set

Grok 4.7 is the cheapest model in any of these comparisons and GPT-Rosalind is the most expensive, which makes the arithmetic blunt.

• Input — Grok 4.7 $2.00 per 1M vs GPT-Rosalind $5.00. The specialist costs 2.5× as much.

• Output — Grok 4.7 $6.00 vs GPT-Rosalind $25.00. A 4.2× gap, and the largest single-number difference in this article.

• Cached input — Grok 4.7 $0.50 vs GPT-Rosalind $0.50. Identical, which is the one line where the specialist is not more expensive.

• Long prompts — Grok 4.7 doubles to $4.00 / $12.00 with $1.00 cached once a prompt reaches 200K tokens. GPT-Rosalind publishes no long-context surcharge.

• Regional billing — Grok 4.7 has a US endpoint at a 1.1× multiplier, which works out to $2.20 / $6.60 below 200K.

The output ratio is where the decision actually lives. A discovery workflow produces a lot of output: candidate lists, protocol drafts, code, long rationales. At $25 against $6 per million, a run that emits ten million output tokens costs $250 on the specialist and $60 on the general model. Whether that $190 buys you anything is the only question worth asking, and no published benchmark answers it.

What the independent evaluator adds

Grok 4.7 has a real independent record, and it is more informative than the vendor's table because it includes the costs nobody advertises.

• Intelligence — 46 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, v4.3.2, which is 21st of 211 models on that board and well above the index median of 26. GPT-Rosalind is not on the board at all; specialist models are out of scope.

• Cost per index run — $3.74, with the evaluator recording a 75% cache discount and an input/output rate matching the vendor's own $2.00 / $6.00. Its comment is that the model is reasonably priced against peers of similar price.

• Verbosity — 240M output tokens across the index, against a median of 88M. Artificial Analysis calls it "very verbose", and that is the single most useful number here for budgeting: a model that thinks in longer chains costs more per task at any fixed token rate.

• Speed — 82.6 output tokens per second, 83rd of 211, which the evaluator nonetheless labels slower than average.

The verbosity figure is the one that should change your mental arithmetic. Grok 4.7 at $6.00 output is not 4.2× cheaper than GPT-Rosalind at $25.00 per task if it emits nearly three times as many tokens getting there. On a per-task basis the real gap is narrower than the rate card says, and nobody publishes a Rosalind verbosity number to compare against — because Rosalind is not on the board.
