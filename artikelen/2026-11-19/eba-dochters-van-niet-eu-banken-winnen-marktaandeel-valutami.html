​The EBA reports growing market share of third-country bank subsidiaries in the EU and reduced foreign currency mismatches | European Banking Authority

​The European Banking Authority (EBA) today published two Reports on the market share of subsidiaries of non-EU banks in the EU, as well as on EU banks’ assets and liabilities in foreign currencies. As of December 2025, subsidiaries of third country banking groups accounted for 10.22% of total EU banking assets, up from 9.8% a year earlier. The Reports also show that EU banks increased foreign currency funding to 25.3% of total funding, up from 24.8% as of December 2024, helping to reduce currency mismatches and largely eliminate the mismatch in US dollar positions.

​As of December 2025, subsidiaries of third-country groups held 10.22% of total assets, 8.56% of loans, 7.37% of debt securities and 30.55% of derivatives. The increase in market share was mainly driven by subsidiaries ultimately controlled from the United States and the United Kingdom, which expanded their activities faster than EU banks.

​The analysis highlights notable differences in business models. Subsidiaries of third-country banking groups are more strongly focused on financial counterparties, with credit institutions and other financial corporations accounting for 62% of their reported assets, compared with 27% for EU banks.

​While both EU banks and third-country subsidiaries conduct significant cross border activities, subsidiaries of third country banking groups have a larger share of assets booked outside the country in which they are established. Their cross-border business is concentrated on financial counterparties, whereas EU banks’ cross-border activities are primarily focused on non-financial corporations.

​In terms of income generation, subsidiaries of third-country banking groups represented 6% of interest income, 15% of dividend income and 11% of both fee and commission income and other operating income. Their presence was particularly strong in specialised fee-based activities, including commodities (70.41%), corporate finance (33.03%), central bank administrative services for collective investment (32.47%) and custody services (26.07%).

​The second Report examines EU banks' exposures and funding in foreign currencies and highlights a continued strengthening of funding positions. As of December 2025, 28.6% of exposures and 25.3% of funding were denominated in foreign currencies. The gap between foreign currency exposures and funding narrowed over the year, particularly for US dollar positions, where the mismatch was largely eliminated. In absolute terms, foreign currency funding increased by 9% compared with December 2024, driven mainly by repo funding and unsecured wholesale funding.

​US dollar activities remain highly concentrated among global systemically important institutions (G-SIIs). Although G-SIIs account for around one-third of total EU banking sector funding, they provide more than half of all foreign currency funding and over two-thirds of US dollar funding. Their role is particularly significant in US dollar repo markets, where they account for nearly 90% of funding volumes.

​EU banks maintained strong funding positions, with Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) buffers remaining comfortably above minimum regulatory requirements both overall and across most significant currencies. The only exceptions were the Norwegian krone, Japanese yen and Swiss franc, which together account for just 2.3% of total available stable funding.

​The identification of non-EU entities and operators was made based on the country of domicile of the ultimate parent.

​The EBA relied on different data sources to carry out the analyses included in the Reports. The analysis on funding structure and assets and liabilities in foreign currency is based on EBA supervisory reporting data. The market share assessment relies mainly on FINREP reporting. While only a limited number of subsidiaries of third-country banking groups report on a consolidated basis in the EU, the analysis also incorporates information from subsidiaries that operate and report on a solo basis.
