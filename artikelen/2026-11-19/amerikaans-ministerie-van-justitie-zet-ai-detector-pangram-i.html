The Department of Justice Is Using AI Detector Pangram Against Defense Attorneys

Lawyers for the Department of Justice are using the AI writing detection service Pangram to argue that opposing attorneys are using AI to write legal arguments, and in one case told the court that opposing counsel was doing “defense by ChatGPT,” and writing “AI slop” filings, according to court filings reviewed by 404 Media.

Over the last few years, we have documented many attorneys getting caught by judges using AI after citing hallucinated cases that do not exist. The filings in this court case over the past month show attorneys, including those working for the government, are trying to catch each other using AI, then are citing their opponents’ AI use in their arguments to the court.

“This case has turned into defense by ChatGPT,” government attorneys wrote in the case, which centers around a high-profile voter fraud case. “Motions practice in this case is the equivalent of writing responses to documents generated by a robot that is attempting to practice law. It cannot be that the local rules envisioned that it was acceptable for defense to use what the kids would call ‘AI slop’ to create multiple frivolous motions that waste judicial resources and fail to serve the cause of justice.”

The defendant is Khalwinder Bhengura, an Indian man who entered the United States on an immigrant visa in 2022 and illegally — and apparently accidentally, according to his lawyers — registered to vote in Texas in 2024 after falsely stating that he was an American citizen on his application for a driver’s license. Bhengura was arrested in September on charges of lying about his citizenship status, illegally registering to vote, and illegally voting in the 2024 election, and was found guilty in October on two of the three counts. His family and lawyers have argued that the whole thing is a misunderstanding, and that Bhengura, who is 70 years old and does not speak English well, did not know he was committing a crime and did not understand what he was doing.

Bhengura has been in prison for months and is set to be deported. He is now not only being held up as an example by the Trump administration of voter fraud but is also now caught in the middle of an AI lawyering dispute.
