6 Best Coffee Machines in Singapore (2026): Capsule, Bean-to-Cup and Espresso From S$198

A home coffee machine in Singapore costs S$198 to S$7,200, and the brand you pick decides the running cost more than the sticker. We read every price off Nespresso, Philips, De'Longhi, Breville, Smeg and Jura's own Singapore stores on 24 September 2026.

A decent home coffee machine pays for itself against a S$6 café latte, if you pick the right type.

Nespresso: cheapest way in, from S$198 with a sale to 27 Oct

Nespresso is the easy answer if you want a good coffee in 30 seconds with nothing to clean but a drip tray. It runs two capsule systems. Original makes espresso-sized shots and takes third-party pods (Lavazza, Starbucks and supermarket brands), so capsules are cheaper; machines run from the Essenza Mini at S$219 and the Inissia at S$259 to the Pixie at S$299. Vertuo reads a barcode on each pod and brews everything from an espresso to a large mug, but only takes Nespresso's own capsules; the Vertuo Pop is S$249, the new Vertuo Up S$339 on pre-order (a launch price, U.P. S$399), and the milk-integrated Vertuo Lattissima S$849. Until 27 October 2026, the Inissia is S$61 off (S$198) and the Vertuo Pop, Pop+, Lattissima, Creatista and Pixie are 15% off, which puts the Vertuo Pop at about S$212. Boutiques are at Raffles City, ION Orchard, Takashimaya and VivoCity, with free delivery from S$80.

Philips: best-value bean-to-cup, Series 1200 at S$699 and LatteGo from S$1,049

Philips was the only bean-to-cup brand with markdowns on its own store on 24 September, across the whole fully automatic range. The Series 1200 (EP1220) grinds fresh beans for espresso and coffee with a classic steam frother at S$699 (was S$888). The Series 2300 (EP2330) adds LatteGo, a two-part milk jug with no tubes that rinses clean in about 10 seconds, at S$1,049 (was S$1,288). The Series 3300 (EP3343) brews five hot and iced drinks at S$1,199 (was S$1,488), and the Series 4400 (EP4446) does 12 at S$1,469 (was S$1,688). All use ceramic grinders and an AquaClean filter that Philips rates at up to 5,000 cups before descaling. For black-coffee drinkers, the compact Baristina (BAR300) grinds, tamps and brews at S$699 (was S$899). Delivery is free islandwide, with a two-year warranty.

De'Longhi Magnifica S: the proven one-touch machine at S$699

The Magnifica S (ECAM12.122.B) is the bean-to-cup machine most Singapore kitchens end up with, and De'Longhi's own store lists it with over 1,700 reviews at a 4.3-star average. For S$699 you get a conical steel burr grinder, a Twin-Shot button that brews two espressos at once, adjustable strength and a manual steam wand for milk. The Magnifica Start is S$999 with a manual frother, or S$1,349 with LatteCrema one-touch milk drinks, the Rivelia with swappable bean hoppers S$1,999, and the range tops out at the Maestosa at S$6,999. If you would rather tamp and steam yourself, the La Specialista Arte (EC9155) with a built-in grinder is S$999. No discount was running on 24 September, and delivery is free above S$150.

Breville Bambino: best for learning real espresso, from S$568

Breville is the brand home baristas recommend first, and its Singapore store sells direct. The Bambino (S$568) heats up in about three seconds and has a manual steam wand for latte art practice; the Bambino Plus (S$698) adds an automatic wand that textures milk to a set temperature. Neither grinds, so budget for a grinder such as Breville's own Smart Grinder Pro at S$428, or buy pre-ground coffee. The Barista Express Impress (S$1,398) puts a grinder on board with assisted tamping that measures the dose for you, the Barista Pro is S$1,598, and the Oracle, which grinds, doses, tamps and steams automatically, is S$3,698. Nothing on breville.sg was on sale on 24 September.

Smeg ECF02: the best-looking espresso machine, S$658

Smeg's 1950s-style machines are bought for the counter as much as the coffee, and the colours match its kettles and toasters. The ECF02 espresso machine (S$658) has a 15-bar pump, a Thermoblock heater, a 51mm stainless-steel portafilter that takes ground coffee or paper pods, and a steam wand for cappuccinos; it comes in several colours, including pastel blue. The bean-to-cup model is S$888, or S$1,088 with a steam wand, and the espresso machine with an integrated grinder is S$1,298 (S$1,398 in matte). Buy from smegshop.sg, Smeg's own Singapore store, where prices include GST. No coffee-machine promotion was listed on 24 September.

Jura: premium Swiss bean-to-cup, from S$1,450

Jura specialises in coffee machines, and its Swiss-built bean-to-cup range is what you buy if you want the machine to do everything and last a decade. The entry ENA 4 (S$1,450) is a compact machine for espresso and black coffee. The E4 is S$1,800, the C9 S$2,150, the E6 S$2,100, and the E8 (S$2,900) adds one-touch milk drinks through its fine-foam frother. The top end runs from the S8 at S$3,600 to the Z10 at S$5,150 and the GIGA 10 at S$7,200. sg.jura-sea.com, which Jura's global site links to for Singapore, showed no discount on 24 September; delivery is free above S$120.

Frequently Asked Questions

It depends on how you drink coffee. For the lowest upfront cost and no mess, a Nespresso capsule machine wins: the Inissia is S$198 and the Vertuo Pop is 15% off S$249 until 27 October 2026. If you drink lattes every day, a bean-to-cup machine pays back its higher price in cheaper beans: the Philips Series 2300 with LatteGo is S$1,049 (was S$1,288) and the De'Longhi Magnifica S is S$699. If you want to learn to pull shots and steam milk yourself, the Breville Bambino at S$568 is the cheapest proper espresso machine from a major brand here.

Bean-to-cup is usually cheaper per cup, capsules are cheaper to start. A capsule machine costs S$200-S$300, but every coffee needs a pod. A bean-to-cup machine costs S$699 or more, then each cup uses about 7-10g of beans, and a 1kg bag makes well over 100 shots. If you make two coffees a day, the gap in running cost usually covers the higher machine price within a year or two. If you make a few coffees a week, capsules stay cheaper.
