Next-day securities settlement, tokenisation and the transformation of Europe’s financial infrastructure

We are approaching an important milestone.[1]

Less than a year from now, on 11 October 2027, Europe will move to T+1 settlement. This means that the time from the moment a security is traded to the moment the buyer receives it and the seller is paid will be reduced from two days to one.

This may sound like a technical change, but it is in fact much more significant.

The transition to T+1 is part of a broader transformation of Europe’s financial infrastructure. And the objective is clear: to make Europe’s financial and payment system more efficient, more resilient and safer, and ultimately to make European financial markets more competitive.

In the Eurosystem, we are contributing to this transformation across the full spectrum of our mandate.

We are ensuring that banknotes remain available and fit for purpose. We are preparing for a digital euro. We are modernising wholesale payments and securities settlement. And we are working to enhance cross-border payments and harness new technologies such as distributed ledger technology (DLT).[2]

These are not separate projects. They are different parts of the same strategy: ensuring that the euro and Europe’s financial markets are fit for the future in a rapidly changing world.

From T+2 to T+1: an essential element of a broader modernisation effort

The move to T+1 contributes to this broader strategy.

It is about more than just reducing the time between trade and settlement to one day.

A shorter settlement cycle can reduce market and counterparty risk.

But, perhaps even more importantly for Europe, the transition is also forcing us to review our post-trade processes. It requires greater standardisation, automation and innovation. Inefficiencies that could be accommodated in a T+2 environment will become much more difficult to sustain under T+1.

In that sense, T+1 is a catalyst for modernising European post-trade markets. And this is important for Europe’s competitiveness.

Efficient post-trade infrastructure makes it easier for capital to flow across borders. It helps investors access European markets. And it supports the EU’s wider ambition to create deeper and more integrated capital markets.

It also ensures that Europe keeps pace internationally with other major markets as they shorten their own settlement cycles.

That is why the ECB, together with the European Securities and Markets Authority and the European Commission, strongly supports the market’s preparations for T+1.

There is another lesson for Europe here which I believe is particularly important: we can deliver when we work together.

Europe’s financial landscape remains complex and fragmented. Yet the work coordinated by the EU T+1 Industry Committee has shown that those differences do not need to stand in the way of common solutions.

I would therefore like to congratulate everyone involved for the progress made so far. It is a powerful reminder of what Europe can achieve when public authorities and the private sector come together around a common objective.

But we have not yet crossed the finish line.

The coming months will be critical. Implementation and testing need to advance with the same determination and cooperation that have been the driving force behind the project so far.

The Eurosystem is also responsible for operating TARGET2-Securities (T2S), our platform for securities settlement in central bank money through which cash and securities can be exchanged simultaneously across Europe.[3]

And the key message here is a simple one: T2S already supports shorter settlement cycles. It already settles substantial volumes of transactions on a T+1 and even a T+0 basis.

At the same time, we are adapting some T2S processes to help the market operate efficiently under the tighter timetable that T+1 will bring. This includes changes to the night-time settlement process, which are planned for June 2027 ahead of the market’s migration to T+1 settlement in October 2027.

We will continue working closely with market participants and other European authorities to ensure that the transition to T+1 settlement goes smoothly.

Preparing for the financial system of tomorrow

But while we prepare for T+1, we also need to look further ahead.

Technology is reshaping finance. Distributed ledger technology and tokenisation offer opportunities not only to make today’s processes faster by enabling the near-instant transfer of securities, but potentially to simplify the architecture of finance itself. Tokenisation could bring issuance, trading, clearing, settlement, custody and asset servicing into a shared digital environment.

The Eurosystem intends to be part of that transformation, and we have already moved from vision to delivery.[4]

Since 30 March, the Eurosystem has been accepting marketable assets issued in central securities depositories using distributed ledger technology as eligible collateral for its credit operations.[5] We are now assessing whether, how and under what criteria DLT-based assets outside currently eligible settlement systems could become eligible collateral. We will ensure that our collateral framework keeps pace with innovation while upholding our statutory requirement for collateral to be adequate.
