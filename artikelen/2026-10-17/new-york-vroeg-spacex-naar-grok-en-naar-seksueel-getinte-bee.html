SpaceX AI policy leader grilled by NYC Council on Grok safety, sexualized images

The SpaceX headquarters is seen on Monday, Aug. 3, 2026 in Hawthorne, CA.

Eric Thayer | Los Angeles Times | Getty Images

[SpaceX](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/SPCX/) artificial intelligence policy leader Gabriela Castillo Madrid faced a tough line of questioning from NYC Council Speaker Julie Menin and other members on Friday as part of a council investigation into AI safety.

When Castillo Madrid claimed that the company had appropriate “safeguards” in place around its AI products, Menin immediately confronted her.

“I’m going to completely push back on that,” Menin said. “I mean, just this morning — myself and my team — we put our own names, my own name, into Grok, and there’s absolutely a sexualized image of myself. So then, how is it that within a second, I could find a sexualized image of myself using Grok?”

Castillo Madrid responded that the company has “strict controls” and would have the safety team look into that specific instance.

NYC Council Member Carmen De La Rosa, who leads the council’s tech committee, told Castillo Madrid that “it is disgusting” that Grok enables users to easily “sexualize” images of real women without their consent.

“I just don’t understand how you can say that there are these safe models,” Menin said. “It’s just unacceptable.”

Castillo Madrid appeared in front of the council’s tech committee by video.

[Elon Musk’s](https://www.cnbc.com/elon-musk/) aerospace, defense tech and AI company originally evaded requests and a subpoena from the city council to [appear](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/05/anthropic-openai-google-meta-execs-testify-nyc-council-ai-hearing.html) alongside leaders from [OpenAI](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/OPENAI.FG/), [Anthropic](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/ANTHR.FG/), [Google](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/GOOG/) and [Meta](https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/META/) on Oct. 5. SpaceX [agreed](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/13/spacex-agrees-to-testify-to-nyc-council-about-ai-safety-.html) to testify earlier this week after the council threatened to have its subpoena enforced by a judge.

The council opened its probe following a month of intense debate surrounding the risks posed by advanced AI models, and is aiming to use testimony and information unearthed through the investigation to guide new regulations.

Before Castillo Madrid took a role in government affairs at SpaceX earlier this year, she worked at X, Musk’s social media company that’s now owned by SpaceX, according to her [LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/gabrielacastillom/details/experience/?locale=en-US&isSelfProfile=false&vieweeProfileId=ACoAAAUmlNQBy3Bs8mnmAApWrOkgsF80GxyQ_9c). Prior to that, she spent almost three years at the U.S. Department of Commerce.

De La Rosa asked Castillo Madrid how many whistleblowers the company has fired, to which Castillo Madrid said she was “not aware” of any.

“I just want to interject for a minute because Devin Kim, one of your former employees, was reportedly fired after he sounded the alarm about serious safety concerns,” Menin said.

“I’m not aware with that specific case,” Castillo Madrid responded, adding that the company has a culture where “everybody has a an ability to say something when they see something.”

Kim is now President of the nonprofit Center for AI Safety, a think tank focused on AI risks and safety.

Kim began his testimony before the council by outlining his experience as one of the first employees at xAI, saying that he “built the systems that researchers use to train and evaluate Grok.” Kim said that he started raising concerns in January 2025 and those worries were shut down by his manager.

“My supervisor responded that AI safety was useless, and that AI would kill us and all anyways,” he said.

Kim appeared by video, along with his attorney, following Castillo Madrid’s questioning.

Regarding Grok-generated images, Kim said under prior standards at the company, “tasteful nudity was acceptable and is the goal.”

“During my time there, there were only three employees working on AI safety, many of whom left after I resigned,” Kim said. “And I think in large part due to the frustration with how safety was being handled.”

The council also pressed Castillo Madrid about other areas of misuse with Grok and the types of safeguards that were in place.

Councilmember Mercedes Narcisse asked why the company promoted the idea of users uploading X-rays to Grok, asking if that was safe and if HIPPA protections applied.

Narcisse said it was dangerous to tell people “openly on X, to put their MRI, X-rays,” into Grok and expect “a definite answer” on health matters. The council member then upbraided the SpaceX representative for the vagueness of some of her replies.

“You’re supposed to give us answers. You’re not,” she said.

In merging SpaceX with xAI in February, Musk combined his AI and social networking business with his rocket launch company, which also owns the Starlink satellite internet service. The merger put SpaceX in control of AI model Grok, which is used by some federal agencies, including the [Pentagon](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/16/usdepartment-of-justice-calls-for-dismissal-of-naacp-xai-lawsuit-.html).

SpaceX [went public](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/12/spacex-ipo-spcx-live-updates.html) in June and is now valued at about $2 trillion. In August, SpaceX completed a $60 billion acquisition of AI coding startup Cursor.

In skipping the earlier NYC Council hearing, SpaceX avoided direct questioning from a member of the U.K. Parliament, Jess Asato, who testified about experiencing AI-enabled online harassment.
