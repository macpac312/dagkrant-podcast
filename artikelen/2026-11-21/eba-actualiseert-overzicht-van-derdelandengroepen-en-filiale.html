The EBA publishes updated information on third-country groups and branches operating in the EU/EEA | European Banking Authority

The European Banking Authority (EBA) has published its 2026 update on third-country groups and third-country branches operating in the European Union and the European Economic Area (EU/EEA). The publication provides a comprehensive overview of the presence, ownership structures and organisational arrangements of third-country banking groups active across the EU/EEA, supporting transparency in the European banking sector.

The 2026 update identified 441 third-country groups from 51 non-EU/EEA jurisdictions, including sovereign countries and other separately supervised territories, with operations in the EU/EEA.

Among these groups, 9 have established an intermediate EU parent undertaking (IPU), including 5 dual IPUs, in line with the requirements set out in the Capital Requirements Directive (CRD). In addition, 58 third-country groups operate through branches, representing a total of 84 third-country branches across the EU/EEA.

By making this information publicly available, the EBA contributes to greater transparency regarding the organisational presence of third-country banking groups in the EU/EEA and supports the consistent application of the EU regulatory framework.
