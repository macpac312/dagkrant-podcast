F1 Las Vegas GP: George Russell tops final practice after Max Verstappen collision

George Russell collided with Max&nbsp;Verstappen in a tense third practice session at the Las Vegas Grand Prix as the Mercedes Formula 1 driver topped the timesheets. After both of Thursday's practice sessions had been derailed by rain, the Las Vegas Strip was buzzing with activity at dusk on Friday. The sole remaining practice session became crucial as it presented the only dry running heading into qualifying and the race, as all 22 cars sprinted out of the&nbsp;pitlane in short order. Amid chilly 14C temperatures, the Thursday rainfall only made the 6.2km circuit around the Las Vegas Strip even more difficult to drive, with surface grip extremely limited. Verstappen survived a sideways scare and steering rack issues to lead the early running with a 1m35.675s on soft tyres, by far the most popular tyre in cold conditions as it was easier to fire up to temperature. Just after the halfway mark Russell took over top spot with a 1m35.124s, the Briton aiming to finish the season on a high as he declined a Mercedes turbo upgrade to avoid a grid penalty. Russell further lowered his own benchmark to a 1m34.805s lap, before becoming one of several cars to find the runoff area on the slippery circuit. The urgency to get plenty of clean laps in was on full display with&nbsp;Verstappen and Franco Colapinto almost coming to blows in their quest for clean air. Verstappen's Red Bull team-mate Isack Hadjar also lobbied for a penalty for Williams' Alex Albon.

Annoyed by Verstappen forcing his way past, Lando Norris couldn't hide his glee when the four-time world champion subsequently locked up and sailed into the runoff area. "Instant karma, amazing," he said over team radio. The biggest clash followed right at the end of the session, again involving&nbsp;Verstappen, as he tried a similar move on Russell on the straight. Undeterred, Russell sent his Mercedes down the inside of Turn 14 to re-pass the Red Bull, making side-on contact as he muscled Verstappen off the track. The incident will be investigated by the FIA’s stewards. "Fun and games," Russell described the incident on the radio. Norris initially came closest to overhauling Russell in the closing minutes of the session, settling for second with a 1m35.060s lap. Ahead of&nbsp;Verstappen, Colapinto was fifth for Alpine on a circuit that should suit the Enstone team's slippery car, as evidenced by the Pierre Gasly's shock pole back in Monza.
