Thousands of Gallons of Heat-Transfer Fluid Spill at xAI's Southaven Site

On Nov. 14, 2026, an estimated 5,000 to 6,000 gallons of DOWFROST HD Heat Transfer Fluid spilled at xAI’s data-center site in Southaven, Mississippi. The product is a propylene glycol-based heat-transfer fluid, and some of the released material reached a nearby drainage ditch before crews intervened.

SpaceX, which operates the facility, attributed the release to a contractor error. A hose was incorrectly connected to a mobile tank, allowing the fluid to escape.

The company said its team stopped the flow and began cleanup within hours. SpaceX coordinated with the Mississippi Department of Environmental Quality, Southaven police, and Southaven fire officials during the initial response.

MDEQ Says the Chemical Stopped Short of Horn Lake Creek

Crews installed berms to contain the spill in the drainage ditch, and state officials issued no public-health alert.

Crews installed berms to contain the fluid, and MDEQ personnel confirmed the material remained in the affected ditch. According to MDEQ, it did not reach Horn Lake Creek.

The agency said the spill posed no immediate threat to public health or safety. That characterization reflects the agency’s assessment at the time of response; it does not constitute a final environmental clearance.

Manufacturer documentation cited in reporting describes DOWFROST HD as a nonhazardous mixture under OSHA standards. That classification reflects the product’s formulation, not a site-specific environmental assessment of the spill’s impact.

Residents and Advocates Still Waiting on Answers

Water sampling results, recovered volumes, and cleanup records have not been publicly confirmed as of available reporting.

Community members and environmental advocates continued pressing for details after the initial response. Those details include water sampling outcomes, final recovered volumes, and documentation of cleanup completion.

As of available reporting, none of those results had been publicly confirmed. Residents and advocates have sought broader information about potential effects on nearby drainage systems and adjacent properties, as well as the broader impact of AI data centers on surrounding communities.
