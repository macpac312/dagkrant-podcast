Elecciones generales del 29 de noviembre · Demócrata

The European Central Bank has warned financial institutions that the impact of recent geopolitical 'shocks' may take time to become "fully visible" in their balance sheets, and therefore urges the banking sector to act with "prudence" in taking on risks, to strengthen "solid" governance, and to plan in advance its capital and liquidity to absorb "possible disruptions" without restricting the flow of credit to households and businesses.

This message accompanies the results of the 2026 Supervisory Review and Evaluation Process (PRES) and the supervisory priorities for 2027-2029, in which the agency finds that European banks maintain solid positions in terms of solvency and liquidity, as well as high profitability.

However, the Chair of the ECB Supervisory Board, Claudia Buch, has reminded in the press conference following the presentation of the report that "resilience does not mean immunity" and has acknowledged that institutions "remain vulnerable" to risks to financial stability, emphasizing that it is still "too early" for all the effects of geopolitical tensions to be reflected in their accounts.

In this context, operational and technological risk continues to be the sector's main weak point. According to the monetary authority, 92% of the supervised entities have "deficiencies in cybersecurity, third-party risk management, and controls in Information and Communication Technologies (ICT)".

To these weaknesses are added persistent problems in the aggregation and reporting of risk data due to failures in the IT architecture and an excessive dependence on manual processes. Therefore, the supervisory roadmap for 2027-2029 will be structured around two main axes: strengthening financial resilience in the face of geopolitical and macroeconomic tensions—by controlling credit granting standards, implementing Basel III (CRR III), exposure to non-bank financial entities, and climate risks—and strengthening operational resilience against technological threats.

In relation to the first block, the representative of the European supervisor has explained that, although most banks show a “solid” understanding of geopolitical risk, the ECB has detected that some entities “underestimate the links between solvency and liquidity”. In this sense, it has warned that mitigation mechanisms such as capital increases “may not be available in a crisis if many banks try to adjust in the same way”, in the possibility that a bottleneck may arise in the markets if multiple entities seek to raise funds at the same time in a scenario of systemic tension.

As for technological risk, the ECB has stated that it will carry out “in situ” inspections on cybersecurity and external providers, penetration tests within the framework of the DORA regulation, and specific analyses on artificial intelligence-driven cyber threats and on the transition to post-quantum cryptography.

Fewer new qualitative measures in the SREP

In parallel, the body this year completed the reform of the SREP review to make it “more agile” and focused on “more severe” risks. This simplification has allowed decisions to be communicated earlier and has reduced by half (-50%) the new qualitative measures, which were directed at 78 banks to correct deficiencies in credit risk (33%), internal governance (24%), and operational risk (12%).

In addition, after debuting in this cycle a new Pillar 2 methodology—one that adjusts capital better to each entity’s individual profile and avoids overlaps with Pillar 1—and applying the liquidity framework in 2027, the supervisor does not envisage any further methodological changes in the next two years.

As a result of the gradual implementation of Basel III (CRR III), the top supervisor for Banking Supervision in the euro area has detailed that, in this evaluation cycle, the Pillar 1 requirements fell or remained unchanged in around two thirds of the entities. In the remaining third, where Pillar 1 requirements increased, the ECB adjusted Pillar 2 requirements downward on a case-by-case basis to avoid double counting of risks.

The annual review, which covers 108 significant entities, yields a stable overall average score of 2.5 (on a scale of 1 to 4), with 86% of banks repeating the same rating as in 2025, 8% improving, and the remaining 7% worsening.

For the next exercise, the ECB has decided to raise by 10 basis points the global capital requirements and recommendations of the highest quality ('CET 1'), to 11.3% of risk-weighted assets from 11.2% this year. At the same time, the specific Pillar 2 requirement (P2R) in 'CET 1' increases to 1.2% (from the current 1.1%). In terms of total capital, the overall threshold rises by one tenth to 15.7% (from 15.6% for 2026), with a Pillar 2 of around 2%.

Surcharges and additional leverage requirements

In the context of the supervision of non-performing exposures and leveraged transactions, the ECB has communicated that, in the 2026 PRES cycle, surcharges were applied in P2R for insufficiently provisioned non-performing exposures to five entities, compared with the nine of the previous cycle. At the same time, the authority maintains close oversight of leveraged funding and incorporates the capital requirements associated directly in the specific P2R for each bank's credit risk, without specifying in this exercise the number of affected entities.

Likewise, the supervisor has imposed an additional Pillar 2 leverage ratio requirement (P2R-LR)—between 10 and 25 basis points—on 14 entities to cover risks of excessive leverage not captured in Pillar 1, which is added to the minimum leverage ratio of 3%, mandatory for all credit institutions. In addition, five banks have received a Pillar 2 leverage ratio recommendation (P2G-LR).

Overall, the total leverage requirements and recommendations for 2027 are located at a weighted average close to 3.3%, compared with the actual average leverage ratio of 5.8% recorded by banking in the second quarter of 2026.
