ECB keeps capital requirements broadly stable for 2027, focuses its priorities on banks’ financial and operational resilience

[Paragraph 12 of this press release was amended at 17:15 on 20 November 2026 to reflect a late revision to theAggregated results of the 2026 SREP, which had not been included here. The percentage of significant institutions mentioned was changed from 92% to 96%.]

The European Central Bank (ECB) today published the results of its Supervisory Review and Evaluation Process (SREP) for 2026 and its supervisory priorities for 2027-29. The assessment covers 108 banks directly supervised by the ECB and reviews their capital, liquidity, business model, governance and risk management.

The euro area banking sector maintained robust capital and liquidity positions amid heightened geopolitical risks and macro-financial uncertainty. The weighted average total capital ratio stood at 20.1% in the second quarter of 2026 and the fully phased-in leverage ratio stood at 5.8%.

Liquidity buffers remained well above the 100% minimum requirement, with the aggregate liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) at 155% in the second quarter of 2026. The banking sector retained good access to retail and wholesale funding, with an average net stable funding ratio (NSFR) of 125%.

Profitability continued to be strong. The aggregated annualised return on equity increased from 9.5% in 2025 to almost 11% in the second quarter of 2026. Net fee and commission income supported revenue growth, and net interest income was helped by higher loan volumes and improving interest margins. Strong revenues helped reduce the aggregate cost-to-income ratio slightly, from 55% to 53%.

Asset quality continued to be relatively stable, with the aggregate non-performing loan ratio remaining at low levels at 1.9% in the second quarter of 2026 and the aggregate Stage 2 loan ratio declining. Nonetheless, vulnerabilities persisted in some portfolios, such as consumer credit and small and medium-sized enterprises. While conditions in commercial real estate markets showed signs of stabilising, uncertainty and downside risks persist.

The impact of recent geopolitical shocks may take time to become fully visible on banks’ balance sheets. Banks therefore need to take a prudent risk-taking approach, coupled with sound governance and forward-looking capital and liquidity planning to absorb potential shocks and continue providing financing to households and businesses also under stress.

Quantitative and qualitative supervisory measures

Overall capital requirements and guidance in CET1 for 2027 remained broadly stable at 11.3% of risk-weighted assets, compared with 11.2% in 2026. The Pillar 2 requirement to be met with CET1 capital also remained broadly stable at 1.2%, compared with 1.1% in 2026. Non-binding Pillar 2 guidance in 2027 remained unchanged at 1.1%.

Similarly, overall capital requirements and guidance applicable in 2027, in terms of total capital, remained broadly stable at 15.7% of risk-weighted assets, compared with 15.6% in 2026, and total capital Pillar 2 requirements continued to stand at around 2%.

To address the risk of excessive leverage, the ECB applied binding leverage ratio Pillar 2 requirements to 14 banks. It also set non-binding leverage ratio Pillar 2 guidance for five banks in order to strengthen their resilience under stress.

The average overall SREP score in 2026 remained unchanged at 2.5. 86% of banks were given the same overall score as in 2025, with upgrades or downgrades mainly attributable to non-financial risks.

In 2026, the ECB issued new qualitative measures to 78 banks. Compared with the previous year, the number of these new measures fell by around 50%. Higher priority is given to material weaknesses and major supervisory concerns. Severe and persistent deficiencies are swiftly followed up and enforcement action is taken if banks fail to remediate these deficiencies. The new qualitative measures primarily concerned credit risk (33%), internal governance (24%) and operational risk (12%). These measures targeted shortcomings in non-performing exposure management, in management body effectiveness, and in risk data aggregation and risk reporting.

Operational and ICT risk continued to be one of the weakest-scoring categories, with 96% of significant institutions revealing deficiencies in cybersecurity, third-party risk management and ICT risk controls. Banks also needed to strengthen their risk data aggregation and risk reporting capabilities, as weaknesses in data quality, IT architecture and an overreliance on manual processes continued to hamper effective risk management.

Supervisory priorities for 2027-29

The supervisory priorities for 2027-29 reflect the risk environment and ensure continuity with the previous set of supervisory priorities. The two key priorities in this cycle are to strengthen banks’ financial resilience to geopolitical risks and macro-financial shocks and to reinforce operational resilience and ICT capabilities.

The first priority focuses on prudent credit underwriting standards and risk-based loan pricing. Furthermore, supervisors will continue to assess whether the Capital Requirements Regulation (CRR III) has been accurately and consistently implemented, particularly with regard to the calculation of risk-weighted assets for credit and operational risk under the standardised approach.

In addition, supervisors will address potential vulnerabilities in banks with material exposures to non-bank financial institutions, including fund financing and other private market activities. They will review significant risk transfer securitisation strategies and how these have been integrated into banks’ risk management and capital planning.

Climate and nature-related risks remain an integral part of the first priority. A thematic review in 2027 will assess banks’ prudential transition planning. Additionally, work will continue on persistent weaknesses in the management of physical risk.

The second priority focuses on banks’ remediation of material deficiencies in ICT risk management. Activities will include on-site inspections on cybersecurity and third-party risk management, threat-led penetration testing under the Digital Operational Resilience Act and targeted reviews of dependencies on cloud-based service providers.

Supervisors will assess banks’ responses to AI-enabled cyber threats and their preparedness for post-quantum cryptography migration. Furthermore, supervisors will increasingly engage with the industry and other relevant authorities with regard to the management of risks stemming from AI use cases.

Finally, banks will be expected to remedy material weaknesses in risk data aggregation and risk reporting capabilities within agreed deadlines, with enforcement measures to be imposed if necessary.

SREP reform and methodologies

The ECB completed its reform of the SREP in 2026, making supervisory assessments more effective, efficient and risk-based. The revised risk tolerance framework and multi-year assessments mean that banks and supervisors can focus on the most material risks and major emerging concerns. As a result, SREP decisions are shorter and focus on banks’ key vulnerabilities, with fewer qualitative measures being imposed. By fully integrating the forward planning of supervisory activities, the examination programmes and the SREP outcomes are now communicated earlier than was previously the case.

The revised Pillar 2 methodology was used for the first time in this year’s SREP cycle to determine the capital requirements effective from January 2027. It reduces complexity and creates a more direct link between capital requirements and risk drivers. It has not led to a change in the overall capital requirements at the system level, rather it changes the requirements of individual banks’ to reflect how bank-specific risk profiles are addressed under the revised methodology.

Additionally, supervisors were particularly attentive to potential overlaps of Pillar 1 and Pillar 2 requirements in the context of the EU’s implementation of the final Basel III reforms, which affect the calculation of risk-weighted assets. The effects of the CRR III so far have generally been minor and different across banks, often leading to a reduction in Pillar 1 capital requirements. Where Pillar 1 requirements increased and overlaps were identified, the ECB made reductions to Pillar 2 requirements. The ECB will continue to monitor these effects and address the potential double counting of risks on a case-by-case basis as the new rules are phased in.

After introducing the revised Pillar 2 methodology this year and the liquidity methodology applicable from 2027, no further changes to SREP methodologies are expected over the next two years and future revisions will be less frequent than during the years of the SREP reform. This will provide banks with a more stable supervisory framework for their capital, liquidity and strategic planning, while maintaining the necessary supervisory rigour and resilience standards.
