Xiaomi 18 Pro Max Geekbench Listing Reveals 5.11GHz Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 Pro — BigGo Finance

A fresh Geekbench listing has given the first concrete look at Qualcomm's next flagship mobile processor, revealing a chip that pushes clock speeds past the 5GHz barrier as Android manufacturers scramble to counter Apple's newly announced A20 Pro silicon.

The Xiaomi 18 Pro Max, identified by the Chinese model number M1544F, surfaced on the benchmarking platform carrying Qualcomm's unreleased SM8975 chipset, tentatively named the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 Pro. The octa-core configuration detailed in the listing shows an aggressive three-tier architecture: three efficiency cores running at 3.74GHz, three performance cores at 4.03GHz, and two prime cores that reach a blistering 5.11GHz.

Paired with an Adreno 850 GPU and 12GB of RAM, the hardware combination posted 3,582 points in the single-core test and 12,247 points in the multi-core test on Geekbench 7. The listing also provided early insight into graphical performance, with the Adreno 850 recording 26,210 points in the OpenCL test and 29,934 points in the Vulkan benchmark. While pre-release hardware scores are subject to software optimization and can fluctuate, these numbers indicate a substantial leap in graphical rendering capabilities for mobile gaming and on-device AI generation.

The benchmark also confirms that the Xiaomi 18 Pro Max will ship running Android 17 out of the box, layered with Xiaomi's latest HyperOS 4 skin. Qualcomm is expected to officially unveil the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 series at its Snapdragon Summit, scheduled for September 22–24 in Maui, Hawaii, with rival flagships like the Vivo X500 Pro Max also making benchmarking rounds.

A Shifting Competitive Landscape

The Xiaomi listing arrives just a day after Apple took the wraps off the iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 18 Pro Max, and the first foldable iPhone Duo, all powered by the new A20 Pro system-on-a-chip. Early Geekbench 6.7 results for the A20 Pro, spotted from an iPhone19,3 device likely to be the iPhone 18 Pro Max, show a six-core CPU arranged in a 2+4 configuration with the two performance cores reaching 4.93GHz. The chip scored 4,725 points in single-core and 12,575 points in multi-core, with 12GB of physical RAM.

A separate listing from Tom's Hardware reported slightly different figures for the same chip: 4,719 single-core and 12,677 multi-core, roughly 25% faster than the A19 Pro from last year's iPhone 17 Pro. Apple's official claim was a more conservative 20% improvement.

The comparison between Apple's and Qualcomm's upcoming silicon reveals a familiar pattern, though the numbers need careful handling. An earlier listing believed to come from an iQOO flagship showed the same SM8975 chip scoring 3,434 single-core and 9,334 multi-core despite hitting 5.01GHz; set against those figures, the A20 Pro is roughly 38% faster in single-core and 35% faster in multi-core. The newer Xiaomi run of 3,582 single-core and 12,247 multi-core cannot be lined up against Apple as neatly, because it was recorded on Geekbench 7 while the A20 Pro results come from Geekbench 6.7.

Primate Labs released Geekbench 7 on July 23 with new workloads, larger data sets and a redesigned multi-core test that runs a task multi-threaded only when real applications do. Early independent testing found that Geekbench 7 produces lower numbers than Geekbench 6 on the same hardware and that the two versions' scores are not directly comparable. Setting the Xiaomi result directly against the A20 Pro would therefore tend to exaggerate Apple's advantage, and the narrow multi-core gap it appears to show is not a like-for-like measurement.

Note: Geekbench 6 and Geekbench 7 scores are not directly comparable. A dash means the figure was not given in the reported listing; the second A20 Pro row is the separately reported result cited above.

MediaTek's Dimensity 9600 Pro, spotted in a Vivo phone, puts up a much stronger fight. That chip scored 4,137 points in single-core and 12,751 in multi-core with a peak clock of 4.55GHz, leaving Apple with a roughly 14% single-core lead while MediaTek pulls fractionally ahead in multi-core by about 1.4%.

Xiaomi's in-house Xring O3 chip adds another interesting data point. In the Xiaomi Pad 9 Pro Max, the 10-core chip scores 3,699 points in single-core and 14,350 in multi-core. The A20 Pro is around 28% faster in single-core, but Xiaomi turns the tables in multi-core, where the Xring O3 leads Apple's six-core chip by roughly 14%.

The A20 Pro's GPU has also surfaced in a separate Geekbench Metal run, scoring 64,069 points compared with 45,683 points for the A19 Pro in an iPhone 17 Pro Max, a roughly 40% generational improvement. Analysts attribute the gains to an extra GPU core and the transition to TSMC's newer GAAFET-based N2 node. Because that result comes from the Geekbench 6.7 Metal test, it cannot be set against the Adreno 850's Geekbench 7 OpenCL and Vulkan scores either.

Xiaomi's Broader Offensive

Xiaomi's hardware push extends beyond the 18 Pro Max. The company officially announced the Xiaomi 18 Fold on September 7, its first wide-format foldable smartphone, just two days before Apple unveiled its own foldable iPhone. The 18 Fold features a 5.38-inch outer display and a 7.58-inch inner display, both 120Hz LTPO panels capable of up to 4,000 nits peak brightness. A "Dragon Bone" hinge gives the phone a rounded profile and a thickness of 5.02 millimeters when open, with the device weighing 219 grams.

The foldable runs Xiaomi's in-house Xring O3 chip built on a 3nm process, featuring a 10-core CPU that reaches up to 4.35GHz, a 16-core GPU, and a 200 TOPS NPU for AI workloads. Xiaomi claims the setup boosts multi-core CPU performance and improves GPU performance by 85% over its previous chip. The rear camera array includes a 200MP main lens, a 50MP telephoto with 3.5x zoom, and a 50MP ultrawide, all from Leica. A 6,000mAh battery supports 67W wired and 50W wireless charging. Pricing starts at 10,999 yuan (approximately $1,640) and goes up to 14,999 yuan (approximately $2,235), with availability initially limited to China.

The Xring O3 itself has posted impressive benchmarks. On Geekbench 6, the Xiaomi Pad 9 Pro Max scored 3,699 single-core and 14,350 multi-core, representing around a 30% CPU uplift over last year's Xring O1. On 3DMark's Steel Nomad Light test, the Xring O3 scored 4,658, over 100% higher than the Xring O1 and about 45% better than the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5.

Apple opens pre-orders for the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max on September 12 in more than 65 countries and regions, including the U.S. and Japan, with retail release on Friday, September 18; the iPhone Duo follows with pre-orders from October 16 and a release on October 23. Review embargoes typically lift a few days before retail, meaning a broader range of independent benchmarks should emerge in the coming week. The timing sets up a direct showdown between Apple's vertically integrated silicon and the best Qualcomm and MediaTek can offer, with Xiaomi positioning itself as the most aggressive challenger in the premium large-screen segment.

What It Means for Buyers in Japan

For Japanese buyers, the Apple side of this comparison arrives first. Apple's Japanese pricing starts at ¥219,800 for the iPhone 18 Pro and ¥239,800 for the iPhone 18 Pro Max, including consumption tax, with pre-orders opening at 9 p.m. Japan time on September 12. The iPhone Duo starts at ¥364,800 in Japan.

The Android side is less settled. Xiaomi's current top model in Japan is the Xiaomi 17 Ultra, which went on sale there on March 5 at ¥199,800 for the 16GB/512GB version and runs on the current-generation Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5. It is sold through electronics retailers and e-commerce sites, the MVNO IIJmio and SoftBank's online shop. The 18 Pro Max itself has not been officially unveiled, and Xiaomi plans to sell the 18 Fold only in China for now, so the Geekbench figures above describe hardware that Japanese buyers cannot yet order.
