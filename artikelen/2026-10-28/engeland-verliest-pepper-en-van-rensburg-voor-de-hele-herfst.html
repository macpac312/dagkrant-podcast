England’s Nations Championship plans hit by Pepper and Van Rensburg double blow

Bath flanker Pepper injured ankle at Gloucester on Friday

Bristol’s Van Rensburg advised to rest after two concussions

England’s head coach, Steve Borthwick, said he will be monitoring this weekend’s domestic Prem games with some trepidation. Already he has lost at least two potentially key squad members for the entire autumn period in the form of Guy Pepper and Benhard van Rensburg, with doubts still hanging over his first-choice fly-half, Fin Smith, and fresh injury uncertainty emerging on virtually a daily basis.

Pepper’s involvement always looked improbable from the moment he sustained a nasty-looking ankle injury against Gloucester last Friday. The young Bath flanker will be sidelined for several weeks, as will the Bristol centre Van Rensburg who has been advised to rest and recuperate after two heavy concussions within the past three months.

Smith, meanwhile, is still being treated for the calf problem he sustained against Leicester last Saturday. Borthwick says it remains “unclear at this stage” whether the Northampton fly-half will be available to face Australia next week. Calf injuries and kicking are not a great combination and England also need their tactical hub to be at the heart of their preparations.

Borthwick, accordingly, will effectively want to make a call by the end of this week although England are slightly helped by the fact the Wallaby fixture is on a Sunday rather than 24 hours earlier. “Typically I would name the team for a Sunday game around the Wednesday, so that would still be my aim,” said Borthwick. “But if Fin’s not available for selection we also have some quality fly-halves in George Ford and Marcus Smith.”

The Bath hooker Kepu Tuipulotu has also limped away from training in Bagshot with a leg injury that will now be assessed, but there is more encouraging news regarding Seb Atkinson (knee) and Manny Feyi-Waboso (hamstring), both of whom should be available to play for Gloucester and Exeter respectively this weekend.

Borthwick’s preference would probably be for both players to be tucked up on their sofas but under the terms of the joint agreement between the Rugby Football Union and the clubs, the latter can pick their international players once they are cleared by England’s medical staff. “If they’re fit then they’re available for selection,” said Borthwick. “That then comes down to the head coach or director of rugby of the club.”

Either way it is a tense time for the national management who are having to do without their customary full extra week of training because of the need to shoehorn the new Nations Championship finals weekend into the calendar. Rugby players need to play rugby but, invariably, it comes with an element of risk attached. “Certainly there is a part of me watching these games and hoping the players get through the 80 minutes and are fit and available for next week,” said Borthwick.

The stakes are further heightened by next year’s looming World Cup, with Borthwick having pointedly reminded his players that only nine Tests remain before England’s World Cup preparations enter their final stages. The primary message is that, for those intending to be on the plane to Australia, performing at the requisite level starts now. “This series of games this autumn is the last opportunity we have playing southern hemisphere opposition before we’re at that World Cup,” said Borthwick.

“I’m looking forward to seeing players compete and perform against this calibre of opposition. You look at the power many of these southern hemisphere teams have. That’s a different challenge. When they come into camp again on Monday night, having recovered from their games, all that matters is performing the way we want to against Australia.”

Borthwick also brushed aside suggestions of potential discord after the fractious East Midlands derby between Leicester and Northampton where several of his squad were at loggerheads. The RFU sought to try to dilute any notion of friction by issuing a photo of Ollie Chessum and Henry Pollock with their arms cheerfully around each other and Borthwick has not yet deemed it necessary to pull anyone aside.

“I actually think it’s a real strength of the game to be able to compete like that in that kind of full-blooded encounter – and then they’re perfectly fine. It’s a strength of English rugby that the players have such pride in their clubs … in modern day sport I see that as a positive. But when you come in [here] it’s all about the England team.”
