Xiaomi 18 Pro Global Launch Confirmed For November

If you are one of the tech fans waiting to get your hands on the global variant of one of the hottest Chinese smartphones being sold now, we have great news to share: The recently unveiled Xiaomi 18 Pro and Xiaomi 18 Pro Max are scheduled to debut globally much earlier than typical release cycles, according to company president Lu Weibing.

Xiaomi confirmed that the flagship lineup will launch internationally in November, targeting markets across Europe, the Americas, and the Middle East. While a specific day remains unconfirmed, this accelerated rollout closes the release gap with China to secure the distinction of introducing the Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 and Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 platforms ahead of competitors.

A notable feature of this generation is the introduction of a secondary rear AMOLED display alongside enhanced privacy screen technology. The standard model features a 6.4-inch front display paired with a 2.7-inch rear screen, while the larger Pro Max sports a 6.9-inch front panel with a 2.9-inch secondary screen, all operating at a 120 Hz refresh rate.

Both smartphones feature memory configurations offering 12 GB or 16 GB of RAM combined with 256 GB, 512 GB, or 1 TB of internal storage, running HyperOS 4 built on Android 17. Photography capabilities are headlined by a co-engineered Leica triple-camera array, featuring two separate 200 MP sensors—a stabilized primary lens and a 3.2x optical zoom telephoto lens—supported by a 50 MP ultrawide camera and a 50 MP front sensor.

Ruggedness is addressed through multi-tier IP66, IP68, and IP69 environmental protection ratings, alongside connectivity options including 5G, Wi-Fi 7, and Bluetooth 6.0. Substantial capacity increases highlight the power system, with the base flagship packing a 7,000 mAh battery and the Pro Max housing an 8,500 mAh battery, both supported by 100 W fast charging.

However, regulatory requirements in specific international regions may require slight battery capacity adjustments compared to domestic models. Official international pricing remains unannounced, though industry forecasts anticipate modest increases corresponding to baseline cost shifts in the manufacturer's home market.
