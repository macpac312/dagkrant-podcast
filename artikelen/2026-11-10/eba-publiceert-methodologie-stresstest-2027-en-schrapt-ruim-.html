The EBA streamlines the 2027 EU-wide stress test with more than 55% reduction in requested data | European Banking Authority

The European Banking Authority (EBA) today released the final methodology, draft templates, and template guidance for its 2027 EU-wide stress test, together with the key milestones for the exercise. Following extensive engagement with industry participants, the EBA has significantly streamlined the exercise, reducing the number of required data points by 55% through the simplification of reporting templates and closer alignment with supervisory reporting. These changes will reduce operational burden and improve efficiency while preserving the robustness, risk-sensitivity and comparability of stress test results. The 2027 exercise will also include, for the first time, a dedicated module assessing climate-related risks. The 2027 EU-wide stress test will formally start in January 2027, following the release of the macroeconomic and climate scenarios, with the results scheduled for publication by the end of July 2027.

The 2027 EU-wide stress test will continue to follow a constrained bottom-up approach, complemented by top-down elements, and based on a static balance sheet assumption. It will assess the impact of adverse shocks on banks’ solvency across key risk areas and income streams under both baseline and adverse scenarios. The methodology also defines the sample of participating banks for the 2027 EU-wide stress test, although the sample may be subject to changes ahead of the launch of the exercise.

Compared with the previous exercise, the number of required data points has been reduced by 55%, mainly by streamlining the templates and relying more closely on regular supervisory reporting concepts, particularly in credit risk. These improvements are expected to reduce reporting burden and improve data quality and consistency.

For the first time, physical and transition climate risks will be assessed through a dedicated module, without affecting the core stress test results.

The draft stress test templates and guidance published today may be subject to minor technical adjustments before the formal launch of the exercise.
