8 High-End Coffee Makers Worth the Money

Moccamaster by Technivorm KBGV Select 10-Cup Coffee Maker

For your first high-end coffee maker, there's no machine more universally loved than the Moccamaster. It's relatively small, counter-friendly, and made simply so that it lasts for decades. It also just looks better than anything else on the market.

Here's its brew deal: Functionally, it is like a drip machine where you set it and forget it. But in practice, it is actually an automated pour-over machine. There is a nine-hole outlet that drips water in stages, at strategically placed times and at specific temperatures, so that you get a proper bloom and extraction phase. It takes a pretty quick six minutes, but the drawback is you can only brew a full or half carafe—no single cup. But if you are looking for the best non-espresso coffee maker in the business, the list could pretty much start and end right here.

Balmuda The Brew

Once you get past (or if you can get past) the sticker shock, Balmuda's coffee machine is bringing something special to the easy-cup-of-coffee table. The small-capacity machine makes just about a cup and a half of coffee, but it is as good of a cup and a half of coffee as you'll ever find. There are a few controls—three sizes and regular, strong, and ice brew options—perhaps a few more than you would expect or want.

However, the brew process is kind of extraordinary to watch. The water comes out in small, steamy drips at specific intervals and temperatures to get the most out of the grounds. The execution is just about perfect, though I find the water doesn't hit the grounds equally. The middle can end up over-extracted and the sides under. I find it brews better when I push some of the grounds toward the center so they get equally saturated, but for a coffee machine that costs $700, I would prefer the water head was a little bigger so that it would do this job for me.

Breville Bambino

“High-end” can mean different things to different people. If you want to move on from a Nespresso pod machine, this is would be our first port of call in figuring out if you will enjoy the life of “espresso guy.” Don’t go onto the expensive stuff until you have at least tried one of these.

What sets the Breville Bambino apart from other value-focused espresso machines is that it has the chops to be an end-game machine. On the build side, that is because the body of the machine is stainless steel, and the accents have the weight you get in the higher-end machines. On the brew side, it’s because it does a true low-pressure pre-infusion—a crucial part of the espresso process—and has incredible temperature control.

The main thing we think really sets it up as an in-between machine is the portafilter. It’s a 54-mm basket, the standard commercial size, and it has the option to go non-pressurized like commercial machines, or pressurized like more budget machines. What does that mean? If you have a good espresso-level grinder—since that is often a more important step—this machine can be as good as the eye-wateringly expensive ones you’re about to see. But, if you aren’t at that level, a pressurized basket can pull a decent shot with even Café Bustelo (no hate, we still drink it).

Breville Barista Touch

If you don’t need another difficult learning curve in your life (trust me, I relate), you might want something a tad easier than the De’Longhi. You don’t need to go fully automatic to turn espresso brewing into an intuitive three-step process. According to coffee snobs, the grind selection and tamping processes are the important and fun part. This machine keeps that intact, while boiling it down (hehe) to just selecting your grind size, dose, and then you can take the mathematically correct number of grounds for your drink of choice and tamper it and then let the machine pull your perfect shot.

It unfortunately doesn’t have some of the more handy automatic cleaning functions of the more full-auto machines. This means weekly upkeep, especially if you make frequent use of the milk wand, which for some people is an absolute no go.

Fellow Espresso Series 1

This is the espresso machine to get if you’re dedicated to learning. It will make your coffee and be your teacher all in one. First off, the thing just looks great. I’m a big fan of the cherry red and walnut color scheme myself. Inside, it’s basically a big computer dedicated to brewing the best espresso possible, and to make sure you improve it will even give suggestions over time—pro tip, if something’s off it’s usually your grind size. You still get all the fun of precision espresso making, with a wand for you to steam your own milk to boot, only you will actually learn how to pull a better shot; you won’t just stumble into it or, conversely, have a machine do your thinking for you.

Now, this is the Series 1 of a technology-based product, which means it may come with its fair share of potential issues. You may get one that works flawlessly. Or... a random system error can cause the whole thing to brick or the screen to be completely unusable. If it breaks, Fellow customer-service is genuinely interested in helping you out, so don’t be afraid to be a first-gen adopter. That said, I’m hoping a Series 2 will roll out a touch more seamlessly.

Meraki Dual Boiler Espresso Machine

This is without a doubt the most high-end espresso machine on the market right now. The rotary pump, dual boiler, and three-tier setup combine to make for a foolproof semi-auto machine that still leaves room for experimenting. Seriously, the shots we’ve been pulling from this have been consistent to an almost scary degree. Through all the bean switches, recipe tweaking, and shot dialing, the coffee was always passable. The dedicated tier for the grinder keeps your beans away from the hot water. In most coffee makers, they sit on top and over time this exposure to heat can lead to burnt beans and worse coffee.

What you end up with is the difference between needing to dial in the right settings, and the freedom to experiment. The Meraki machine is always the latter, giving you room to grow your hobby without making bad coffee along the way.

De'Longhi Magnifica Duo

DiFor as much as I love my KitchenAid automatic coffeemaker, the Magnifica Duo is a much better value. It’s one of the rare consumer machines where you can sit back and genuinely let it do all the work. It gives more options too, which is exactly what people want from an automatic espresso machine. Absolutely no fuss and the ability to choose any drink from the screen on a whim. It’s as easy as selecting what you want and waiting a couple of minutes. That’s the magic of fully automatic (or superautomatic) espresso.

If you’re really into the milk and foam art side of being a barista, this is the high-end machine to get. The dual milk frothing tank can make hot or cold foam from any kind of milk or milk alternative. From the touch-screen menu, you can adjust foam texture and tell it which dairy alternative you’re using too. But, really, at the end of the day you can still just put in whatever you have and tell it to make you a latte. The result will be tasty enough. If you care more than that, you can start making adjustments; just don’t try to dive too deep, it is fully automatic for a reason.

The Cumulus Machine

Remember when I said that if you wanted cold brew, you should get a dedicated machine for that? Well, here we are. This is the Cumulus and it’s overwhelmingly the easiest and quickest option for at home cold brew there is. It has three options that let you brew cold brew, nitro, and cold espresso. After just a 15 minute warm up (or cool down, I supposed) you’ll be able to brew any of the three options in just five minutes.

There is, however, a catch. The Cumulus makes really good cold brew because it brews from pods filled with coffee concentrate; not grounds. To do this you need to buy Cumulus’s proprietary pods because, right now, it is the only one in the game. That means you’re really at the whims of the company in terms of pricing. And if the company ever goes under, despite you loving your Cumulus, you won’t be able to keep using it without access to more pods.

A Note on Espresso Machine Terminology

Manual or Lever Machines (none featured):We couldadd a lever machine to this list. But, having tried and owned models, they are simply too fussy for the purposes of this story. If you’re on that level, we assume you don’t need this particular list.

Semi-automatic Espresso Machines:This is the bulk of the category. Everything from the La Marzocco your barista uses, the Lelit your boss raves about, and the entry-level Breville we mention. You are responsible for the entire process, so there is plenty of room to mess up, but they are not “manual” because of the existence of truly manual lever machines. If you have dreams about getting “serious” about your at-home barista hobby, this is the category you want to aim at.

Super Automatic Espresso Machines:This is the category where everything is done for you. Typically, you interact with a touch screen. You fill up the milk and water tanks, press cortado, and your drink is made for you. That’s it. You get to choose your beans and save money on expensive cafe drinks, but you’re not really tasked with much work. Get this if you don’t want to faff about with becoming a coffee guy.

What to Look for in High-End Coffee Makers

The size on some coffee machines can feel way different in person from how they appear online, particularly if you need to fill a reservoir or a burr grinder. You might need clearance, not just for the machine but for around the machine. When you're sizing your space, take into account the space on any side of the machine.

Size can be, but isn't necessarily, indicative of capacity. And when you get into these pricier high-end coffee machines, you'll notice capacity generally goes down.That's because the focus is on making a single great cup of coffee, often with methods that are more involved than a drip. When we are talking drip, we mean brew capacity, aka how much it can make at once. When talking espresso, it’s all about tank capacity; how much water can it hold at once before you need to refill?

For some people, automation is the entire point of getting a high-end coffee maker, as opposed to what we would call an espresso machine that's manual. And for that, we understand. Most of these are semi-automated, but we tried to include something for everyone, including one superautomatic machine.
