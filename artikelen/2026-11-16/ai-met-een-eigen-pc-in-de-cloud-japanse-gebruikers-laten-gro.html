SYSTEM NOTICE

Lately, whenever I open note, I keep seeing the term "Grok Bot" everywhere.

There are so many stories about AIs with similar names like Dots and Muse that I honestly got confused, but it seems Grok Bot is an "AI that has its own dedicated PC in the cloud." Hearing that, I thought I should look into it properly.

I haven't used it myself yet. This is just based on what I've researched.

It is an AI agent released as a beta version on August 11, 2026, by xAI (it seems it's now called SpaceXAI).

Normal AI just gives you an answer when you ask it in a chat and that's it, right? Grok Bot is different; it has a PC prepared in the cloud for each user, and it can open browsers, create files, and run commands on that PC to finish tasks completely before returning the results.

It keeps working even while you're asleep.

In overseas articles, it's described as a "24-hour colleague who works while you sleep," which I thought was a great way to put it.

It can also do things like: ・Create multiple named bots with assigned roles and have them collaborate in a group chat ・Learn procedures when you show them and automatically execute them at set times ・Follow up on stalled tasks on its own

The underlying PC environment is handled by Cursor, and the login is apparently "Sign in with Cursor" as well. It was a bit surprising to see an AI company and a code editor company teaming up.

It was released in August, so why the sudden surge now in November?

When I looked into it, there has been a lot of activity over the past two weeks or so.

On November 3, the calling feature was opened to all users (though only for US numbers). On November 10, integration with internal networks was announced. Around the same time, Mr. Musk stated that a new model would be released to Bot users "within this week."

Meanwhile, at the beginning of November, there were quite a few complaints that "weekly usage limits suddenly dropped." Mr. Musk himself posted that "the provision cost was too high," and the flow was that everyone's limits were reset on November 9.

Also, the pricing entry point has changed, and it seems that new subscribers must have at least the Premium (30 dollars/month) tier of "The X Subscription" to use it. There is also talk that those who already have Cursor Pro (20 dollars/month) can use it, but the information varies depending on the article. Honestly, I'm not sure.

In addition to that, new models from Claude and Meta's Muse came out around the same time, and there are many articles on note comparing them, asking "Which one should I actually use?" Even looking at popular articles, there were more comparison pieces like "Dots, Muse, or Grok Bot?" than stories about Grok Bot alone. It seems everyone is confused.

It seems convenient, but there was a point that made me think, "Oh, this might be scary" while reading about it.

It seems that all bots created by the same person share a single cloud PC and login information. In other words, even if you intend to separate them by saying "this one is only for accounting, this one is only for SNS," the underlying content is the same computer. Separating them doesn't provide security, it seems.

Also, the approval button only works for operations 'before' they are executed, so you can't cancel emails that have already been sent or items that have already been purchased. It might seem obvious, but with an AI that acts on its own, I think this is quite important.

Also, the PC runs in the US, and you can't make calls to Japanese phone numbers. If you use it in Japan, there seem to be several things to be careful about.

I've been trying to delegate tasks to AI myself, but the computer windows open and close on their own, and they clash with the tabs I'm looking at... That's subtly stressful.

So, the idea of having an AI-dedicated PC in the cloud where nothing happens on my own screen looks quite attractive just by itself.

However, 30 dollars a month is quite a lot with the current exchange rate, and the fact that the usage limit isn't public is a bit of a concern. If I were in the middle of using it and it said 'That's it for this week,' I'd probably be heartbroken.

I think I'll wait and see for a bit longer, and once the talk about usage limits settles down, I'd like to try it out at least once.

The feeling of AI changing from 'answering' to 'doing things for you'—I'm looking forward to what's next!
