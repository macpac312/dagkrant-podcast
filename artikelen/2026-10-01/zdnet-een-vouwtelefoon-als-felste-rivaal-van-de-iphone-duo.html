This foldable phone might be the iPhone Duo's fiercest rival - if not for one thing - ZDNET

Ever since I used the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8, I’ve come to prefer the wide-fold design over traditional book-style foldables. That’s why I was more excited about the foldable iPhone Duo than any other Apple product announcement at the fall event earlier this month. After seeing it first-hand, I was amazed by Apple’s thoughtful software and UI elements, which put it a league ahead of current-generation Android foldables. However, its 254-gram weight and the absence of a flagship camera system hold it back. That’s where the Xiaomi 18 Fold comes in.

I first held the new Xiaomi foldable at IFA 2026. It impressed me with its three cameras on the back, big battery, and a comfortable-to-hold design. Fast-forward to a few weeks later, and now that I’ve been using the 18 Fold for over a week, I can say it has its quirks, but its hardware is fantastic. Xiaomi’s 18 Fold isn’t available outside of China (without jumping through some hoops) but it’s still a strong competitor to the Duo. Here’s how they compare.

Design, display, and durability

Winner: Tie

Both the iPhone Duo and Xiaomi 18 Fold have a similar design. Unlike Samsung’s symmetrical sides on the Galaxy Z Fold 8, they have curved edges, giving them a distinct D-shape.

The iPhone Duo weighs 254-grams, while the Xiaomi foldable is 35-grams lighter. During my hands-on, I was impressed with Apple’s weight distribution on the Duo — it doesn’t feel hefty when unfolded. However, it is on the heavier side when closed, especially if you’re using it with a single hand.

By comparison, Samsung’s 201-gram Galaxy Z Fold 8 is lighter than both of these foldables and doesn’t feel uncomfortable whether opened or closed. I’ve had a similar experience with the Xiaomi 18 Fold. It isn’t the lightest foldable out there, but the 219-gram weight hasn’t bothered me. It is also thinner than the Duo, measuring 10.7mm when folded (vs. 11.3mm) and 5mm when unfolded (vs. 5.2mm on the Duo).

That said, Apple wins in durability. It is only the third brand after Google and Honor to offer a truly dust-resistant design on its foldable phone. The Duo is IP68-rated while the Xiaomi 18 Fold has an IP58 and IP59 rating for dust and water resistance. The “6” in IP68 signifies that it is resistant to small dust particles, which the “5” misses. This true dust-resistance is important for a foldable phone because the gaps around the hinges can allow pocket lint to penetrate to the internals.

Both phones have similar-sized displays. The iPhone Duo has a 5.4-inch outer panel that unfolds to a 7.6-inch inner screen. In comparison, the Xiaomi foldable has a 5.38-inch cover display and a 7.58-inch folding panel. Both phones are meant for content consumption, thanks to the wide design that doubles as an A4 sheet-like size in portrait orientation.

To compare, I liked Apple’s nanotexture coating and an under-display camera on the inner panel. During my brief hands-on, its matte finish looked great for both video consumption and reading, and the UDC camera allowed for an uninterrupted experience. I used to love this feature on Samsung foldables till the company dropped it with the Fold 7 last year.

As for the crease, it is minimal on both, but Apple’s is harder to spot. Nonetheless, they disappear in regular use and haven’t been an issue. In short: Xiaomi’s phone is thiner and lighter, but the Duo has dust resistance and a better inner screen.

Hardware and cameras

Winner: Xiaomi 18 Fold

I’m amazed by everything Xiaomi has managed to pack inside its thinner design. The Xiaomi 18 Fold has a bigger 6,000mAh battery and, more importantly, a triple rear camera system.

It packs a 200MP main camera, 50MP tele lens with 3.5x optical zoom, and a 50MP ultrawide-angle lens. It is refreshing to see the telephoto camera making its way to a wide-fold device, especially when Samsung, too, limited the optical zoom capabilities to its book-style foldable, the Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra. Xiaomi pulls ahead in camera versatility, and I’ve enjoyed playing around with various Leica color profiles.

In comparison, the iPhone Duo has two 48MP cameras (main and ultrawide), and misses out on a telephoto sensor. While I’ve yet to use them, it is disappointing to know that the Apple camera system doesn’t support features like ProRAW photo capture and ProRES video recording. I’d have loved for it to have the same main camera as the new iPhone 18 Pro series, but I’ll wait for the launch to compare the optics on these two phones. After all, megapixel count doesn’t tell the full story.

While Apple promises an all-day battery life on the iPhone Duo, the Xiaomi 18 Fold delivers it through a bigger 6,000mAh cell, which also supports 67W fast charging. However, it lacks built-in magnets and doesn’t support Qi 2.2 wireless charging. In comparison, the Duo supports MagSafe technology for 25W wireless charging and support for magnetic accessories.

Both phones are powered by in-house chips. The iPhone Duo has the latest A20 Pro processor, based on 2nm platform and improved power efficiency, whereas the Xiaomi 18 Fold uses the Xring 03 chip to deliver its smooth performance. I look forward to stress-testing the two chips head-to-head once I get the iPhone Duo in my hands.

Software and UI

Winner: Apple iPhone Duo

Apple’s biggest strength comes in its software. It has sprinkled several thoughtful UI touches throughout iOS 27 for the Duo (and I’m not just talking about the unfolding animation). I like how the content transitions — no matter if it is a game, movie or an article in a browser — from the cover screen to the main screen almost immediately. On Android folding phones, including the Xiaomi 18 Fold, there’s that split-second gap where the inner display turns on from a black screen.

Similarly, if you’re watching a movie with the iPhone Duo folded in half, it’ll shift the video to the top and give you controls on the bottom half. After a few seconds, the lower half mimics the frames of the video to give you a mirror effect on the matte screen. These aren’t huge differences, but Apple’s implementation contributes to a pleasant user experience.

The Xiaomi 18 Fold, on the other hand, can run more apps simultaneously, with a more versatile multitasking experience. While it doesn’t come pre-installed with Google services or the Play Store, it took me just a few minutes to install both and set up all the apps I need.

Final thoughts

Apple’s foldable wins in terms of software, polished UI elements, and its pleasing, uninterrupted inner screen experience. However, Xiaomi has managed to pack much more hardware into a slimmer design. The Xiaomi 18 Fold also has more versatile cameras, a big battery and a lighter build. It is a strong iPhone Duo rival and deserves a wider rollout.

But if you’re in the U.S., availability is probably the deciding factor. As of now, the Xiaomi 18 Fold is only available in China, whereas the iPhone Duo is set to hit shelves on October 23. You can still import the phone directly or purchase from seller marketplaces like eBay, but note that it won’t be optimized for 5G/4G band support on U.S. carriers.
