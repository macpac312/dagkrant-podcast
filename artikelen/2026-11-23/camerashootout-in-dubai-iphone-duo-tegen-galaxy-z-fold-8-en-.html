iPhone Duo vs Galaxy Z Fold 8 vs Xiaomi 18 Fold camera shootout in Dubai

For the first time, Apple, Samsung, and Xiaomi are all fighting on the exact same turf: wider, passport-style book foldables built to feel natural both closed and open. In theory, that levels the ergonomic playing field. Once you open the camera apps, though, three wildly different hardware philosophies emerge.

Both the iPhone Duo and the standard Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8 rely on dual-camera systems – a primary wide sensor paired with an ultrawide – leaving every zoom step past 1x to sensor crops and computational upscaling. Meanwhile, the Xiaomi 18 Fold squeezes a full Leica-engineered triple-camera array into its sleek frame, anchored by a 200MP main camera and a dedicated 50MP 3.3x optical periscope telephoto.

Before the iPhone Duo launched, much of the criticism focused on Apple dropping the telephoto lens compared to its flagship Pro range. Samsung was rightly lambasted for the same compromise just a few months earlier, but can a passport-size foldable actually offer it all?

To judge this and see how those hardware choices hold up in the real world, I took all three foldables around Dubai Marina from harsh midday sun through sunset, blue hour, and neon-lit nights.

Here is how they actually compare, starting with the specs on paper.

50MP, 80mm equiv f/2.8, OIS 3.5x optical zoom

1. Daylight & HDR (1x Main Camera)

It's hard for a modern smartphone camera to take bad photos in good lighting, and all three foldables capture crisp, share-worthy photos. Yet each phone immediately reveals a distinct personality, starting with framing.

Both the Galaxy Z Fold 8 and Xiaomi 18 Fold shoot at a wider 23mm focal length, while the iPhone Duo's 26mm main lens crops noticeably tighter into every scene.

The Ocean Empress yacht along the marina is a textbook showcase of how these three pipelines diverge.

Xiaomi's Leica Vibrant profile produces the most inviting, rich-looking photo straight out of the camera, giving the red-mahogany hull and gold latticework warm depth without blowing out the silver skyscrapers behind it.

Samsung favors cooler tones and aggressive contrast, leaving the lower-left hull signage significantly darker, while Apple prioritizes balanced exposure across the entire frame.

Push dynamic range to the limit—like shooting from underneath a massive concrete bridge out into harsh midday sun—and the iPhone Duo's computational HDR takes the lead.

Apple renders the shaded underside of the concrete arch smoothly and keeps the lower boat deck legible, trading away a bit of blue saturation in the bright sky. Samsung keeps the sky bluest, but crushes the bridge overhang into a flat, greenish-grey shadow. Xiaomi sits comfortably in the middle, holding better sky color than Apple while avoiding Samsung's crushed shadows.

2. Ultrawide

Switch to the ultrawide lenses, and we see further subtle differences, largely due to hardware choices. While Samsung (13mm, 0.5x) and Apple (14mm, 0.6x) deliver true sweeping ultrawide perspectives, the Xiaomi 18 Fold's 17mm (0.7x) ultrawide is noticeably narrower – and slower at f/2.4, which impacts the amount of light it can capture.

Standing in front of Pier 7, that 4mm focal-length gap between Samsung and Xiaomi is dramatic: the Galaxy Z Fold 8 captures the entire skyscraper on the far left edge, whereas the Xiaomi 18 Fold slices it clean out of the frame.

Between the two wider shooters, Samsung gives you slightly more real estate and bolder blue skies, while the iPhone Duo handles harsh architectural reflections on the sunlit right-hand tower and deep shadows under the skybridge with noticeably better dynamic range

3. Daytime zoom

If you care about zoom on a foldable, or you're actively considering these three devices, this is the entire ballgame.

Neither the iPhone Duo nor the standard Galaxy Z Fold 8 includes an optical telephoto lens, leaving both reliant on main-sensor digital crops. Do you actually need a dedicated telephoto lens, though?

The Xiaomi 18 Fold packs a native 80mm (3.3x) periscope telephoto – and it embarrasses the competition from 3x all the way out to 96x. Look no further: this is the passport-style foldable made for an actual pro camera experience.

At 2x (46mm–52mm), all three phones lean on high-resolution main-sensor crops and look sharp enough for social media. Push past 3x, however, and the gap opens immediately.

In front of La Brasseria, the iPhone Duo actually outshoots the Galaxy Z Fold 8 at ~4x: even though Apple crops tighter to 4.5x (107mm) compared to Samsung's 3.8x (92mm), the iPhone's 48MP sensor crop resolves cleaner lettering on the red menu banners and crisper horizontal slats behind the sign, whereas Samsung's denoising smears the striped awning into watercolor mush.

Then you switch to the Xiaomi 18 Fold's 3.3x (80mm) periscope – and neither digital crop comes close. Real glass resolves individual threads in the awning fabric, crisp white diamond motifs, and clean metallic edges on the sign, staying remarkably sharp even pushed to 6.7x (160mm) and 9.6x (230mm).

Pushing to maximum range across the Marina skyline reveals an interesting reversal between Apple and Samsung at ~10x.

While Apple won at ~4x, its 11x (263mm) ceiling turns soft and muddy on the distant Grosvenor House spire, while Samsung's 9.6x (230mm) AI reconstruction sharpens straight architectural lines, keeping the GH monogram and pyramidal latticework distinct.

Still, Xiaomi's 9.6x (230mm) periscope shot easily beats both with natural, optical-grade clarity – and then keeps going to 29x (690mm), 58x (1380mm), and an outrageous 96x (2300mm), pulling usable detail out of a rooftop emblem that is barely a speck at 1x.

4. Night & Low-Light Zoom

Once the sun drops over Dubai Marina, metering strategy becomes just as important as sensor size – and Samsung's auto-exposure makes some puzzling choices.

At night, Xiaomi and Apple trade blows on the primary lenses, depending on your taste. Dropping to 1/20s lets the Xiaomi 18 Fold hold sensitivity down at ISO 400, capturing a wide 23mm frame with rich contrast, deep water reflections, and well-tamed storefront screens.
