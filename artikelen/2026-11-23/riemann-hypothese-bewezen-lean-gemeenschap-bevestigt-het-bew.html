Lean and Mathlib community officially confirms Levent Alpöge's proof of the Riemann hypothesis is correct

The Lean and Mathlib community has confirmed that Levent Alpöge's proof of the Riemann hypothesis is correct. "We have full confidence that Alpöge's proof is correct, and we congratulate him on this achievement," the community said in a statement.

Alpöge, a Stanford mathematician, published the proof on Nov. 20, 2026, as 17 million lines of code written in Lean, a tool that checks mathematical proofs for errors. Dozens of people ran the code independently and got the same result. The verification programs are designed so they provably cannot accept a flawed proof. MIT mathematician Andrew Sutherland lists the independent checks on his website.

The Riemann hypothesis had been one of the most famous unsolved problems in math, making a prediction about how prime numbers are distributed. The video below, generated with Fable 5.2, explains the proof if you want to dig deeper.Ad
