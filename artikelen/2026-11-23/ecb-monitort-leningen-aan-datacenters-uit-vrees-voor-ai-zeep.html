ECB monitors loans to data centres over fears of an AI bubble

AI-related investment has already contributed to global growth, according to Buch. The question for the supervisor is how banks assess the risk of the projects they finance if market conditions change. In the case of data centres, the ECB wants to verify that institutions apply prudent criteria when granting loans and manage default risk properly.

Almost all supervised banks use AI

Artificial intelligence is also part of day-to-day banking. According to Buch, virtually all banks supervised by the ECB use AI tools and around a third are studying the tokenization of deposits, that is, the representation of bank deposits through digital units that can be recorded and transferred on distributed ledger platforms.

Buch noted that advanced AI models are accelerating cyberattacks and making them more sophisticated. In July, the ECB asked the chief executives of major banks to draw up action plans against these threats. The submission deadline ended in late October and the supervisor is already reviewing the plans in order to share with the sector the practices it identifies.

Dependence on a small number of technology providers is another of the weaknesses identified. Around 40% of incidents reported under the European digital operational resilience regulation, known as DORA, originated with external providers. Almost a quarter of the ECB's qualitative measures on operational and technology risks relate to failures in business continuity plans.

Consumer credit shows signs of deterioration

The supervisor's annual assessment paints a profitable, liquid sector with capital above the levels applicable in 2027. The top-quality capital ratio (CET1) stood at around 16% in the second quarter of 2026. CET1 requirements and guidance remain practically stable for the banking sector as a whole, at 11.3% of risk-weighted assets for 2027, compared with 11.2% in 2026.

Non-performing loans remain low, although the ECB already detects a deterioration in the quality of consumer credit and of loans to companies that are energy-intensive or affected by supply chain disruptions. Commercial real estate-linked mortgage loans remain exposed to refinancing risk. According to the banks themselves, the tightening of lending criteria is mainly due to their assessment of these risks and to economic uncertainty.

This year's geopolitical stress test also left a warning. In this regard, the ECB asks banks to imagine a scenario capable of reducing their capital ratio by three percentage points. Some institutions underestimated how a solvency problem can worsen a lack of liquidity. Others counted on raising capital in the middle of a crisis, an exit that could prove difficult if several institutions try to turn to the market at the same time.
