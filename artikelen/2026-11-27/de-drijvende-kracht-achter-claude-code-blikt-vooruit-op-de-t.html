The Core Figure Behind Claude Code Reflects on the Future of Vibe Coding

Anthropic has been going through a very rough patch recently.

On one hand, it fell below its issue price less than a week after listing under the aura of the largest IPO in history; on the other hand, its most capable Claude Code team has seen successive core members switch to rival OpenAI.

Yesterday, Thariq Shihipar, core engineer of Claude Code, officially announced his joining OpenAI. And just now, some overseas netizens directly questioned him publicly, saying that anyone who truly believes in safe AI would not change jobs, and essentially he did it all for a huge pay package.

In response, Thariq gave a polite public relations response, stating that he believes in upward competition, that a single dominant player benefits no one, and he does not consider Anthropic to be the only lab building safe AI.

The situation was extremely delicate for a period of time.

Familiar faces from Claude Code are reuniting at OpenAI

If you lay out the timeline of the past week, you will understand how complicated the mood of Anthropic's shareholders is at this moment.

First look at the capital market. On the evening of November 19 Beijing time, Anthropic was grandly listed on NASDAQ with an issue price of 83 US dollars. On the first trading day, its share price surged to a maximum of 115.1 US dollars during intraday trading, and closed up 17%, with market bullish sentiment peaking for a time.

However, Morgan Stanley, the underwriter responsible for stabilizing the share price, fully exercised the greenshoe option very early, depriving Anthropic of a potential share price support measure. Coupled with weakening market sentiment, the share price continued to come under pressure afterwards.

By the close of US stock market in the early hours of November 25, Anthropic's share price fell to 81.91 US dollars, with the lowest intraday level hitting 75.61 US dollars, officially breaking the issue price after only four trading days of listing.

In addition to the pressured share price, what makes Anthropic even more uncomfortable is the loss of core workforce.

The person in charge of this round of recruitment is Thibault Sottiaux, head of core products at OpenAI, who is known as Tibo in the industry. Throughout November, Tibo picked off every member of the four-person core team of Claude Code one by one, just as if he was checking names off a contact list.

The first to make the official announcement was product lead Cat Wu.

This female senior executive, who graduated from the Department of Computer Science at Princeton University, used to be an investor at Index Ventures, and also led product teams at YouTube and Scale AI. It was her who turned Claude Code from an internal prototype into a hit commercial product. She left Anthropic at the end of October, announced her joining OpenAI on November 5, and is directly in charge of ChatGPT, Codex and the developer API.

Austin Lau also announced his joining on the same day. He was the first growth marketing employee at Anthropic, who was specifically responsible for promoting Claude Code to global developers, and announced his joining the Codex growth team at OpenAI simultaneously on November 5.

On November 18, leaker Arfur Grok released news that Anthony Morris, founder of the Claude Code desktop version and core client developer, has also joined OpenAI. Although he has not made an official public announcement yet, he left his job as early as October 24.

The last and most notable recruit is Thariq Shihipar, who announced his joining on November 26.

He resigned on the same day as Anthony Morris on October 24, and was responsible for Agent interaction and tool design at Anthropic. Tools including the AskUserQuestion tool, Claude Agent SDK and Skills dynamic workflow were all developed by him.

In just three weeks, four key positions including product lead, core Agent interaction developer, core desktop developer and first growth marketing employee have been poached one after another, OpenAI even saved the team running-in period, and directly took over a ready-made Claude Code team.

AI is not only an industrial revolution, but also a renaissance

Putting aside the public debate in the public opinion field, OpenAI spent a huge sum of money to poach Thariq and his team as a package, and what it values most is their judgment on the next generation of programming tools.

Around the time Thariq officially announced his joining OpenAI, a nearly 54-minute interview he recorded earlier during the AI Engineer Code Summit was also officially released.

Combined with this interview, we may get a glimpse of how young people should embrace AI in the future and the possible development direction of the next generation of programming agents from Thariq's reflections.

Figure | Screenshot of Thariq's interview, 🔗 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qe9d9dWyED0&t=1s

The first direction is to let humans re-participate in software development.

Over the past year, Thariq was a firm advocate of the ideas of "Bash is all you need" and "React is all you need", believing that all operations should be put into the command line, the front-end should only be written in React, and even MCP was redundant.

Now he publicly overturned his old judgment. Today, AI can already directly operate Figma to draw front-end interfaces through Computer Use, the development method that used to require humans to take the initiative to adapt to AI is turning into AI actively adapting to human workflows.

Following this line of thinking, he began to re-examine the currently popular Vibe Coding.

Recently, Thariq used AI in his spare time to develop a game themed around element manipulation, only to find that the model would make a lot of design decisions on its own, and when he tried to play it himself, he found that the characters had a lot of behaviors that he had never known about before.

The black-box sense of loss of control made him very uncomfortable. To solve this problem, he specifically wrote a set of scripting languages, created a directory called /human, requiring all codes inside to be fully commented, single-character variable names to be disabled, and the length of each line to be limited.
