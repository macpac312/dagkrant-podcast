Epochal: Ex-xAI team builds an AI time machine using the internet's past

What if you could drop an AI into 2016, hide everything since, and ask it to call the next big thing? Three former xAI engineers think this odd experiment is the key to better AI forecasting.

The trio has launched Epochal, an AI startup developing tools that let models experience the internet as it existed at a particular moment in history. Its first product, Time Portal, creates a time-gated online sandbox where AI agents can search and browse archived webpages without accessing information published later.

Unveiled on 24 November 2026, Epochal was founded by Guodong Zhang, Manuel Kroiss, and Zihang Dai, who were part of Elon Musk's xAI founding team. The team also includesFrank Luan and Alex Bocharoff, plus engineers from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind.

The idea is that if AI models are expected to predict what happens next, they should first learn to make predictions without knowing how the story ends.

Taking AI back in time

Most large AI models learn from vast amounts of internet data, including information published after the events they are expected to analyse. This creates a problem when testing their forecasting abilities. A model might appear to predict an outcome when it is actually relying on information it has already seen.

Time Portal aims to remove that advantage. Epochal says its platform uses a timestamped internet archive and a point-in-time search index covering more than one trillion webpages across roughly 30 years of web history, with granularity down to the minute.

An AI agent can be placed in a historical moment, such as an election, market movement, public health event or geopolitical development, and asked to predict what happens next using only information available at that time.

Built in 90 days, the product is designed as a testing and training environment for AI forecasting, not a consumer crystal ball that promises to reveal tomorrow's headlines.

Can AI learn to predict the future?

Epochal believes better forecasting could improve decisions across finance, supply chains, public policy, research and enterprise planning.

For Indian businesses, such tools could eventually help assess demand, anticipate risks and improve operational decisions, provided their predictions prove reliable.

The startup has also attracted attention over its reported backing. Reports say Zhang is personally investing $100 million, although Epochal has not publicly detailed its funding.

However, historical simulations are only the starting point. Real-world events involve incomplete information, unpredictable human behaviour and sudden shocks that even sophisticated models may struggle to anticipate.

Epochal's real test will be whether its AI systems can do more than explain what happened in hindsight. They will need to demonstrate that they can make accurate, useful predictions before the future becomes history.
