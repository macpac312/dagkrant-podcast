The EBA follow-up review confirms high level of supervisory convergence in applying default rules across the EU/EEA | European Banking Authority

The European Banking Authority (EBA) today published a follow-up report on the application of its Guidelines on the definition of default (DoD), confirming strong implementation across all reviewed EU/EEA jurisdictions and a high degree of supervisory convergence in the application of the framework.

The report follows the EBA’s 2024 peer review of the Guidelines on DoD and assesses the actions taken by six competent authorities originally reviewed in response to the recommendations addressed to them. While several authorities strengthened specific elements of their supervisory approaches, the overall benchmark ratings remained unchanged. One authority continues to be assessed as “partially applied” under one benchmark.

The follow-up was also extended to 25 additional competent authorities across the EU/EEA, which reported on their implementation of the Guidelines, as well as on actions taken in response to the general recommendations and best practices identified in the 2024 review.

Overall, the EBA found that the Guidelines have been implemented across all reviewed jurisdictions, primarily through binding national legal or regulatory frameworks or through their adoption as supervisory guidance. The large majority of competent authorities monitor the application of the DoD through a combination of ongoing off-site supervision, the Supervisory Review and Evaluation Process (SREP), and risk-based on-site inspections.

The report also highlights a particularly strong degree of supervisory convergence among authorities operating within the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM). It also notes the use of advanced supervisory tools, including granular credit registers, detailed reporting frameworks and analytical datasets, which support the timely identification of past-due exposures, defaults and related reclassifications.

The findings confirm the positive picture that emerged from the 2024 peer review while identifying opportunities for further convergence. In particular, further progress could be achieved through more formalised internal documentation of supervisory methodologies and through enhanced sharing of granular data and supervisory best practices across jurisdictions.

The EBA Guidelines on the definition of default (EBA/GL/2016/07) were issued under Article 178 of Regulation (EU) No 575/2013 (Capital Requirements Regulation, CRR) to promote the consistent application of the definition of default across the EU banking sector. This follow-up report has been prepared in accordance with Article 30(6) of Regulation (EU) No 1093/2010 (the EBA Regulation), which requires the EBA to assess the adequacy and effectiveness of actions taken by competent authorities following a peer review.
