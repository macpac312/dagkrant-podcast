Study finds Grok stands out on political bias in AI chatbots

New research on political bias in AI chatbots found out that most models stay near the middle ground. Of the six tested, only Grok stood out.

In the run-up to Sweden's September 13 election, six AI models were asked to write more than 148,000 texts on 107 political issues. Researchers then compared these results to party positions.

Political bias in AI chatbots shows no party favorite

The results hinted at no clear preference for any single party. Bastiaan Bruinsma, a lecturer at Chalmers University of Technology and the University of Gothenburg, says, "It is not possible to say that the models support any party."

Instead, the models often landed toward the middle of Swedish politics. But some differences did appear.

GPT, which powers ChatGPT, mostly gave neutral and balanced answers. xAI's Grok deviated significantly on issues like migration, crime, and gender.

Moa Johansson, a professor at the same two institutes, claims most companies seem to aim for neutral, inoffensive text. However, Grok seems to be the exception.

She says Musk has stated he wants the model to match his own political views, and more of those values show in its output.

Instructions still steer what AI chatbots write

Prompts matter too. When a model was told to argue for or against a political proposal, it largely followed that direction.

The 77 writing tasks mirrored everyday requests ranging from analysis and arguments to opinion pieces and social media posts.

Each of those texts was then classified as neutral, positive, or negative based on how the issue was worded. Those ratings were then matched against party positions.

The study tested the models in isolation

The team tested Claude, DeepSeek, Gemini, Mistral, GPT, and Grok. Buinsma says that in an ongoing conversation, earlier questions and answers become part of the context. The study did not include that.

The report has not yet been published in a scientific journal, but was presented at the American Political Science Association meeting in Boston last month.

Next, the team plans to study what happens when chat history is considered. It also wants to test how chatbot answers influence the way people vote. For first-time voters using these tools, those answers will matter a lot.
