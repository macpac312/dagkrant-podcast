Milwaukee Brewers close out Tampa Bay Rays to end 57-year wait for first World Series title

Brewers win first championship with 4-1 win in Game 5

Misiorowski hurls five and two-thirds scoreless innings

Contreras named World Series MVP after batting .381

The Milwaukee Brewers captured their first World Series crown on Wednesday night, defeating the Tampa Bay Rays 4-1 to complete a four-games-to-one victory and end more than half a century of waiting for baseball’s biggest prize.

Rookie shortstop Cooper Pratt drove in two runs and Jacob Misiorowski pitched five and two-thirds scoreless innings as Milwaukee put the finishing touches on a charmed season that began in spring training with championship ambitions and ended with the first title in the franchise’s 58th year.

Trevor Megill was mobbed by his teammates on the mound after Richie Palacios grounded to second baseman Brice Turang, who threw to Jake Bauers for the final out.

In the US, all World Series games will air on Fox and Fox Deportes, with streaming available through Fox One. In the UK, the series will be carried by TNT Sports. In Australia, fans can watch on ESPN, with streaming available through Disney+ and Kayo Sports.

“It’s the best feeling ever. All of our fans are here. I know the city’s waited a long, long time for this and we’re so happy to bring it home to them,” said Christian Yelich, the 2018 National League MVP and the Brewers’ longest-tenured hitter. “Can’t wait to get back there and celebrate.”

William Contreras was named Most Valuable Player of the series after batting .381 with four RBIs across the five games, including a run-scoring double in the clincher. The 28-year-old became the first catcher to win the award since fellow Venezuelan Salvador Perez for Kansas City in 2015.

Contreras also helped contain Tampa Bay’s vaunted running game, throwing out four would-be base stealers, including two in the opener, while using the automated ball-strike challenge system to overturn 10 of 13 calls behind the plate. The honor capped a year in which he also won the World Baseball Classic with Venezuela.

“I don’t want to offend any of the other catchers in the league,” Contreras said. “But sitting up here today, champion of the world, I feel like I’m the best catcher in the league.”

The Brewers, who arrived in Milwaukee in 1970 after one season as the Seattle Pilots, had reached the World Series only once before, bowing to the St Louis Cardinals in seven games in 1982 as American League champions. They switched to the National League in 1998, but that narrow defeat remained the nearest they had come until this year, when a team blending young talent with October experience finally went one step further.

Milwaukee had been three outs from completing a sweep on Tuesday night in Game 4 before surrendering a 12th-inning lead. Given another opportunity on Wednesday, the Brewers built a four-run advantage behind Misiorowski, the brilliant right-hander who scattered two hits and one walk while striking out two before departing with a right calf cramp.

Tampa Bay’s best early opportunity came in the fourth, when Liam Hicks was hit by a pitch and Chandler Simpson singled, with Sal Frelick’s fielding error leaving runners at second and third with two outs. Misiorowski induced a groundout from Ben Williamson, whose pinch-hit single had won Game 4, to leave both runners stranded. He then put down the Rays in order in the fifth.

“I don’t even have words,” said Misiorowski, the overwhelming favorite to win the NL Cy Young award who broke the World Series velocity record with a 103.5mph fastball on the first pitch of Game 1 last Friday. “I know they’re going crazy in Milwaukee.” Asked about the Brewers’ fans, thousands of whom had transformed Tropicana Field into a home away from home, he added: “They’ve ​been nothing but supportive this year and in years prior and it has been amazing.”

Pratt, who made his major league debut in June and collected four hits in Tuesday’s defeat, again supplied an important part of Milwaukee’s offense. Turang doubled in the second inning and, after Frelick was hit by a pitch, scored on Pratt’s single to center. Pratt was caught trying to reach second, but the Brewers had the lead.

“This is one of the greatest moments of my life. Oh my gosh. It doesn’t even feel real,” Pratt said afterward. “This is surreal. It only took four months!”

Tampa Bay starter Drew Rasmussen, pitching on three days’ rest, kept the deficit at one through four innings before manager Kevin Cash turned to his bullpen.

Milwaukee immediately doubled their lead. Pratt singled off Kevin Kelly, Garrett Mitchell followed with another single and David Hamilton’s ground-ball single to right brought Pratt home for a 2-0 advantage.

The Brewers widened the gap in the sixth. Yelich was hit by a Garrett Cleavinger pitch and scored on Contreras’s double to left. Turang singled, then stole second after Tyler Wells entered. Following a Frelick strikeout, Pratt lifted a sacrifice fly into foul territory in right, scoring Contreras to make it 4-0.

The Rays threatened in the ninth. Hicks doubled with one out and, after Jonny DeLuca struck out, Simpson lined an RBI double to right off Megill, cutting Milwaukee’s lead to 4-1 and bringing the potential tying run into the on-deck circle. But Megill stranded Simpson at second to end Tampa Bay’s campaign.

The championship capped a season in which Milwaukee posted a major league-best 103-59 record before winning the NL pennant for the first time with a sweep of the deep-pocketed Dodgers in the NLCS. The Brewers switched over from the American League in 1998 as part of the realignment accompanying the arrival of the expansion Rays and Arizona Diamondbacks.

For Pat Murphy, it was the crowning achievement of his third season managing the Brewers. The 67-year-old, previously Craig Counsell’s bench coach, had already won consecutive NL manager of the year awards before helming Milwaukee to the promised land.

“This group of guys right here, the 2026 team, will be remembered forever – and they deserve that because they were a team,” Murphy said. “When you talk about this team, you’ve got to talk about people who care about each other. They’re not worried about their own stats. No one cares about that. They care about coming together and winning tonight.”

The Brewers established their total command of the matchup with three consecutive victories: a narrow win in the opener, a comeback from four runs down in Game 2 and a 10-1 rout in Game 3. Tuesday’s setback denied them the chance to become the first team since the 1976 Cincinnati Reds to sweep both the league championship series and World Series, but merely postponed the inevitable.

Tampa Bay, who won an American League-best 98 games, were left with a third World Series defeat following losses in 2008 and 2020.

The Brewers’ triumph marked the city of Milwaukee’s first World Series championship since 1957, when Lew Burdette’s three complete-game victories powered the Braves past the New York Yankees in a seven-game classic, delivering the city’s first professional sports title. The Braves relocated to Atlanta nine years later.
