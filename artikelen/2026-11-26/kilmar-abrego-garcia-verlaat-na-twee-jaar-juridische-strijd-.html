Today's top stories

The Army will detonate a huge explosion in Middletown, Iowa, this morning to safely dispose of thousands of pounds of lead azide that could spontaneously explode. The explosive has been used since WWI and was moved to the Iowa bunker 17 years ago. The detonation is happening after officials found that the lead azide had crystallized and become unstable. Authorities have evacuated a 5-mile radius, and those within 20 miles are advised to stay indoors to avoid exposure to toxins.

Kilmar Abrego Garcia has voluntarily left the U.S. after a nearly two-year legal battle.He is the Salvadoran immigrant who became emblematic of the challenges posed by President Trump's mass deportation campaign. Abrego Garcia was arrested in 2025 and mistakenly deported to El Salvador. He returned to the U.S. but departed for Spain yesterday. His lawyer, Simon Sandoval-Moshenberg, said that Abrego Garcia boarded a commercial flight to a destination of his choice and "left the United States a free man." Sandoval-Moshenberg described the move as "a temporary departure," saying that Abrego Garcia, whose wife and child are U.S. citizens, has options to return to the U.S.

Nvidia's newly reinstated CEO, Jensen Huang, apologized to his staff yesterday for his previous behavior, according to the New York Times. He reportedly denied that Trump pressured the board for his return, despite the president saying otherwise. Huang had stepped down following reports of affairs, including one with a woman reportedly suspected by U.S. intelligence agencies of being recruited by China's spy agency. Some members of Congress still have concerns about the national security scandal.
