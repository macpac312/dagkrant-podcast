Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei says letting AI govern society would be "a bad idea"

Dario Amodei said it was "absurd" to suggest he believed people should "surrender human free will" to AI systems.

In a lengthy post on X, he responded to an Axios report that cited people familiar with internal discussions. The outlet reported that some Anthropic employees believed humanity would safely hand authority to superintelligent systems, and often should, rather than fight for control.

AI must be governed by "legitimate democratic processes," Amodei wrote. Political decisions should stay in human hands, he argued, and he rejected handing power to AI as firmly as he rejected autocracy.Ad

[...] We should never sacrifice legitimacy or self-determination on the altar of efficiency or security. This argument against autocracy applies word for word to governance by AI, and I feel just as strongly that handing over governance to AI is a bad idea.Ad Dario Amodei

Automating specific tasks such as driving, developing cancer drugs, and writing software could benefit humanity, Amodei said, but that was a different matter from giving up free will in politics.

Amodei says that full automation isn't always worth the risk

End-to-end automation could also bring risks, Amodei wrote, including serious job disruption and AI building new models without human oversight. "It is clearly desirable in many cases and will save a lot of lives," he wrote. "But it has downsides as well, and in some areas it may not be worth it." In many cases, he added, "humans would simply prefer to do some activities themselves, so they can stay deeply engaged in what matters to them, even if AI can do those activities better."Ad

Amodei said AI companies didn't differ much on automating specific tasks. Some AI CEOs, he wrote, repeated slogans like "human control and agency" and "AI should be just a tool" but still shipped products built for more and more automation.

Anthropic, by contrast, refused to allow its existing models to be used for autonomous weapons during its dispute with the Pentagon. He was "willing to risk Anthropic's future as a company" over that refusal, "even in the face of legal sanctions," Amodei wrote.Ad
