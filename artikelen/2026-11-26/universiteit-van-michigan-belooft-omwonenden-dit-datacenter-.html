University of Michigan Mailer Promises Its Data Center Isn’t Really a Data Center

Over the past month, the University of Michigan sent postcards and pamphlets to people in Michigan telling them its new data center is “NOT a commercial data center” and is, instead, a “research computing center.”

According to one of the postcards viewed by 404 Media, the University’s data center will “advance breakthrough research in medicine, clean energy, cybersecurity, and engineering,” and "accelerate discoveries like new drug therapies and cancer treatments that improve people’s lives.” The University's postcard also promised to create 300 “high-quality union jobs during development” and “preserve approximately 80% of the 124-acre property as green space.”

One of U of M’s mailers said the building will not operate as a commercial data center. “This facility will not support streaming, entertainment, social media, or e-commerce,” it said. It also said the facility will not “burden the local community with the cost of its energy or water use, drain resources from the local community or raise utility rates,” or “create smoke, odors, or significant noise.”

The University has said it’s not sure how much water the data center will use but that it could be as much as 650,000 gallons a day. It also said it’s “evaluating several cooling techniques to identify the most efficient approach,” a process that “includes continued evaluation of reclaimed wastewater for cooling, which would significantly reduce demand on municipal potable water supply systems.”

Los Alamos National Labs (LANL), America’s nuclear weapons scientists, have partnered with the U of M to construct a massive $1.2 billion 290,000 square-foot data center in Ypsilanti, Township, Michigan. Residents of the Township are angry about the “research computing center” and worry about how much water it’ll use, what it’ll do to electricity bills, and its connection to nuclear weapons.

In April, the Township’s utilities authority approved a moratorium on delivering water to data center projects while it studied how they might impact the community’s water supply. In response, U of M sent a letter to the Township utility board threatening legal action.

Concerned Township residents have spent the past year strategizing with community leaders about how to delay or stop construction. The Township’s anger caught U of M off guard and it attempted to address community concerns in several public meetings. It hasn’t gone well.

The pamphlet attempted to contextualize the data center’s place in America’s nuclear weapons research. “It would not involve nuclear weapons manufacturing, physical weapons testing, or the storage of hazardous nuclear materials,” the pamphlet said. But it would help “improve cybersecurity practices to safely manage existing nuclear material out of state.” In January, LANL confirmed its portion of the data center would be used for nuclear weapons research.

U of M did not return 404 Media’s request for comment.

On November 19, the board of regents approved the next phase of development for the data center in a vote of 7-1. The same night, Ypsilanti Township hosted a panel discussion about the data center where its leaders promised to continue to fight. “We have a number of strategies right now that we’re going to be pursuing,” Township attorney Douglas Winters said at the panel. “We believe that U of M has gone far afield when it comes to the constitutional autonomy which they have enjoyed by virtue of being a university.”
