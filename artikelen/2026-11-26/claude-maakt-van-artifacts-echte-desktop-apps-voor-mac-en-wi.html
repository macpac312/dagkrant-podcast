Claude can now turn your artifacts into real desktop apps

Anthropic is letting Claude users turn their artifacts into desktop apps. A tool you’ve built inside Claude can now sit on your Mac or Windows computer with its own icon and its own window, instead of being buried in a chat. The “install as app” feature is in beta and available on every Claude plan, free and paid. There’s no Linux version.

Artifacts first appeared in 2024 as interactive pieces of content Claude could generate alongside a conversation. Since then, many people have used them as small reusable tools for work: trackers, calculators, dashboards and the like. Until now, using one meant opening Claude or a browser tab. As standalone apps, they can run beside Claude while you work on something else in it, or without opening Claude at all.

Installing one is easy. After updating the Claude desktop app, open the artifact, go to the Share menu and choose Install as app. The app lands on your computer, and you decide which permissions it gets. Installed apps can still call on Claude and any connectors you’ve set up, and they update automatically when you edit the original artifact.

There are some limits. The apps need an internet connection and a signed-in Claude account, so they don’t work offline. Anything an app asks Claude to do counts against your usage limits, which are tightest on the free plan. Legacy artifacts, live artifacts from Claude Cowork, and artifacts built from the Docs, Slides and Sheets templates can’t be installed. These are also not full apps of the kind you’d build with Claude Code.

Permissions are the part to think about. By default an app gets roughly what a browser tab would. You can also give it extra permissions, such as sending notifications or full computer access. Full access lets an app run programs and read, change or delete files without asking each time, which is a lot of power for something that might have taken a few prompts to make. Anthropic has some guardrails. You can only give full access to apps you made yourself, or, on Team and Enterprise plans, apps made by coworkers. Every edit to an app also asks you to confirm its permissions again. Even so, Anthropic says it does not review these apps before installation, so judging whether an app is safe is up to you. On Enterprise plans, the feature stays off until an admin turns it on.

The bigger picture is that Claude is becoming more like a work platform than a chatbot, where the things you make start to behave like ordinary software. The open question is how companies will handle a wave of apps built by employees, which IT never vetted and which may have full access to work machines.
