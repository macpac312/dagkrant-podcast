The EBA examines the use of stablecoins, including EMTs, in decentralised finance | European Banking Authority

The European Banking Authority (EBA) today published a Report analysing the use of EMTs and other selected stablecoins in DeFi and the implications for consumers and supervisors. The EBA finds that stablecoins, including some EMTs issued under the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA), play a central role in the DeFi ecosystem by providing liquidity, collateral and settlement in DeFi trading and lending activities. The Report also highlights consumer protection, and regulatory arbitrage risks arising from the growing interaction between stablecoins and DeFi.

Although EU consumer participation in DeFi remains limited, access to these services is becoming increasingly straightforward. This is because of the growing role of CASPs in facilitating access to DeFi and offering products linked to DeFi activities, as well as increasing institutional interest in DeFi with 42% of European-based investors expecting to engage with DeFi by 2028. Developments in artificial intelligence, including AI agents, are also lowering barriers to entry for consumers.

The EBA highlights several risks arising from DeFi activities. Consumers may lose all their invested assets due to inadequate risk disclosures, highly leveraged positions, non-repayment of loaned stablecoins and operational vulnerabilities. The use of EMTs in DeFi may also create channels through which risks can be transmitted to issuers and to CASPs acting as gateways to DeFi ecosystems.

Additionally, the Report identifies potential risks of regulatory arbitrage. These include the use of DeFi structures to circumvent MiCA requirements relating to the offer to the public or admission to trading of EMTs in the European Union, as well as the prohibition on granting interest on EMTs.

Against this backdrop, the EBA reiterates the recommendation made in its response to the European Commission’s targeted consultation on the review of MiCA that the Commission assess whether additional regulatory requirements should apply to CASPs facilitating access to DeFi, in particular lending protocols.

The EBA will continue monitoring developments in the use of stablecoins in DeFi and will provide further evidence and technical input to the European Commission as appropriate.
