Fred Vasseur backs FIA ruling after Charles Leclerc's Las Vegas clash with Lewis Hamilton

Formula 1 team bosses usually rush to soften the blow when stewards penalise one of their drivers. After Charles Leclerc clipped Lewis Hamilton into a spin at Turn 7 during the 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix, Ferrari team principal Fred Vasseur took the opposite approach - endorsing the FIA’s ruling and leaving no doubt over where responsibility lay.&nbsp; Speaking to F1 TV after a bruised night in Nevada, Vasseur was asked directly whether Leclerc alone had defied team instructions or if both drivers shared fault in their heated battle for sixth place. "No, I think... the decision of the FIA was quite clear," Vasseur answered.&nbsp;

Lewis Hamilton, Ferrari, Charles Leclerc, Ferrari Photo by: Patrick T. Fallon / AFP via Getty Images

The stewards’ official verdict left equally little room for interpretation. Reviewing video and onboard footage under Appendix L, Chapter IV, Article 2 d) of the International Sporting Code, the stewards noted in Document 60 that Car 16 launched its inside lunge at Turn 7 from a considerable distance behind Car 44. Applying the FIA Driving Standards Guidelines, officials determined Leclerc’s front axle was not at least alongside Hamilton’s mirror at the apex - concluding Leclerc "had not earned the right to the corner at Turn 7 and was wholly to blame for the resulting collision". The resulting 10-second time penalty dropped Leclerc to 10th at the flag, while Hamilton recovered to seventh - leaving Ferrari with a meagre point return from a race where both cars had surged into the lead pack from 10th and 11th on the grid. Vasseur: “Once again we destroyed the race” What aggravated Vasseur most was not just the lost points, but the timing. Just three weeks after clear-the-air talks following their Lap 1 tangle in Mexico City - and moments after Ferrari’s pitwall had radioed a warning to Leclerc five corners prior - the same breakdown repeated itself.&nbsp; "I know that he apologised, I know that he was sorry, but once again we destroyed the race today, that we could have done much better," Vasseur said. "I don't know what could be the outcome of the race because it was quite chaotic, but we can't lose points like this. And more than points, it's the motivation of everybody, the reward of the job done, the reward of the effort, and this - it's a huge frustration for me."&nbsp;

Vasseur wasted zero time making his anger felt, breaking onto team radio during the cooldown lap to summon Leclerc "before anything else". Speaking to F1 TV afterwards, he confirmed the initial meeting had already taken place while hinting further talks would follow once emotions cooled. "I already had the discussion with Charles tonight, but now it's better for me to digest my frustration," he added.&nbsp; Later addressing written media in Las Vegas, Vasseur clarified just how blunt that first encounter had been: "It was not a discussion. I spoke." Rejecting calls to impose strict team orders in a slipstream-heavy race, he argued the issue came down to basic professionalism: "It's not a matter of rules. What can you do differently, except that you have to respect your team-mates?"
