How to choose your grassroots prime minister?

One thing to watch out for ishowthe Student List will pick its top figures, including the next prime minister and their presidential candidate.

One of the key features of the movement so far is that everything has been covered byveryextensive and time-consuming consultations with student plenum meetings across the country, including the vetting of all candidates on the electoral list.

That may, of course, not always be possible in the future given the urgency some decisions will demand. The students will quickly need to figure out how to streamline these decision-making processes and make them work at the pace governing demands.

It may be easier to run a similarly complex process to choose their presidential candidate, as there is still some time to go until the vote in late December, but less so when it comes to the top jobs in the government, starting from the prime minister, and his key ministers.

“There is almost certainly going to be a complex process. It will not be decided by a small group of people sitting somewhere in a room. It’s only a matter of how complicated it will be,” Nikola Burazer, programme director at the Belgrade-based thinktank Centre for Contemporary Politics explained.

“It will have to be sped up because the process of choosing candidates for the list actually took a long time, months, to finish … [and finished] literally one day before they submitted the list. So, it has to be shorter.”

And the constitutional deadlines (16:31) will “force them to choose their candidates more quickly,” Aleksandar Ivković, a researcher at the Centre for Contemporary Politics added.

Former central bank governor Dejan Šoškić, who in 2012 resigned two years into the job in protest against alleged interference with his independence,is widely seen as the frontrunner for the job of the prime minister, but let’s see what other names can come up and who can emerged unscathed through the vetting process.

“After people are done celebrating and thinking about what happened yesterday, we will focus a bit more on what’s coming up, and how the government will look. 24 hours ago that all seemed like a fantasy to us,” Burazer said.
