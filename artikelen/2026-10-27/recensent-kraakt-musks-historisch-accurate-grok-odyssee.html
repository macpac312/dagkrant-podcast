Elon Musk’s ‘historically accurate’ Grok Odyssey deserves to be put to the sword

Elon Musk spent much of the summer throwing a strop over Christopher Nolan's diverse casting choices in his adaptation of The Odyssey and pledging to make his own version. True to his word, the tech mogul has now remade the tale just as the ancient Greeks would have wanted: as a seven-minute AI horror show so creepy and uncanny you can feel your eyeballs twitch as it unfolds in all its sloppy gruesomeness.

Nolan's Odyssey struck gold at the box office but divided purists while the presence of Lupita Nyong'o as Helen of Troy had Musk foaming at the figurative chops. One of the most pointed critiques was from classics scholar Emily Wilson, who complained about its lack of sex scenes and horrible-looking food. Well, you have to compliment Musk for going one better with the "first chapter" of his Odyssey, which was made using his AI tool Grok and has just debuted on X.

It is so sexless that it makes Nolan's effort look like the orgy scene in Caligula. Nor can Wilson single out the horrible food. Here everything is listless, lifeless and as fake as a £10 football jersey bought off the internet.

In its defence, the Grok-dyssey is one of the few films released this year not to feature Tom Holland and Zendaya, both of whom had star billing in the Nolan movie. But instead of flesh-and-blood actors, this "creative collaboration" between AI specialists Wonder Studios and director Dev Walker features lookalike virtual hunks who all resemble baddies from the God of War video games. Except God of War would never lumber its characters with the verbal porridge that here passes for dialogue ("Any man who raises a sword against one of us… will answer to all of us!").

It does mimic Nolan's film-making style in one respect. It is initially impossible to work out what's going on. The "action" opens with two warriors standing in a field, glaring at the infant that they have plonked in the mud. This is tiny Tom Holland, aka the baby version of Telemachus, the puny prince played by the Spider-Man actor in the Nolan Odyssey.

He is the son of Odysseus, who has been ploughing his fields with salt in order to fake madness and wriggle out of waging war on Troy. But these lifeless beefcakes have tested his resolve by inviting him to run his plough over his firstborn. Bluff called, he bids farewell to his wife Penelope and heads out to sea. Odysseus seems confused as his cowardice catches up with him, and well he might, given that Penelope looks completely different in each scene – a quirk that may or may not be corrected when future episodes are unleashed. (Musk has promised to release in full a "historically accurate" Odyssey by the end of 2026.)

You do have to credit who or whatever oversaw the battle sequences, which have some of the epic quality of Zack Snyder's Spartan romp 300. But the glassy-eyed characters, with their flat, expressionless voices, give the whole thing a zombie-like quality. That effect is not helped by the constant fast edits, which suggest Grok's circuits might fry at the thought of a shot lasting more than 1.5 seconds.

Add a deafening score that sounds like a ZX81 trying to do Hans Zimmer (Nolan's former collaborator) and you have a take on the tale of Troy that deserves to be put to the sword, burned to the ground and hidden forever beneath the sands of time.
