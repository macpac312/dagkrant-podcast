SAP plans to cut at least 10,000 jobs as it pivots to AI

SAP is preparing to eliminate at least 10,000 positions as part of a sweeping AI restructuring, Bloomberg reports, citing people familiar with the matter. That's at least nine percent of the company's global workforce of more than 110,000. Every department has been asked to identify savings, with product development, engineering, and sales taking the biggest hit. The final number hasn't been set and could change after negotiations with employee representatives.

In Germany, where about a quarter of SAP's workforce is based, a job guarantee runs through 2028. CEO Christian Klein wants to embed AI tools across SAP's product lineup. The company already cut roughly 10,000 jobs in 2024. Its competitors are making similar moves. Oracle slashed 21,000 positions over twelve months, and Microsoft is offering buyouts to around 60,000 employees. SAP declined to comment on the potential cuts, saying only that it reviews its organizational structure on an ongoing basis. The company recently announced a partnership with Mistral AI and has acquired several startups.
