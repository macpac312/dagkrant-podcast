Huawei’s upcoming wide-screen Pura 100 could borrow its best display tech from the Mate 80 Pro Max

Huawei's upcoming Pura 100 wide phone will use a dual-layer OLED display, according to Weibo tipster SmartPikachu. The tipster says the device is in development. If the claim holds, it would be the first phone in the Pura line to ship with the stacked panel.

Dual-layer OLED, also known as tandem OLED, places two light-emitting layers on top of each other. The design lets a screen reach higher brightness while using less power and aging more slowly than a single-layer panel.

Huawei first used the technology on the MatePad Pro 12.2-inch tablet. The Mate 70 RS Ultimate Design then became the company's first phone with a dual-layer screen, and the panel later appeared on the Mate 80 Pro Max and Mate 80 RS Ultimate.

Earlier leaks had pointed to the Pura 100 Pro Max as the first Pura model to get the display. Tipster Digital Chat Station said in July that Huawei was testing a 6.9-inch dual-layer screen for the series, alongside a 6.6-inch LTPO OLED panel.

The same tipster said in October that the Pro and Pro Max would switch to a wide screen of about 6.39 inches, citing engineering samples. The standard model is said to keep a 6.6-inch display. That leak did not name Huawei, but it described the lineup as an “all-round wide-screen imaging flagship.”

Other Pura 100 leaks point to customized dual 200MP camera sensors and a square 1:1 selfie camera. SmartPikachu has separately claimed the Pro Max will get an improved triple RYYB camera system.

Huawei already sells two wide-format Pura phones. The Pura X Max foldable launched in March 2026, and the non-folding Pura X View followed in September with a 6.39-inch OLED screen in a 16:9.5 aspect ratio, rated at up to 6,500 nits. The Pura X View starts at CNY 5,999 and packs a 7,000mAh battery into a 6.68mm body.

Huawei has not announced the Pura 100 series or confirmed any of its specifications. Based on the company's usual release cycle, the lineup is expected in the first half of 2027.
