OpenAI asked investors to steer clear of xAI, Anthropic and three other named rivals

During its $6.6 billion raise, OpenAI told backers it expected them to skip funding five competing AI startups

Fri 27 Nov 2026 · 18:17 EST · 3 min read

OpenAI wanted investors’ money. It also wanted that money to stay away from its competitors.

During its $6.6 billion funding round in October 2024, OpenAI asked participating investors not to back five rival firms: Anthropic, Elon Musk’s xAI, Ilya Sutskever’s Safe Superintelligence (SSI), and AI companies Perplexity and Glean.

Frontier AI labs run on capital, so limiting a rival’s access to it, even informally, is a competitive tactic.

What OpenAI actually asked for

The October 2024 round valued OpenAI at between $150 billion and $157 billion. Microsoft, Nvidia, SoftBank and Thrive Capital were among the backers.

Along with the term sheet, OpenAI sent a clear message about loyalty: it asked backers to refrain from supporting the five named competitors.

The request was not a formal legal requirement. OpenAI framed it as an expectation tied to meaningful involvement with the company. Investors were not contractually barred from writing checks elsewhere. They were simply put on notice about what OpenAI considered good-faith partnership.

OpenAI had two goals. It wanted to limit rivals’ access to funding, and it wanted to keep sensitive information from flowing to competitors. Investors in a company like OpenAI can see strategy, product roadmaps and financial performance. An investor sitting at the table with OpenAI and Anthropic at the same time could, in theory, carry insights between the two. OpenAI’s request was meant to make that kind of overlap less likely.

Competition kept going anyway

Many venture firms kept stakes in both OpenAI and its competitors.

OpenAI itself did not slow down. In March/April 2026, the company closed another funding round worth $122 billion at a post-money valuation of $852 billion. That’s up from the $150 billion to $157 billion range set in October 2024.

Anthropic, one of the five names on OpenAI’s do-not-fund list, has also kept growing. The company has filed for a public offering that is expected in November 2026. If that listing proceeds, public market investors would get a direct way to back one of OpenAI’s most prominent competitors.

Why exclusivity is hard to enforce in AI

The way the request was structured explains much of its limited effect. A non-binding expectation carries social and relational weight, but it can’t be enforced in court.

An investor who ignores it may risk a cooler relationship with OpenAI. They don’t face a lawsuit.

There’s also the question of which rivals made the list. Anthropic and xAI are building frontier models. SSI, led by Sutskever, is focused on safe superintelligence. Perplexity and Glean are AI companies operating in other parts of the market. By naming all five, OpenAI showed it saw competitors beyond the companies building models.

What this means for investors and AI labs

The episode shows a tension between labs and investors that will likely persist. Leading AI companies want committed partners who won’t leak information or bankroll the competition. Investors want exposure to the whole sector, because nobody can say with certainty which lab will dominate.

For AI labs, the lesson is that market leadership buys influence but not control. OpenAI’s scale is what let it make the request at all. Few startups could ask Microsoft, Nvidia and SoftBank to avoid other deals and expect to be taken seriously. Even so, capital still reached competitors.

Anthropic’s expected November 2026 public offering is the next development to watch. A successful listing would give a direct OpenAI competitor access to money that private exclusivity requests can’t reach.

The size of recent rounds also raises the stakes. When a single company can close a $122 billion round, competitors face pressure to raise comparable amounts.
