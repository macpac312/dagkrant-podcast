Xiaomi 18 Fold Looks Like iPhone Duo—but Misses Apple’s Real Idea

Quick Answer iPhone Duo and Xiaomi 18 Fold may share almost the same physical idea, but they do not deliver the same software experience. Xiaomi learned the visible language: the D-shaped outer screen, the compact half-width form and even a glass-like transition effect. Apple went further. On iPhone Duo, the outer screen is treated as a logical continuation of the right half of the open display, while the Dock, status information and key controls remain spatially stable. That is why Apple’s transition feels coherent rather than merely animated.

iPhone Duo and Xiaomi 18 Fold Look Almost Identical Put the iPhone Duo and Xiaomi 18 Fold side by side and the resemblance is impossible to miss. Both use a book-style folding body, a compact outer display on one half and a larger display inside. From the front, each cover screen can read like a capital “D”: straight beside the hinge and rounded along the outer edge. That similarity naturally creates the question: Did Apple copy Xiaomi, or did Xiaomi copy Apple? But arguing from announcement dates misses the most important point. Modern flagship phones are developed years before they reach stores. Their display proportions, hinge architecture, batteries, antennas, software layouts and accessory dimensions are decided long before a public launch. The first company to announce a similar silhouette is not automatically the company that invented the complete experience. The more revealing question is this: which company understood what this shape is supposed to do?

Confirmed by Apple Two displays designed as one system Apple describes iPhone Duo as a dual-display system with a 7.6-inch inner display and a 5.4-inch outer display. It says the aspect ratio is consistent across both screens and that transitions between displays and orientations are seamless.

Confirmed by Xiaomi Nearly matching screen sizes Xiaomi lists a 7.58-inch inner display and a 5.38-inch outer display for Xiaomi 18 Fold, while promoting an “inner-and-outer consistent” flagship display experience.

ZEERA Design Analysis Apple starts with interaction The iPhone Duo cover screen is designed to correspond visually with the right-hand region of the unfolded interface. Closing the phone therefore feels like folding the same workspace, not switching to a second unrelated device.

The Core Difference Xiaomi reproduces the surface Xiaomi reaches a closely related physical form, but the visible animation is not the whole idea. Without the same spatial relationship between screens, the effect can look similar while feeling less natural in use.

ZEERA’s verdict: Xiaomi may have learned the appearance of Apple’s foldable concept, but Apple’s real advantage is the thinking underneath it. The iPhone Duo shape, screen ratio and iOS 27 layout work together as one interaction system. Shop FoldTitan Pro Why Does iPhone Duo Have a D-Shaped Outer Screen? The D-shaped iPhone Duo cover display makes more sense when you stop treating it as a decorative front screen. Open the phone and the inner display forms a rounded rectangle with four curved corners. Fold that canvas through the center. The right side becomes a half-width visual region with a straight boundary at the hinge and rounded corners at the exposed edge—the same basic geometry suggested by the outer display. The inner and outer panels are physically separate screens. Apple is not literally bending the same OLED pixels around the device. The continuity comes from mapping the interface so that the cover screen feels spatially related to the right side of the open canvas. That distinction matters. A conventional foldable can simply resize an app from a tablet-like layout into a narrow phone layout. Apple’s apparent ambition is more specific: preserve the user’s sense of where the content and controls are while the device changes shape.

Shape A folded half, not a miniature tablet The straight hinge edge and rounded outer edge visually connect the closed display with one half of the open display.

Reachability Designed for one hand Apple says that in the closed state, essential controls move to the side for easier reach. That is especially important on a short, compact 5.4-inch cover screen.

Continuity Stable visual anchors Keeping navigation, Dock and status information toward the right creates stable reference points. The phone can change shape without making the whole interface appear to jump somewhere else.

Result Closing feels less disruptive The content may reflow, but its scale, hierarchy and primary controls can remain familiar enough that the transition feels continuous.

The Real iPhone Duo Trick Is Not the Folding Animation Xiaomi and some third-party foldable concepts focus on the most visible part of Apple’s transition: a soft zoom, translucent layers or a glass-like blur as the device opens and closes. That can imitate the surface style. It does not necessarily reproduce the interaction. The essential idea is spatial continuity. If the outer screen represents the right-hand zone of the larger interface, important elements should not travel unpredictably across the canvas during the transition. The Dock, status information and common controls become anchors. Content can expand into the newly available left side while the user’s main point of reference remains stable. This is why Xiaomi can reproduce a beautiful blur effect and still miss the essence. If an app recenters every element, changes scale abruptly, shifts the Dock, loses scroll position or rebuilds the page after unfolding, the animation is only decoration covering a discontinuity. In other words, the glass effect is the polish. The layout relationship is the product. Apple designed that relationship first; Xiaomi appears to have learned the part that is easiest to see. Shop FoldStand Pro Copying the Shape Is Easier Than Copying the Logic It is relatively easy to recognize the visible ingredients of the iPhone Duo design: two rigid halves, a central hinge, a short D-shaped cover screen and a larger rounded display inside. A manufacturer can reproduce those proportions. A software team can also add a zoom, blur or translucent layer to make the opening animation look more polished. But those are the parts users can see in a five-second demo. They are not the deepest part of Apple’s solution. The official dimensions make the resemblance even stronger. Apple uses 7.6-inch and 5.4-inch displays, while Xiaomi uses a nearly identical 7.58-inch and 5.38-inch pairing. Xiaomi clearly understands the physical appeal of the half-canvas form. Where Apple separates itself is the relationship between that hardware and the interface. iOS 27 does not merely shrink the large screen into the outer screen. It treats the right side as the stable part of the composition and uses the newly available left side when the phone opens. This means the physical fold and the software layout tell the same story. On Xiaomi 18 Fold, the hardware can look remarkably close, yet a transition that mainly relies on visual effects still feels like one screen being replaced by another. It learns Apple’s visual vocabulary without fully reproducing its grammar.

What Foldable App Developers Often Miss An iPhone Duo app does not become well adapted merely because it stretches to fill 7.6 inches or adds a fashionable opening animation.

01 Preserve the user’s exact state Keep the same document, video position, selected item, draft text and scroll location when the display changes.

02 Give controls a stable home Primary navigation should remain predictable instead of jumping from one side to another as the layout expands.

03 Add space without replacing context The unfolded view can reveal a sidebar, detail pane or second app while keeping the original focal content recognizable.

04 Test more than the ideal demo Games, camera apps, video players and fixed-aspect-ratio tools may not map one-to-one. They still need deliberate behavior rather than a forced stretch.

This is where Apple’s iPhone Duo design philosophy becomes more interesting than the physical resemblance to Xiaomi. The goal is not to make every screen identical. It is to make every transition understandable.

Why the D-Shaped Screen Changes iPhone Duo Case Design The unusual cover screen also creates new accessory requirements. A built-in screen protector must also preserve touch response and fit the D-shaped opening precisely. MagSafe alignment matters just as much.
