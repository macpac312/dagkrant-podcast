iPhone Duo Vs Galaxy Z Fold8 Vs Xiaomi 18 Fold: Which Foldable Phone Is Actually Worth Your Money?

Foldable phones – Comparing Apple, Samsung & Xiaomi

We’ve seen foldable bicycles, foldable umbrellas, and even foldable tables make life a bit more convenient, and now, the trend has made its way to tech. Foldable phones are shaping up to be the next big thing, literally, offering a bigger screen without the bulk.

If you’re tempted to get one but can’t decide which to pick, we’ve put 3 new passport-style foldables head-to-head to see how they compare in price, features, and more, so you can figure out which one’s worth the splurge.

The current exchange rate isS$1 = ~RMB5.20as of 16th October 2026.

Which phones will we be comparing?

Introducing the contenders: Apple’s iPhone Duo, the tech giant’s 1st attempt at a foldable; Samsung’s Galaxy Z Fold8, from the brand that popularised modern foldable smartphones; and the Xiaomi 18 Fold, the underdog brand looking to give the big players a run for their money.

How do the phones compare on price & availability in Singapore?

The 1st thing most of us look at when buying a phone is its price tag. Foldable phones don’t come cheap, so it’s worth comparing how much each device costs and how easy it is to get your hands on one in Singapore.

The Xiaomi 18 Fold was officially unveiled in Chinaon 7th September 2026, but there are currently no plans for a global release, which means Singapore buyers can’t purchase one locally directly from Xiaomi for now. If you’re still keen to get one, you can try your luck on online marketplaces like Lazada, but be mindful of seller reliability, or pick one up directly from a Xiaomi store if you happen to be travelling to China.

In China, the Xiaomi 18 Fold starts atRMB10,999 (~S$2,108.44), with it going up to RMB14,999 (~S$2,875.21) for the highest-end model. The pricing is competitive for a premium foldable, but without official local availability, you’re on your own when it comes to warranty coverage and repairs, which is a headache you simply wouldn’t have with the other 2 on this list.

Apple’s iPhone Duo is the newest of the 3 phones, with pre-orders starting at 8pm on 16th October 2026. The phone itself will hit shelves in Singapore on 23rd October 2026, where it’ll be available for online delivery and in-person pick-up.

As Apple’s 1st foldable, the iPhone Duo is also one of its priciest devices to date. It starts at S$3,099 for the base model and goes all the way up to S$4,899 for the highest configuration. That’s S$1,000 more than the iPhone 18 Pro Max’s top price.

To soften the blow, you can make payment ininstalments over 24 months at 0% interest, rather than forking out the full amount upfront. Alternatively, you can also trade in an eligible iPhone for credits towards the new device, helping to shave a little off the hefty price.

Samsung’s Galaxy Z Fold8 is the newest addition to the Galaxy Z series, alongside the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra and the clamshell-style Galaxy Z Flip8, which has been in stores since 14th August 2026. This makes it the most readily available option of the 3 if you want a foldable in your hands right now.

The Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 starts from S$2,498, with it going up to S$3,398 for higher-end models, placing it in between the iPhone Duo and Xiaomi 18 Fold in terms of affordability.

Similar to the iPhone Duo, Samsung lets you make payments through instalment plans ranging from3 to 36 months, with most participating banks offering 0% interest as well. You can even save up to S$1,170 on the new foldable if you decide to trade in your current Samsung device.

TL;DR:If you’re already eyeing a foldable, the Galaxy Z Fold8 is your best bet, with local availability and flexible payment plans. The iPhone Duo is also easy to get in Singapore, but comes with a higher price tag. The Xiaomi 18 Fold’s lower price might look attractive, but getting your hands on one might take extra effort.

What are the differences in designs & durability?

Another big factor when it comes to buying a phone is how it looks and feels in your hands. While these foldables may be compact enough to fit in your pockets, their screen displays, designs, and durability may differ.

While the Galaxy Z Fold8 claims to be the world’s lightest foldable, we have it here at 3rd due to its weaker durability and less versatile hinge. It’s rated IP48, so while being water-resistant, the foldable phone offers less protection from dust compared to the other 2.Image credit: Samsung

But don’t count the Galaxy Z Fold8 out just yet; coming in 3rd doesn’t mean it’s poorly built. Its display is strengthened by a shock-absorbent layer, along with layers of Flex Titanium to reduce creasing. Its Armour FlexHinge is specially designed to support the phone as it folds and unfolds, while its reinforced aluminium frame protects it from knocks and drops.

The iPhone Duo stands out for its design and display, with an ultra-fine matte finish that helps to minimise creasing when the phone is unfolded. The inner display also features anano-texture surface to reduce glare under harsh lights, so it’s easy on the eyes even when you’re outdoors.

It may be the heaviest of the 3, but the iPhone Duo makes up for it with its durable build. Its titanium frame and hinge cover, along with theCeramic Shield on the front and back, help protect it from drops. There’s even fingerprint-resistant and scratch-resistant coatings on the inner display that’ll help keep the screen in good condition.

The Xiaomi 18 Fold strikes a good balance between durability and portability. It has a physical design that’s almost identical to the iPhone Duo, down to the D-shaped display, with a lighter and slimmer build that makes it more comfortable to carry around.

Its inner display is protected with a dual layer of ultra-thin glass and a stretchable organic film to help absorb impact. The frame is made of high-strength aluminium, and its hinge wraps around the folding screens for added protection. The Xiaomi 18 Fold also comes with a pre-applied screen protector, which is simply 1 less thing to worry about.

TL;DR:If portability is your priority, the Xiaomi 18 Fold offers a good balance between a slim build and durability. The iPhone Duo has a sturdier build, though it’s on the heavier side. The Galaxy Z Fold8 is the lightest and thinnest, but falls short in dust protection and hinge flexibility.

How are their performance, battery life & features ?

A foldable phone needs to do more than just look good. It should run smoothly, hold its charge for daily use, and actually make use of its larger screen. From multitasking tools to AI-powered features, here’s how the iPhone Duo, Galaxy Z Fold8, and Xiaomi 18 Fold stack up.

Since the iPhone Duo hasn’t launched yet, we can’t speak to real-world performance or battery life just yet. On paper, though, it looks promising. The foldable is powered by theA20 Pro chip, the same processor as the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max, which Apple promises to have 20% better processing and 40% better graphics performance than the previous generation.

Running on iOS 27, it gets a refreshed interface designed for the foldable display, an updated Siri, and new photo-editing capabilities. It supports the USB-C Apple Pencil for jotting down notes, and integrates the Apple ecosystem, making it the natural choice for those who are already Apple users. But until it’s officially out, it’s hard to say how it’ll hold up against the other 2.

If you’re constantly on multiple apps at once while on your phone, the Xiaomi 18 Fold is practically made for you. You can open up to 6 apps in a single window, or 8 if you count floating windows, and it’s easy to navigate with just the tap of your finger. Its 6,000mAh silicon-carbon battery is also the largest of the 3, being able to last you a full day.

But as mentioned, because the phone isn’t readily available in Singapore, you’ll have to put in extra effort to install English and Google support, given its China-based operating system. App optimisation is also the weakest of the 3, with many apps failing to make full use of the larger screen.

The Galaxy Z Fold8 handles everyday tasks and demanding games smoothly, as you’d expect from a flagship, but according to reviewers, it can get warm after extended use. Beyond its performance, it also comes with new Galaxy AI features that make everyday tasks more convenient.
