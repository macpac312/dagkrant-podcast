Ditch Keurig: Use This Eco-Friendly Alternative That's Also Cheaper - House Digest

Keurig coffee makers and other pod-based machines have become a go-to for casual coffee drinkers and self-professed caffeine addicts alike, and for good reason. These machines are accessible, incredibly easy to use, and quick, so even if you're essentially a zombie first thing in the morning, you can produce a delicious cup of joe with the click of a button. After a few years of living with one of these models, however, the cons might start to become more apparent. Pod-based systems aren't exactly eco-friendly, and having to use an entire pod for each cup of coffee means costs can quickly add up. Looking to make a change? Consider trying out a bean-to-cup machine.

Bean-to-cup coffee machines, at their most basic level, grind beans and dispense drip coffee without requiring extra effort from the user. More advanced machines essentially combine every tool you'd find on a home coffee bar into one device, doing everything from steaming the milk to dispensing ingredients in specific ratios. In most cases, they're just as convenient to use and simple to understand as a Keurig, but the big difference is that no pods are necessary. You're free to buy your coffee beans from local businesses, pick up a massive bulk bag, or even try your hand at roasting them yourself, which seriously cuts down on both single-use plastics and spending over time. While you might have to make a larger up-front purchase, this swap will save both the environment and your wallet in the long run. Get ready to repurpose your old coffee pod holder!

How bean-to-cup coffee machines can save you money

Let's break down the costs associated with both of these styles of machines. The most basic, single-cup Keurig machines start at around $90, though sales and promotions typically bring the price down to approximately $50. Larger Keurigs for home use typically hover in the $170 to $220 range before any sales or promotions. In the grand scheme of coffee makers, this isn't all that expensive, but when you factor in the fact that K-Cups usually run between 40 cents and $1.10 per pod, things start to become clearer. If a single user were to use average-priced K-Cups (75 cents per pod) and enjoy one cup of black coffee every morning, they'd be spending nearly $275 solely on K-Cups yearly. That's just one cup per day — if two people in the household both drink a daily morning coffee, that's nearly $550.

Bean-to-cup machines, on the other hand, require a bigger initial purchase. Prices vary widely for these Keurig coffee maker alternatives, but basic drip coffee makers with grinders are usually around $100 full-price. Bean-to-cup espresso machines are on the higher end, between $500 and $1,600. The long-term savings are where you start to see real differences. Whole-bean coffee from a standard grocery store brand is around 75 cents an ounce. You need around 0.38 ounces of coffee per cup, so at a cup a day, that's just over $100 for the entire year. It might take some time to break even on the initial investment if you go for a higher-end machine, but a savings of around $175 on coffee per person per year ain't too shabby.
