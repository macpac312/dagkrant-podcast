Lando Norris: F1 needs better rain tyres as wet Las Vegas would be &quot;scary&quot;

World champion Lando Norris hopes Formula 1 tyre supplier Pirelli will field much-improved rain tyres in 2027 as he expects the current range won't work properly at a low-downforce circuit like Las Vegas. Following partially rain-affected weekends in Malaysia and Singapore, F1's 2026 cars went through their first proper qualifying and grand prix held in wet conditions at the Brazilian Grand Prix, with both Pirelli's full wet and intermediate compounds being put to the test at Sao Paulo. Qualifying had to be halted for over an hour due to standing water in several corners, with the risk of aquaplaning prompting drivers to run full-wet tyres in Q1 before switching to intermediates. The race both started and finished on intermediates, with a dry middle spell, dispelling early fears over how the 2026 generation of cars would run in the wet. But while Brazil put on an entertaining spectacle and Pirelli's rain tyres largely worked as expected in the right operating window, drivers still felt this year's stiffer intermediates need work on how they perform in low temperatures as they struggled to keep their cars on the track at the start of a stint and on laps to the grid. With Interlagos being one of the easiest circuits on the calendar to get tyres into the working range, several drivers voiced concerns that Pirelli's offering won't be robust enough on circuits where that isn't the case, like the upcoming Las Vegas street circuit. The FIA's official weather forecast is not available yet, but other predictions suggest the chance of rain is over 50% for Free Practice 1 on Thursday evening, with conditions more likely to be dry after that.

With temperatures dipping well below 10C over the Vegas weekend and the circuit lacking any high-speed corners to put lateral load into the tyres, McLaren driver Norris said a wet practice would be quite "scary" because the intermediates don't work when they're too cold. "When the tyres get cold, it's pretty nasty," Norris said when asked by Motorsport.com for his verdict on F1's first proper wet-weather weekend of this rules cycle. "On laps to the grid, when they get very cold, they felt horrible. If they get into a good window, they're good. So, I think the scary times are if you go to Vegas and you can't get any tyre temperature. You need a much better tyre than what we have here because that's going to be horrible." Brazil runner-up Max Verstappen added: "I think it's mainly that the working range is a bit too small. When they're hot, it's okay, but when they're cold, they're really like a rock. That's something that we need to try and work on, to generate a bigger working range on the extreme wet [tyres] and the intermediates. I think that would help a lot with switching on the tyre better and then being able to run even in heavier rain." Williams driver Carlos Sainz also felt Pirelli's entire range needed improvements. "I think the level of the wet and the inter is not good enough and we need to work with Pirelli on finding a better tyre because in the mixed conditions the inter is still a very weak tyre at the rear; by the time it warms up the front is gone. "And the extreme honestly, [in qualifying] we had so much aquaplaning and a big lack of rear grip, so still missing a bit of development on these two tyres."

Pirelli faced a difficult task developing rain tyres for F1 2026's all-new regulations suite with their high-torque power units and active aerodynamics, putting significant forces through the rubber. It meanwhile chased a moving target of just how much downforce the teams would be able to generate, with fierce in-season development already ahead of the supplier's projections. Over the off-season the FIA implemented a partial Straight Mode in the wet to ease safety concerns, with only the front wing now being activated. Then, in April, the governing body also reduced electric output in wet conditions. Those two measures are believed to have made a big difference to the 2026 cars' ability to race in the wet relatively safely, but they obviously arrived well after Pirelli had to define its 2026 tyre range. F1's first proper running on both the extreme wet tyres and intermediates is coming so late in the season that Pirelli can't take Brazil's lessons into account for 2027, as the Italian tyre manufacturer has already finalised its homologations for next season. But following earlier development work at the start of the year, it was able to conduct crucial wet-weather testing in Magny-Cours last month, trialling the various solutions it worked on over the course of 2026. At the French circuit, Mercedes reserve driver Frederik Vesti conduced two days of testing, including one day in the wet to trial various new intermediate tyres featuring different compounds and constructions.

"We tested some solutions, especially for the intermediates, and we ended up with good options that we would like to introduce for next season," Pirelli's trackside engineering chief Simone Berra said in Mexico. "For sure the idea is to improve the wet-weather product for next year. "We worked on the compounds, we worked on the tread thickness, we also worked on different structures. So, we worked a lot on different tyre aspects and we would like to combine the best solutions. We are working on the data now and we are going to homologate new wet-weather compound specifications." Speaking after qualifying in Sao Paulo, Pirelli's head of motorsport Dario Marrafuschi was adamant the company has "made some steps" for 2027. "Of course, we took quite seriously the scepticism from some drivers, and we addressed the development during the year. We know there is always room for improvement and we believe we made some steps ahead for next year," he said. "It is a great value having this information [from Brazil]. Unfortunately, it is coming only towards the end of the season. We have already closed the wet development for next year. I don't want to disclose what will happen on the wet next year, but we made the wet development based on the information we had at the beginning of the season and some of the testing we did in Suzuka, so that was good for us to address the priorities for this year.
