Grok Bot users hit usage limits as Musk cites serving costs

Subscribers to Grok Bot found the week’s usage pool already drained over the Halloween weekend, and Elon Musk, chief executive of SpaceX, has blamed the squeeze on money. “The cost of serving Grok Bot was unsustainably high relative to revenue,” Musk replied on X on Sunday, November 1.

Several people said in X posts that their meters went from as little as 50% to the full 100%, even when they had not run the agent overnight. One of them had asked Musk for a reset, writing that many users had burned through their quota quickly that week, and his cost explanation arrived in response.

About 11 minutes before that answer, Musk had posted that Grok Bot would research presents for friends and family and then buy them. Neither post gave figures for the new limits, the expense of running the agent, or how long the tighter ceilings will last.

Grok @Bot will research gifts that your friends and family would love and buy them— Elon Musk (@elonmusk) November 1, 2026

Later that Sunday, a subscriber complained about being locked out of the bots until Wednesday after reaching the cap. Responding to that complaint, Musk asked if paying more to move up to a bigger plan felt too steep.

Grok Bot, an AI agent product, logs in to a person’s apps and carries a multistep job through to the end, and it is listed apart from the Grok chatbot on the company’s plans. Access starts at $30 per month with Premium, which carries four times the usage of the $8 Plus tier, according to the official pricing page.

Buyers who want more headroom can move to Super at $100 monthly, rated at 15 times the Plus level, or Ultra at $200, rated at 50 times. No absolute caps appear on that page for any tier.

SpaceX, which absorbed xAI in a February merger, closed its $60 billion acquisition of Cursor in August and now sells the coding tool inside those same subscription plans. Under Cursor’s help documentation, the agent has no meter of its own and pulls from one weekly pool it splits with Cursor itself plus Grok’s own apps.

On Sunday, the xAI status page logged a five-hour, 15-minute slowdown in its usage and billing dashboard that hit cursor.com and the enterprise dashboard, and that incident has since been resolved. Posters on Cursor’s user forum separately described Grok Bot meters flipping between roughly 77% and 100% without any real spike in activity. Any link between that dashboard fault and those meter readings remains unconfirmed.

Twelve days before the weekend complaints, during SpaceX’s October 20 call with investors on third-quarter results, Musk said he had never watched a product grow as quickly as Grok Bot and that each user’s weekly activity kept rising over time. He added that a small group of trusted enterprise clients already had the agent, wider business rollout was expected within weeks, and Grok Bot might end up consuming most of SpaceX’s Super Intelligence compute.

President and COO Gwynne Shotwell told analysts on the same call the agent, launched in August, counted 800,000 weekly paying users earlier in October, over twice the total of a month before. Business customers, in her description, were putting it to work as their “force multiplier across sales, marketing, IT, and engineering.”
