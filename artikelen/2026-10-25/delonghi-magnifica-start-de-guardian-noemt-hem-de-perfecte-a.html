The perfect everyday coffee machine: De’Longhi Magnifica Start review

This automatic bean-to-cup machine will be ideal for many: a single button press serves up espresso, black coffee and cappuccinos – and it’s affordable, too

You don’t need to spend big money to get a good coffee machine. Strictly speaking, you don’t need a coffee machine at all – a nice cafetière will do just fine. If you do crave a lower-effort solution that doesn’t cost a fortune, then a fully automatic bean-to-cup machine, such as the De’Longhi Magnifica Start ECAM220.60, is a good place to, um, start.

It makes espresso. It makes black coffee. It makes cappuccino. It produces hot water on demand. Fill the hopper with good coffee beans, top up the water and fill the little milk carafe (if you want milk, that is), and all you need to do is plonk a cup on the drip tray and prod the corresponding button. Whirr, hum, splutter. Your cup is now full of coffee. Nice coffee, too.

How I tested

As with every coffee machine I test, I put the Magnifica Start ECAM220.60 through its paces in my home for several weeks. I used the full range of its drink settings and assessed how easy it was to set up, use and maintain.

I’ve tested more than 30 coffee machines for the Filter, so not only do I know a good cup of Joe when I taste one, but I also have a sense of where this machine sits in an overwhelmingly crowded market.

What you need to know

The Magnifica Start is one of De’Longhi’s most affordable fully automatic models. It’s simple to use, well designed and if you time it right you can sometimes find it on sale from about £300.At this price you can expect to forgo features such as touchscreens or LCD panels, but many people will find the Start’s fuss-free controls more user-friendly.

Before you rush out and buy one, be aware that – as with many of De’Longhi’s machines – the Magnifica Start comes in an array of models – four on the brand’s website, and a few retailer-specific options. Cheaper ones swap the milk carafe for a simple wand, which you can use to steam and froth milk manually. They share the same grinder, brew group and general design, though, so will make an equally pleasant espresso or longer black coffee. They’ll still make a decent milk-based coffee, but you’ll need to hone your barista skills to manually steam milk using the steam wand. If you develop a knack for it, this can help with the lack of adjustability you’ll find with fully automatic models, but there’s a learning curve. If you like the sound of manual milk steaming, look for a model with a lower number at the end of its part code, such as ECAM220.22. These are usually cheaper.

If you prefer your bean-to-cup machines to be fully automated – and I think most people do – then make sure you’re buying a Magnifica Start with a 6 in its part code, such as the ECAM220.60 I have here. This will create frothy coffees poured into a cup at the touch of a button.

What we love

Despite all the fancy – and pricier – coffee machines at my disposal, the Magnifica Start has kept its place in my kitchen over the past few months. That’s because it makes a decent coffee at the touch of a button, and it’s simple to use. All the buttons are easily accessible on the front panel, meaning choosing drinks and adjusting the strength of each is straightforward. The buttons are touch-sensitive rather than old-school clicky ones, but I had no trouble with accidental presses or prods not registering.

The drinks menu is small, but it’ll suffice for most people. You can choose between espresso, long black coffee, cappuccino or hot water. I found the default amounts on the small side – 45ml espresso, 150ml cappuccino and 200ml of black coffee. Thankfully, it’s easy to adjust each drink if you want a longer coffee. For cappuccino, you can adjust the volume of coffee and milk individually to get the strength and consistency you want – and that means you make it more of a flat white, or a latte, depending on what settings you choose. Just hold the corresponding drinks button for a few seconds, then tap it once you’re happy with the amounts of coffee or milk in your cup.

The Start’s milk carafe is smaller than on pricier models, but it clips on and off easily and opens the door to one-touch cappuccinos. It does a cracking job of producing a reasonably fine, creamy foam, too. It’s not as silky as the best bean-to-cup machines, but it’s not a million miles away.

There’s no ability to adjust the amount of froth, however: if you want hot, unfrothed milk, then you’ll need to either make it yourself in the microwave or on the hob, or upgrade to one of De’Longhi’s machines with more adjustability, such as the rather excellent Rivelia, which starts at £599.

Black coffee and tea drinkers can press the quick-release button on the carafe’s side, slot the water spout into place, and the little LED light under the hot water button illuminates. This allows you to top up a black coffee or turn an espresso into an americano. Or even just make a cup of tea if you don’t want to boil the kettle, although puritans should be warned that the water is hot, not boiling.

The rest of the design is similarly sensible. Lift off the plastic lid on top of the machine, and you can fill the bean hopper with up to 250g of coffee beans. A dial in the middle gives you 13 steps of grind adjustment, which is plenty enough to suit most coffee beans. In its centre, a flap hides the ground coffee chute – if you run out of beans or you want to switch to decaf, you can add a dose of preground coffee with the supplied plastic scoop.

The 1.8-litre water tank slots in on the right-hand side and is made from clear plastic so you can see how much is remaining. A water filter comes in the box, and this lasts for about two months under normal usage. Replacements cost about £15 at full price from De’Longhi, but if you shop around you can get the price down to about £7.

The drip tray has a decent capacity, so it doesn’t need emptying too often. Slide it out, and you’ll discover that it’s attached to the grounds bin, which is large enough to contain at least a few days of coffee grounds, depending on your dose settings and caffeine consumption. The various parts separate for easy cleaning – and are dishwasher safe aside from the grounds bin – but normally a quick rinse and wipe down to remove coffee and milk residue and clean coffee grounds out of the bin is all that’s required.

Once the water tank is out, you can quickly pull out the coffee infuser, also known as the brewing head, where the ground coffee sits during brewing. This is very welcome. These kinds of machines can get mucky inside, so being able to pull out the infuser and give it a thorough rinse is definitely a good thing.

What we don’t love

One minor irk is that, at 75dB, the Start’s grinder is noisier than any coffee machine I’ve tested. That’s 12dB louder than the otherwise comparable Philips LatteGo 5500. This is mostly because the thin plastic lid doesn’t have a tight seal, so it doesn’t muffle the noise as much as other designs.

My main complaint, though, is that the milk carafe could – and should – be bigger. Its 220ml capacity is much smaller than on De’Longhi’s pricier models – such as the Rivelia, which is more than double the size. You’ll get a couple of small, default-sized cappuccinos out of it, but if you increase the preset volume of milk, you’ll need to refill the carafe after every drink.

Also, unlike De’Longhi’s pricier machines, there’s no dial to adjust the milk froth – you get a fixed amount, and that’s it. Yes, it produces a fine, creamy foam without too many big bubbles, but it’s a shame you can’t adjust it to your preference, especially if you’re a fan of a flat white, which tends to have less frothy milk than a cappuccino.

That said, my review model did occasionally produce barely frothed hot milk. It turned out, however, that this wasn’t a hidden setting. It was because I hadn’t cleaned one crucial part of the carafe. The button on the side of the carafe doesn’t just allow you to remove it from the machine, it also releases the light-coloured plastic connector for cleaning. Fail to rinse this regularly, and even small amounts of residue will block the air hole and prevent optimal frothing. Lesson learned.

Although not exclusive to the Magnifica Start ECAM220.60, a downside of any coffee machine with a removable milk carafe is that you need to be diligent when it comes to cleaning – forget, and the milk residue may mature overnight. Milk has a tendency to taint plastics when it goes off, so once it’s got into the rubber tubes and plastic parts, you’ll need to buy milk cleaning tablets to soak it off – or potentially replace them. Washing-up liquid does not suffice. My advice is to dismantle and clean it after every use, either by hand or in the dishwasher if you’re feeling lazy. And if there is milk left in it, then pop the carafe in the fridge overnight to prevent the milk from spoiling. Some users may find this fiddly – if you’re at risk of forgetting to clean the frother, you might want to consider a manual steam wand instead.

And if all this talk of spoiled milk hasn’t put you off your babyccino, then you’ll be disappointed to hear that there is no dedicated hot milk button with the Magnifica Start. To get the Start to froth milk without making a coffee, it’s necessary to use a trick: namely, to select the cappuccino option and set the strength setting to use ground coffee. Do this without adding any ground coffee to the chute, and the Start will froth hot milk into your cup. Once it detects that there’s no coffee, it abandons the brewing cycle. I’d prefer a button, but as workarounds go, it’s not hard.

The only other criticism is one I could level at most bean-to-cup machines that come through my door: the coffee preset – the long black – tends to taste bitter. This is because the machine uses the same dosage and grind size of coffee as it does for an espresso, but pulls more water through the grounds to brew a longer drink. This is drinkable at default settings, but noticeably more bitter than espresso. Adjust it to pour a longer coffee, however, and bitterness overwhelms.

This can be fixed. One option is to grind coarser, but then you’ll find that your espresso will become weak, watery and under-extracted. My advice with this – and most bean-to-cup machines – is to make an americano instead. Get the espresso tasting good by adjusting the grind size (normally a little finer than default) and reducing the volume slightly. Then simply top this up with hot water to make an americano. You can add milk if you like. Yes, it’s a bit more of a faff, but it tastes way better.

Warranty and repairability

You get a standard two-year warranty from De’Longhi, covering the usual faults, and the brand will attempt free repairs in that period. If not, you’ll get a replacement, which may be new or a refurbished model – at De’Longhi’s discretion.

Once you’re out of warranty, De’Longhi still offers servicing for a flat fee. With its £99 Essential Service, De’Longhi will pick up the machine from your home, identify any show-stopping or potential issues, descale the machine, change seals and advise of any repairs. If there are issues to be fixed, you can either upgrade to the £150 Comprehensive Service, or else pay that fee upfront if you’re specifically sending it to repair a problem. Bear in mind, though, that parts will cost extra – depending on the scope of the repairs required, it may cost significantly more.
