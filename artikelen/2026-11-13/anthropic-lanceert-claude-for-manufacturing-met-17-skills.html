Anthropic launches ‘Claude for Manufacturing’

Artificial intelligence firm Anthropic has launched a plugin for its popular AI assistant Claude called Claude for Manufacturing, the company announced Thursday.

The new software includes 17 skills, or instructions for specific tasks, according to the company. The skills can run processes that include quoting a new job, investigating a failed inspection, diagnosing a breakdown, sourcing from another supplier, comparing designs with Autodesk Fusion and reporting on production.

Claude for Manufacturing also supports four connectors to manufacturing systems, including Autodesk Fusion, Infor, Microsoft 365 and Oracle NetSuite. According to Anthropic, connectors let Claude access company apps and services, retrieve data and take actions within connected services. Manufacturers can also use the plugin with systems that have no connector.

The plugin can be used to accomplish a single task or multiple tasks in a sequence before reviewing the final output. New skills will be added periodically, according to the company.

Anthropic launched Claude for Manufacturing in large part to help companies work more efficiently, Anthropic Enterprise GTM Leader Lauren Collett told Manufacturing Dive.

"Manufacturing, as we all know, is complex, judgment-heavy work, and we've actually hit a point where model capabilities match this really well," she said. "And what I'm hearing from a lot of the manufacturers that we work with is that the people within their organizations are spending too much of the day on that internal toil... gathering information, chasing it down between systems, writing up the results and then redoing that again the next time."

She said Anthropic's new product allows estimators, quality engineers, maintenance technicians and other personnel to concentrate on more complex jobs that can leverage their expertise. This includes "things like getting more quotes out, making harder pricing decisions, stopping repeat defects [and] getting machines running sooner," Collett said.

Claude for Manufacturing also allows people to work faster on repetitive tasks.

"Each of the different skills that we built gives Claude written instructions for a specific task, like writing up a part that failed inspection or searching past reports for the same defect," she said. "That way, when a part fails inspection, the quality engineer is starting from there and from what the plant already learned instead of starting from scratch."

Claude for Manufacturing also helps support multistep work, such as turning a customer's request into a draft quote, by linking the skills it supports together.

Collett emphasized that humans remain in charge continually. "One of the core principles to us that was really important is control, and so making sure a person is signing off on every draft."

In other words, the people responsible for each step in a process review and approve the work before it reaches a customer or supplier. For example, an estimator releases a quote and a quality engineer signs a corrective action.

"Think of it as Claude really doing that first pass, and then coming to that expert within the business with that recommendation," she said. "Claude's work can always be checked, and Claude's not operating equipment. The experienced people running the process are really keeping the control and the oversight that we all know is so critical within manufacturing."

Software company Infor is providing its enterprise resource planning software to power the plugin. Anyone with a Claude enterprise plan can install the plugin, Collett said.

"We go beyond feature function into the processes themselves and operate at a micro-vertical level," Infor CEO Kevin Samuelson told Manufacturing Dive. "We're good at understanding processes and workflows, and our systems do a very good job of managing them. We've been partnered with Anthropic for a while, kind of running our AI, but [Claude for Manufacturing] is kind of a game changer and really allows customers to yield maximum value out of our systems."
