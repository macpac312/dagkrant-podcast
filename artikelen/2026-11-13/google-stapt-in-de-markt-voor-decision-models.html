Gemini Decisions API: Google introduced a new API backed by Gemini Decision 1. The model handles closed-set decisions, so it scores choices in a single pass instead of generating text token by token (Sundar Pichai, Logan Kilpatrick, Google).

Specs: It supports yes/no, multiple-choice and rubric scoring, with up to 255 multiple-choice options. It accepts text, image, audio, video and PDF inputs, handles up to 1,000 questions per request, has 256K context, returns answers in 150–250 ms end to end, and attaches a confidence score to every answer (@_philschmid).

Pricing: $0.10 per 1M input tokens, with $0 billed for output. Google says that is about 5x cheaper than Flash-Lite (Sundar).

Origin: The project started as an internal Google DeepMind prototype. A small team shipped the model and API about six weeks later (@_philschmid).

Google's comparison with TypeSafe's Jev: Google's own tests show Decision 1 beating Jev 1.14 on 5 of 7 shared benchmarks, including 92.2% vs 90.0% on JevBench. Jev costs $0.042 per 1M input tokens (@kimmonismus).

Artificial Analysis evaluation: Independent results are more mixed (Artificial Analysis).

Overall score: Decision 1 scores 66 on the Decision Index. That ties Elephant 9B and Jev 1.14, and sits 7 points behind Drex Max v1.1 and GLiDE v1.2.

Strengths and weaknesses: It leads AA-Omniscience-Decision (66) and HLE-MultiChoice (49). On AA-Paperclip, it answers every question correctly on only 33% of documents, versus 63% for Drex Max.

Cost and latency: It costs $345 per 1M tasks, versus $103 for Drex Max and $156 for Jev. Its Brier score is 0.18. Average time per task is 211 ms, slower than Jev at 92 ms (latency).

Early adopters: Stitch built a rapid-design experiment on the API that goes from a rough prompt to a multi-screen product flow in about 5 seconds (Stitch). LangChain shipped day-zero support with a base class shared with TypeSafe classifiers (LangChain).

Architecture pattern: At AI Engineer, Cloudflare described a hybrid agent design. Fast decision models handle tool selection and log extraction, and a large reasoning model handles diagnosis only. In its demo, this cut six LLM calls to one and about 40K tokens to 1.8K (TheTuringPost).

Frontier Model Evals: Fable 5.2 Leads, Carbon Draws Speed Complaints

Claude Fable 5.2 on Agent Arena: Fable 5.2 (Max) ranks #1 with a +30.4% net improvement across 4.7K real agentic sessions. That is about 1.6x the score of #2, Opus 5.5 (+19.5%). It is also #1 in Code, Work and Chat (Arena, Pareto view).

Pricing: API prices drop from $10/$50 to $8/$40 per 1M input/output tokens. Median cost per task is $7.3, versus $5.5 for Opus 5.5, $1.5 for Gemini 4 Carbon (High) and $6.0 for GPT-6-Astra.

AA-Robotics v0.1: Fable 5.2 leads at 75, ahead of Astra (69) and Sol (65) (AA).

VIALS-AA (visual reasoning): It scores 67% with tools but only 44% without them. Haiku 5.5 with tools reaches 49% at $0.07 per task (AA).

Debate benchmark: It won 209 of 224 debates in a Cambridge Union-style contest (@SamuelFitouss10).

Effort calibration: Within the Claude lineup, Artificial Analysis finds that moving up a model tier beats raising effort: Opus 5.5 at xhigh outscores Sonnet 5.5 at max with 40% lower cost per task (AA). Vals separately reports that Fable's "high" setting is often cheaper and better than xhigh or max (@RayanKrishnan).

Gemini 4 Carbon: Vals ranks Carbon #3 on its index, and the fastest and cheapest of the top five (Vals). Practitioner opinions conflict (Theo, Martin Casado, @MParakhin).

Latency complaints: Theo measured Carbon at low effort taking 22m21s, versus 9m33s for Opus 5.5 at high (timing). He also reports about 11 seconds to first token, roughly 20x Fable's, and attributes the delay to the server side (TTFT).

Coding and math: Casado calls Carbon "really good at code." Parakhin says Carbon often beats Fable on math and ML problems but that he does not trust it with coding.
