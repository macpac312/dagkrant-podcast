Musk’s xAI Faces Skeptical US Judges Over California AI Disclosure Law | PYMNTS.com

A three-judge panel of the 9th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals in San Francisco appeared skeptical of parts of the company’s constitutional arguments while also questioning the scope of the state’s disclosure requirements, according to [Reuters reporting published November 18](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/musks-xai-urges-us-appeals-court-block-california-ai-disclosure-law-2026-11-18/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

The legal battle underscores mounting tensions between technology companies seeking to protect proprietary information and state regulators pushing for greater transparency in the rapidly expanding artificial intelligence industry.

The dispute centers on a California law signed by Democratic Gov. Gavin Newsom in September 2024 that requires generative AI developers to publish summaries describing the datasets used to train their models. The legislation took effect January 1, 2026.

xAI sued California in 2025, arguing that the disclosure requirements violate the First Amendment by compelling companies to release information about their AI development practices. The company also maintains that the law could force developers to reveal commercially sensitive details and undermine protections for trade secrets, Reuters reported.

The company appealed after U.S. District Judge Jesus Bernal in Los Angeles rejected its request for a preliminary injunction in March. Bernal concluded that xAI had not established a sufficient likelihood of prevailing on its constitutional claims to justify temporarily blocking enforcement of the statute.

During Wednesday’s hearing, Circuit Judge Johnnie Rawlinson questioned xAI attorney James Xi of Clement & Murphy about the company’s argument that California’s disclosure requirements improperly interfere with its free-speech rights.

Rawlinson’s questioning focused on whether the state could legitimately require developers to provide information that would help consumers and the public better understand the materials used to build artificial intelligence systems, according to Reuters.

The judges also scrutinized California’s defense of the legislation, suggesting that the panel was weighing the government’s interest in transparency against the potential burden on technology companies.

Circuit Judge Danielle Forrest pressed California attorney Joe Meeker about the extent of the information the state could demand from AI developers under the law.

Her questions highlighted concerns about whether disclosure requirements could extend beyond general descriptions of training data and potentially reach information companies regard as confidential.

The dispute comes as policymakers across the United States consider how to regulate increasingly sophisticated AI systems, including whether developers should be required to provide greater visibility into the information used to train their models.

California has emerged as a prominent state-level regulator of artificial intelligence, pursuing legislation intended to increase accountability and public understanding of technologies that are becoming more widely integrated into business and consumer applications.

For AI developers, mandatory disclosures raise questions about the balance between regulatory compliance and protecting intellectual property in an industry where training methods and data resources can provide significant competitive advantages.

The appeals court’s consideration of xAI’s request could have implications for the enforcement of California’s transparency requirements and similar measures adopted by other jurisdictions.

The panel did not issue an immediate ruling following Wednesday’s arguments, Reuters reported.

The case is _x.AI LLC v. Bonta_, No. 26-1591, in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 9th Circuit.
