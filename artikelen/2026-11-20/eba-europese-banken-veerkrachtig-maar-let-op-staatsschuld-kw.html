EU/EEA banks continue to display resilience amid sovereign debt pressures, borrower vulnerabilities and rising cyber threats | European Banking Authority

The EBA today published itsAutumn 2026 Risk Assessment Report (RAR)together with athematic note on lending to small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs)and the results of itsAutumn 2026 Risk Assessment Questionnaire (RAQ). The findings show that EU/EEA banks remain resilient, supported by strong capital, liquidity and profitability, while continuing to increase financing to the economy. At the same time, geopolitical uncertainty, sensitivity to sovereign debt repricing, growing interconnectedness within the financial sector and evolving operational threats warrant continued vigilance.

Digitalisation creates opportunities while reshaping operational risks

Cyber risk and data security stayed among the leading operational concerns, with fraud and third-party risks also gaining prominence. Geopolitical tensions can amplify threats to banks and critical financial infrastructure, and reliance on common ICT providers could create channels through which disruptions spread across multiple institutions.

Banks expect AI to improve employee productivity and customer services, and are already using AI, including frontier AI models, to detect system weaknesses and strengthen controls. Realising these benefits will depend on effective implementation, sound governance and appropriate human oversight. At the same time, AI may increase exposure to cyberattacks and fraud, while introducing additional model risks and dependencies on external providers.

Lending to households and non-financial corporations continued to expand over the year to June 2026, although growth differed significantly across countries and sectors. Investment in infrastructure, energy and security-related activities is expected to be a key driver of corporate loan demand. The financing needs associated with the growing AI ecosystem also warrant close monitoring of banks’ exposures and any related concentration or valuation risks, even though indications are that banks have on average only limited exposures to the ICT sector.

Lending to other financial corporations, including non-bank financial institutions (NBFIs) increased particularly strongly, growing by 18.5% year on year. These expanding interconnections can transmit stress within the financial system through funding relationships, committed credit lines, common borrowers and correlated exposures. At the same time, the continued expansion of sovereign portfolios has increased banks’ sensitivity to adverse movements in sovereign yields, with potential implications for valuations, liquidity, capital and funding costs.

The aggregate non-performing loan (NPL) ratio remained broadly stable at around 1.8% in June 2026. However, NPL volumes increased during the first half of the year, with the impact on the ratio largely offset by continued lending growth. Consumer credit, SME lending and commercial real estate related exposures remain the areas requiring the closest monitoring.

The accompanying SME lending note highlights the importance of bank financing for smaller businesses and examines the vulnerabilities within SME portfolios. Although some credit-quality indicators have improved, SME loans continue to exhibit higher levels of non-performance and elevated credit risk than loans to larger corporates. The analysis also points to a significant overlap between banks’ SME lending portfolios and their commercial real estate exposures. Rising insolvencies, pressure on margins and debt-servicing burdens underscore the need for close monitoring.

Profitability and capital support resilience, while funding activity has been increasingly dependent on windows of opportunity for bond issuances

EU/EEA banks’ profitability stayed robust, with net interest income continuing to be the main driver of earnings, although developments differ across jurisdictions. Looking ahead, rising deposit costs, higher operating expenses and potential credit losses could weigh on earnings. The extent to which banks continue to benefit from higher interest rates will depend on their repricing structures and borrowers’ resilience.

Capital positions remain healthy, with the CET1 ratio at 16.2% in June 2026. Banks continued to generate capital, although growth in risk-weighted assets put some pressure on the capital headroom (4.7%, above OCR and P2G, down by around 10 bps YoY).

Aggregate liquidity positions are also at on average comfortable levels. However, the growing share of sovereign securities in liquidity buffers increases sensitivity to sovereign market developments, while banks’ funding plans are increasingly exposed to rising market volatility and changes in investor demand.

Strengthened operational resilience as well as prudent capital and liquidity planning continue to be important

The EBA emphasises the importance of prudent capital and liquidity planning, sound lending standards and close monitoring of vulnerable borrowers and concentrated exposures. Strengthening operational resilience and managing technological dependencies are essential to preserving EU/EEA banks’ ability to support households and the wider economy.
