AI industry growth remains strong, but the pace is slowing

US companies are using AI models more intensively than ever and paying less for the privilege. The latest update of the Ramp AI Index, which draws on transaction data from more than 85,000 US companies, lays out the numbers.

Tokens, the units of computation in which AI providers bill model usage, got 21.2 percent cheaper in six weeks, according to Ramp lead economist Ara Kharazian. At the same time, token volume rose 1.5 percent to a record high.

Token spending, meaning what companies actually pay for AI APIs, fell 6.3 percent from its peak two weeks earlier. Month over month (the week ending November 15 vs. the week ending October 18), total token spending still rose 12 percent, but token volume jumped 27.2 percent over the same span.

The gap between usage and revenue keeps widening. The average price per million tokens for OpenAI and Anthropic combined fell to $0.45, according to Ramp, down 11.2 percent from the prior month.

In mid-November, the average price per million tokens sits at $0.45, with OpenAI at $0.38 and Anthropic at $0.50. | Image: Ramp AI Index[/caption] API routing platform OpenRouter shows a similar pattern.

According to an analysis Peter Walker published on X, weekly token volume for closed-weight models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google grew 1,215 percent since the start of 2026. Weekly spending rose 455 percent over the same period, far more slowly.

The spending peak came in the week of October 5, according to the OpenRouter data, which runs through the week of November 9. Spending has been declining since then. Spending has fallen since its early-October peak. The chart covers only closed-weight models from OpenAI, Google and Anthropic. | Image: OpenRouter[/caption] Ramp's token metrics rely on usage and billing data imported from AI providers, while OpenRouter measures traffic through its API routing platform.

The fact that both sources show a spending peak in October followed by declining outlays and rising volume reinforces the trend, though neither service is representative of the overall market.

OpenRouter in particular offers only a narrow window into a very specific user base. Anthropic dominates the token market on IPO day In the week ending November 15, 2026, Anthropic accounted for 58.7 percent of all token spending, according to the Ramp AI Index, up 1.7 percentage points from the prior month.

Its share of token volume was even higher at 59.4 percent. OpenAI fell to 37.2 percent of spending and 37.7 percent of volume. Google came in at 1.4 percent and xAI (SpaceXAI) at 2.4 percent.

Kharazian wrote on X that Anthropic holds this share on IPO day. In October, however, Anthropic's token spending grew only 0.5 percent month over month, while OpenAI's grew 2.1 percent.

In October 2026, 56.8 percent of all tracked US companies paid for AI products or services, according to Ramp, up one percentage point from September and 13.3 percentage points more than a year earlier.

Anthropic leads with an adoption rate of 44.8 percent, while OpenAI sits at 40.6 percent. Stripe data also shows strong AI growth and signs of cooling The Ramp AI Index tracks the demand side, what companies spend on AI.

Payment processor Stripe has published its own AI Index that measures the supply side: incoming payments at roughly 20,000 AI companies that use Stripe. [caption id="attachment_43095" align="alignnone" width="1220"] The Stripe AI Index shows rising incoming payments for AI companies since 2022.

The increase steepens starting in 2025, and the index crosses 600 points in 2026. | Image: Stripe[/caption] According to the Stripe AI Index, revenue at AI companies grew 103 percent over the 52 weeks ending November 8.

The typical (median) AI company grew only 41 percent, though, suggesting that a few large providers are skewing the overall picture. The slowdown over shorter time frames is pronounced.

Annualized over 13 weeks, growth stood at about 80 percent, down from 140 percent just a few months ago. The US leads with 104 percent year-over-year growth, while the EU sits at 67 percent.

AI companies are growing far faster than traditional software firms, according to Stripe: 103 percent compared with 33 percent in the SaaS Index. [caption id="attachment_43096" align="alignnone" width="1220"] Payment activity among tracked AI companies rose about 104 percent in the US over the 52 weeks ending November 8, 2026.

The UK followed at 83 percent, the rest of the world at 76 percent and the EU at 67 percent growth. | Image: Stripe[/caption] Both indexes paint a similar picture. AI is still growing fast, but momentum is cooling.

At Ramp, that shows up through falling prices and concentrated demand. At Stripe, it shows up through slowing growth rates on the provider side. New growth could emerge from more capable or more reliable models, or simply from better integration of current models into business workflows.

The potential is there, considering how AI has upended the entire coding market in a matter of months. The AI industry still depends on extraordinarily strong growth, though, if it wants to recoup the massive capacity buildout it has planned.
