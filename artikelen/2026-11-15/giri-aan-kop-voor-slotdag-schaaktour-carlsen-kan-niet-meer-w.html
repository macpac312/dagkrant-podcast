Total Chess: Firouzja, Caruana, Giri, MVL Lead Before Final Day; Carlsen Out Of Contention

GM Magnus Carlsen can no longer win the Total Chess World Championship Tour Pilot 2026 after two draws against GM Shakhriyar Mamedyarov left him unable to win Group C, where GM Anish Giri leads. Carlsen is still world number-one after GM Fabiano Caruana also made two draws, leaving him four points clear of his only Group B rival, GM Liem Le. That group also saw GM Praggnanandhaa Rameshbabu blunder mate-in-one in a winning position.

GM Alireza Firouzja still leads Group A, while Group D is a thriller. GM Maxime Vachier-Lagrave was beaten and caught by GM Arjun Erigaisi, but still tops a three-way tie, which includes GM Hans Niemann, because of his Blitz Tiebreaker performance.

Day six, which features the final two rounds of the Group Stage, starts on Sunday, November 15, at 9 a.m. ET / 15:00 CET / 7:30 p.m. IST.

Five games were decisive in both rounds on the penultimate day of the group stage—just the four group winners will play a knockout for overall first place, while those in second in their group will play for fifth, those in third place for ninth, and so on.

Group A: Firouzja Keeps Lead, Van Foreest Climbs

"The players who are happy with their tournament situation are stabilizing," said GM Levon Aronian when asked to explain a flurry of draws in the penultimate round of the group stage. He himself missed some chances in the first game as he ended two games peacefully against the struggling GM Nodirbek Abdusattorov, while Firouzja also made two draws as he continues to lead GM Jan-Krzysztof Duda by two points.

The greatest interest there was perhaps Duda's first appearance in the Confessional—he got somewhat carried away and talked for five minutes about his choice of a line of the Petroff that he mentioned World Champion Gukesh Dommaraju had warned him against. During that time Firouzja had quickly made his move.

No harm was done, however, and the real action in the group came in the matchup between GM Andrew Hong and GM Jorden van Foreest. The young American scored a remarkably smooth win with Black in the first game, but in the second Van Foreest struck back in a Najdorf. He got a huge helping hand when Hong decided to neglect defending a pawn in favor of an attack, but the attack soon fizzled out into nothing.

Van Foreest's rollercoaster ride earned him a point more than he'd have earned for two draws and took him up to joint second place in the group. He faces Duda on the final day, while Hong plays Abdusattorov, and leader Firouzja faces Aronian.

Group B: Caruana Almost Uncatchable After Holding Le

Let's start with the most dramatic moment in Group B. Praggnanandhaa had survived a very shaky position against GM Richard Rapport to emerge with a winning position, with the only real issue his clock. Still, even down to a minute, it's not often a grandmaster blunders mate-in-one!

He pressed in the next game, but was held to a draw by Rapport, who can still fight for a high place in the group.

The key clash was leader Caruana vs. second-placed Le, with the Vietnamese star needing to win one of the games to catch his rival. Instead both remained unbeaten, with the first game a solid draw, while the second was jam-packed with tricky tactics and positional assessments.

Caruana summed up, "I felt like I had very serious pressure at some point—I don’t know where I went wrong, because at the end I was even trying to hold!"

Caruana was asked afterward if he was thinking of the world number-one spot (he's currently rated 2798.6 to Carlsen's 2804), and admitted: "I had a brief thought during the game because I thought maybe I can win this game, it was looking promising, at least, and then it’s very close if Magnus doesn’t win!"

In fact a win would still have left Caruana 0.4 points short of the number-one spot, but his four-point lead means winning the group is all but certain. Even if Caruana loses both games to GM Vladislav Artemiev on the final day, Le would have to beat Praggnanandhaa twice to finish first, since Caruana also has a better tiebreak. Le is focused on something else: "Tomorrow I’m playing Pragg, and I think I need to at least not lose that match to make sure I get second place."

The day's other result in Group B was a late but welcome first win for GM Vincent Keymer, who exploited the fact that Artemiev got down to under a minute on his clock by move 19.

Group C: Giri Takes Over As Leader; Carlsen Out Of Contention

Carlsen remains winless in fast classic and, with two draws against Mamedyarov, can now at best finish second in Group C, since Giri vs. GM Haik Martirosyan on the final day will see one of those players score more than the 13 points Carlsen would get if he defeats GM Ian Nepomniachtchi twice.

Carlsen's first game against Mamedyarov was yet more evidence that he's not firing on all cylinders. Mamedyarov went for a speculative pawn sacrifice ("Of course, it looks very strange, because not enough pieces for the attack, but anyway, at some moment I started to play for a win!"), but the world number-one's response was hesitant. He went for a line giving up his queen, but could have switched to a close-to-winning position with queens on the board when Mamedyarov delayed.

Carlsen essentially had to win with Black in the second game to have any chance of topping the group, but, despite an offbeat opening, he then played carefully to equalize and set some small tests for Mamedyarov before the game fizzled out into a draw.

Operation "save the world number-one spot and a 2800 rating" will continue on Sunday against Nepomniachtchi, who took down previous leader GM Haik Martirosyan in a complex second game of the day. The new leader, however, is Giri, who defeated GM Abhimanyu Mishra in impressive style.

A draw in the second game wrapped up an excellent day for Giri, but he admitted he'd got lucky when Mishra spoiled much of his advantage and then suddenly offered a draw:

Sometimes there are these jokes, 'I offer you to resign!' kind of jokes, I thought maybe that was one. I was really struggling. I mixed up my prep somewhere, just forgot it, and then I started to think on my own and I knew that this was not the way, and I just started calculating, which was a mistake. And I blundered 21.Nd3. I thought there was something there, and then I’m strategically busted, a desperate piece sacrifice, hoping for some miracle draw, but I don’t think it’s there.

Group D: Arjun Beats And Catches MVL, Niemann Joins Them

This is the group that's most perilously balanced, with Vachier-Lagrave top of the table only by virtue of having finished second in the blitz tiebreaker before fast classic began. The Frenchman's serene progress was interrupted by Arjun, who dominated their second game even if he briefly allowed his opponent a way to escape in the rook endgame.
