Claude Meetings beta launches for Google Meet — CASRAI

Anthropic began rolling out Claude Meetings in beta on November 12, 2026, for Team and Enterprise plans. The feature brings Claude into Google Meet as a visible participant that takes notes, answers questions and creates shared work during calls. For university research groups, the launch makes transcript access and organizational settings part of the collaboration decision.

November 12 brings beta availability

Anthropic’s November 12 release notes confirm the rollout in Claude and Claude Desktop, including documents, whiteboards and slides created during a call. The launch article identifies Google Meet as the currently supported conferencing platform; other platforms are planned.

This is a separate development from the company’s November 10 collaboration announcement, which described Meetings as coming soon. CASRAI’s coverage of Whiteboards, Sheets and Data concerns that earlier release.

Team and Enterprise have different defaults

The live Help Center documentation distinguishes two owner-controlled settings:

Owners also control whether members’ connectors can be used in meetings. Google Calendar must be enabled for the organization before a member connects it. These are separate controls over availability, outside participation and connected tools.

The launch excludes some organizations from the beta, including those with zero data retention, HIPAA-ready configuration or customer-managed encryption keys. Having a Team or Enterprise plan therefore does not establish eligibility by itself.

Guest speech and transcript access are different

Anthropic says Claude saves a transcript, summary and screen descriptions, but no audio. A meeting page holds the notes and work created during the call. Organizational invitees can view it and edit the artifacts, and the page can be shared with others inside the organization.

When external guests are allowed, their speech enters the notes and transcript, but they do not receive the meeting page or recap email. Claude posts a notice in the call chat without replying there. The Help Center distinguishes Meet chat, visible to everyone on the call, from internal meeting-page chat.

For research data governance, our assessment is that this creates an access asymmetry: a collaborator’s contribution can enter a record they cannot open through the meeting page. The absence of stored audio still leaves a textual record to consider.

What changes for research offices

Anthropic asks users to ensure everyone accepts transcription before adding Claude or enabling auto-join. It also says connected tools require each person’s permission: initial approval carries forward, while spoken requests currently require speaker confirmation each time.

For universities assessing Claude’s research capabilities and limits, our interpretation is that call participation, transcription and access to the resulting record are three distinct questions. An institution enabling the feature has a collaboration option to evaluate, rather than evidence that every research conversation is suitable for it.

Anthropic’s beta-feature documentation warns that functionality and reliability can change and that some features never reach general availability. The November 12 development remains a beta rollout.
