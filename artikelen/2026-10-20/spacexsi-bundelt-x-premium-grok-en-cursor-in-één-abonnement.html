X Bundles Grok, Cursor and Premium Into One Subscription

X has merged its AI products and its paid social features into a single membership. The company announced new bundled subscription packages this week and each one includes X Premium features plus access to Grok as well as the Cursor coding and X’s agent assistant. Social Media Today reported the news on October 13.

It is a bigger reshuffling than it appears at first glance. Until now, any person who wanted the full set had to buy X Premium for the platform perks, a Grok plan for chat and image tools and also a Cursor plan for its AI coding. Three products meant three bills. The new setup puts all of that under one account and one price ladder.

What X Announced

X’s pitch is simple: One subscription now bundles coding with Cursor, getting work done with the agent assistant and chatting and creating with Grok, with the usual X Premium features layered on top. The amount of AI access increases with each tier, as does the Premium perks.

The company also claims that every plan in the new lineup offers more usage per dollar than the one it replaces. That’s the claim that X makes, and it’s worth considering in light of how much the tools you use really are.

The rollout is gradual, and the packages are being rolled out one region at a time, starting with the U.S., so plenty of users won’t see them.

X posted the plans in a post from its SpaceXSI account on Monday, and the next day Cursor’s own pricing page had the same tiers. That’s a sign of how tightly the products are being tied together now. A coding tool’s pricing page is describing levels of a social platform’s subscription.

For teams that have been juggling logins, it’s an administrative as well as a financial relief. Fewer vendors, fewer invoices and one place to see who is using what.

The Five Plans

There are five individual tiers, and the gap between the bottom and the top is wide. Pricing below comes from Cursor’s pricing page and outlets covering the launch.

The Premium tier is where it’s at. Rumors point to it bundling benefits that used to be on separate tiers (SuperGrok, Cursor Pro and X Premium+) and that would save you around $90 a month if you bought them separately. At $30, which is nothing to sneeze at if you were paying for all three.

Teams get a version, too. The Premium, Super and Ultra tiers are available as team plans at the same per-seat prices as the individual versions, and larger companies can request a custom enterprise plan.

One Usage Pool, With A Catch

The most significant change lies within the pricing structure. Grok and Cursor now share the same included usage allowance, which encompasses the Grok applications, Grok tab in X and Cursor itself. Capacity that a user leaves unexpended in one remains available in the other.

For someone who codes in one part of the day and chats to Grok in another, the arrangement presents a less confusing alternative to two separate allowances, one of which is inevitably exhausted before the other.

The exception concerns third party models. Claude and GPT remain available in Cursor on Premium and higher subscriptions, however their usage does not count against the shared allowance. It is billed separately at the API listed price, starting with the first request. Developers who use such models for the majority of their workload should not expect the bundle to reduce their expenses.

Grok Bot is also subject to restrictions. It is not available on Free or Plus subscriptions, only from Premium.

This is of particular concern for those who wish to use the agent assistant. Those who expect it to be available on a Plus subscription will be disappointed to learn it is not available on lower subscriptions. An upgrade to Premium is needed to access it, along with the cloud agents in Cursor.

What Current Subscribers Can Do

Nobody is being forced over on day one. Existing subscribers can keep their current plan or move to the new offering and link their Grok, Cursor and X accounts. Current SuperGrok, Cursor and X Premium customers keep their existing pricing and features for now, and the option to migrate is showing up gradually.

X says comparable new plans cost the same or less than the X Premium and Premium+ subscriptions they stand in for. If you’re on Premium+ today, it’s worth comparing the new Premium tier line by line before switching, because the feature mix isn’t identical.

The Xsi Naming

Anyone who was confused by “xSI” in X’s announcement is not alone. This is the new name to which xAI changed its previous one, according to Social Media Today. The reason for this is that xAI was prompted by the recent statement of U.S. President Donald Trump, who suggested that artificial intelligence should instead be referred to as “super intelligence.”

As a result, SpaceXAI, the previously unwieldy name for the organization that combines SpaceX, xAI and X, is now known as SpaceXSI. This conglomerate acquired Cursor – an AI coding tool – in August. Its presence within the new social media subscription is therefore unsurprising.

Why A Bundle Changes The Picture

X Premium subscription initially offered a rather thin set of benefits: a checkmark, longer post limit, and increased reach. However, adding Grok and Cursor to the mix changes the game considerably, as it provides users with features they may be willing to pay for in good faith.

The focus of the subscription appears to shift from the status symbol (badge) to the tools (agent and assistant). The latter two are much more likely to justify the expense for the average user, while the badge is a common reward for any active engagement on the platform.

The combined allowance serves as a strong reminder that the free version of the service is ultimately not suitable for serious development. When the tools require a separate allowance within the same account, their very presence becomes a powerful incentivization to upgrade. In other words, the developers who sign up for Cursor to enjoy advanced coding assistance will also gain access to the social media platform’s core features. The cheapest plan (Plus) acts as a perfect entry-level subscription for those who wish to move up to the Premium and use the more substantial AI technologies. The competition from similar products offering premium subscriptions for around $200 per month makes the price appear reasonable, but the additional attention from the social network itself appears to be the ultimate motivator for upgrading.

X changed the nature of the subscription to encourage more spending by making the new features integral parts of the development process. Developers who wish to utilize advanced coding assistance must essentially subscribe to a social network first and then invest in an additional allowance to enjoy the benefits of Cursor’s technology. It is yet to be seen how many users will accept the proposition in the given financial environment.

There’s also a bigger industry question behind it. Social Media Today has asked in an earlier piece whether social platforms will eventually charge all users for access. X’s lineup doesn’t answer that, but a subscription ladder that runs from free to $200 suggests where the company wants its revenue mix to head.

What Marketers And Creators Should Watch

For creators, the tools associated with the paid sublevels are the practical attraction. According to reports, creator tools begin at the Plus level, and the checkmark is included.

For advertisers, the detail to track down is the ads. Reports indicate that Plus subscribers see fewer ads, while higher plans can remove all ads in the timeline. If a large percentage of active users upgrades to higher levels, some timeline inventory can disappear. It is way too early to estimate how many users will pay, and X has not yet announced adoption rates for the new products, so take any prediction with a grain of salt.

Agencies that operate X accounts for clients should also check up on which level each team member requires. Team pricing is similar to individual plans, so costs explode at the Super and Ultra levels.

A sensible first step is to audit who on your team subscribes to any of the three products. Overlapping subscriptions are the easiest way to save, and the Premium tier is aimed right at overlapping subscriptions.
