Kitchenaid KF4 review – Pickr

Compact and practically effortless, the Kitchenaid KF4 makes coffee easy at home, regardless of if you need a daily cuppa or are making babycinos for the kids.

Written by Leigh :) Stark, an award winning journalist and reviewer with almost 20 years of experience. Heard on ABC, 2GB, 3AW, and more regularly.

Quick review

A more compact take on lattes and cappuccinos, the Kitchenaid KF4 is a cafe-ready addition for the home so easy, your kids can use it. No more excuses for coffee ever again.

For some of us, coffee is one of the most important rituals of the day. It’s the reason you might be able to move in the morning after one cup. Not having it might be the reason you don’t move at all.

Some of us feel totally disconnected from reality until we’ve had our morning cup. Maybe two.

It’s for that reason why the coffee machine can be one of the more cherished and loved appliances in the home. While not necessary the same way a fridge is, or even a kettle or toaster, the coffee machine can be the first appliance you want to see every morning.

That means it needs to be easy. It needs to be effortless. It needs to give you what you need and nothing less.

While some coffee machines feel like they need a degree to operate, when you just want your daily dose, you want it to be simultaneously seamless and speedy.

And that’s where Kitchenaid appears to have been dabbling, building an automatic coffee machine focused on these ideals, all while keeping the space down. The KF4 is exactly that: a compact take on coffee that aims to be easy to use. Like your phone, it even has a touchscreen.

What is the Kitchenaid KF4?

Kitchenaid’s model numbers don’t do a lot of explaining their purpose, but the KF4 is a coffee machine. Maybe “KF” is supposed to sound like “kaw-fee”, we don’t know. What we do know is the KF4 is designed to be a slim take on making coffee, adding the much-loved appliance to the kitchen without eroding too much space in the home.

It’s one of several in the Kitchenaid automated espresso machine range, also arriving with the less obvious model number “5KES8454”. Catchy. Don’t go using that in a store. They won’t know what you’re talking about.

Mention the KF4, however, and you’ll find a fairly compact coffee machine with a 3.5 inch touchscreen, simple buttons on the outside, plus a 1.8 litre water tank and a smaller milk container with an even smaller hose.

There’s a built-in grinder, plus a system Kitchenaid calls “IntelliGrind” which basically uses sensors to fine-tune the grind so you don’t really need to do anything.

It’ll take care of the grind, the dose, the tamping, and the extraction. You just bring the glass or mug, and potentially milk in the external container, which can be the container Kitchenaid supplies or any jug or cup you put the hose into.

It’ll even handle clean-up quickly of those milk-based drinks, too, with a quick milk cleaning mode that takes you though a few instruction screens to deal spare milk in the line, something you definitely will want to take care of after you’re done making cups.

The concept here is simple: make coffee quickly and easily. Clean it up that way, too.

What does it do?

Of course, coffee comes in lots of different varieties, and everyone has a different style they prefer. Kitchenaid’s small touchscreen aims to cover these with customisations you can use, delivering built-in recipes for:

Each recipe can have its strength and volume tweaked, basically giving you a way to make an espresso slightly longer than the 25 to 30ml it normally is, or even tweaking how much milk you might want in a textured milk bevy. You can also simply choose frothed milk if you want to make the kids a babycino of sorts, BYO sprinkles and/or cocoa.

Once you’ve made those tweaks, you can easily map them to a profile, with four available.

Does it do the job?

To make coffee, you’ll need to fill the water tank at the back, plug in the included water filter, and pour in some fresh coffee beans, and the machine will take care of the rest. There’s a spot where you can scoop in some pre-ground coffee of your own, handy if you happen to want a decaf later on, but you don’t want to actually fill your machine with decaf beans (totally understandable).

From there, simply turn the machine on and select the coffee you want.

If it’s just a coffee-based extraction — espresso, long, Americano, or simply “coffee” — you’ll just need your cup. If you plan on adding milk, almond, soy, or something else for texture and sweetness, you can use the included milk container, or dangle the steam hose into a jug with whatever milk-type you want to use.

The latter is the easiest approach, though it doesn’t look as nice. There’s no manual milk steaming here, but it all happens for you, and there’s even a quick cleaning process that makes clean-up a cinch, too.

Whatever you pick, the process from no cup to something you can drink is speedy, and is available in little more than a few presses.

You might decide on a tweaked formula you like and will want to reuse. Once that’s been sorted, you can save it to a profile, with four user profiles that support names. Ours were “Mum”, “Dad”, and “Kids”, the latter of which was named because babycinos became a norm that even Ms 9 could work out how to do. If Ms 4 could reach, she would have never stopped, clearly.

Making coffee with the KF4 is really quiet, too. The company has apparently achieved a “Quiet Mark” certification, but that doesn’t mean much outside of the industry. To people at home, however, the sound isn’t a high-pitched pressure system, but rather a minimal assortment of parts spinning up and water coming out.

There’s a grind, a long consistent deep hum from the machine, the dribble of liquid, some vibration, and at the end some mechanical changes moving the coffee out. It takes about a minute depending on the length of the coffee, and is rather easy.

It’s not totally quiet — making coffee makes noise — and you can’t set it ahead of time, but it won’t take you long to use, so it hardly matters at all.
